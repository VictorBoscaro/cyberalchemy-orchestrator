# Reader Journey

## 1. Key Findings

- **The first question must be “what does this let one person do?”, but the current opening answers “what must complex work represent?”** The overview begins with work involving any mixture of people, agents, tools, and systems, then defines the product through a shared representation (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:22-36`). The accepted editorial anchor is narrower and more concrete: expand one person's capacity to organize and execute work through AI agents while preserving understanding and control (`2026-08-12-1757-work-overview-editorial-drift.md:21-32`). The reader therefore needs the personal outcome first, followed immediately by the practical reason infrastructure becomes necessary at agent-enabled scale.

- **The strongest causal explanation already exists, but it should answer a question created by the user promise rather than establish the product's subject.** The sequence from incomplete intention, through decomposition and local action, to divergence, lost decisions, and costly reconstruction is intelligible and practical (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:40-58`). Placed after the objective and user capabilities, it would answer the natural question “why can't I just give tasks to agents and collect their outputs?” The recorded desired progression explicitly puts what the user must be able to do before detail about agent communication, contracts, and provenance (`2026-08-12-1757-work-overview-editorial-drift.md:29-32,55-59`).

- **The usage scenario provides the right first local explanation, but it names and formalizes several concepts before the reader has seen the whole user loop.** The scenario naturally moves from intended result, to an inspectable proposal, approval, delegated work, intervention at authority boundaries, and assessment (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:82-108`). However, `dispatch`, `participant`, and `role` receive formal names mid-flow (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:92-100`), before the reader has completed the experience. A cleaner order is to let the first pass answer “what happens when I use it?”, then revisit the same sequence to name the durable concepts and explain why each exists. This follows the documented requirement that concepts receive names only after their practical relations are understandable (`2026-08-12-1757-work-overview-editorial-drift.md:21-25`).

- **The return from local concepts to a larger composition is already latent at line 160, but it needs to become an explicit synthesis rather than another definition.** The overview separately explains dispatch, attempt, authority, and orchestrator, then says they “together” provide the operating structure for bounded work (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:133-164`). That is the earned point to look backward: show how objective, approved description, participants, authority, execution history, and results compose a controlled unit of work, and state what that composition achieves that none of the pieces achieves alone. The earlier aggregate at lines 76-78 arrives before use has made its parts concrete, so it reads as a catalogue rather than synthesis (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:60-78`).

- **The second major composition—work connected to reusable knowledge—is well positioned late, but knowledge is announced too early and its user value is not used to motivate the return.** The detailed progression is sound: storage is insufficient, review establishes support and scope, provenance preserves origin, and only then do work and knowledge connect in both directions (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:184-218`). Yet the opening introduces this second concern before the user has understood the primary work problem (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:33-36`). Delaying it would let the reader first understand one controlled body of work, then ask the natural temporal question: “how does what was learned improve the next body of work without becoming detached from its evidence?”

## 2. Gaps or Inconsistencies

- There is no dedicated user-capabilities section in the current overview, despite the recorded decision that the progression should move from objective and introduction to what the user must be able to do (`2026-08-12-1757-work-overview-editorial-drift.md:29-32,53-59`). As a result, the reader meets the representation model before receiving a compact picture of their own interaction with the product.
- The document offers three partial wholes without distinguishing their purposes: a generic “representation of the work” (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:76-78`), an “operating structure” for bounded work (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:160-164`), and the work-system/knowledge-system relation (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:215-218`). A first-time reader is not told whether these are nested compositions, alternative views, or successive levels of detail.
- Observation is named only after execution structure has already been composed (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:169-182`), even though the user-facing promise includes following what matters and recovering how the current state arose. It therefore feels like an added subsystem rather than the means by which the user experiences control and intelligibility.
- The document repeats formal aggregation before and after the scenario (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:28-36,55-58,76-78,160-164`) but never performs a concise backward-looking recap in user language: what the person can now do because these elements work together.
- The late status section correctly separates proposal from current implementation (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:220-238`), but until that point the repeated “the system” formulation can let a fast reader mistake conceptual flow for current product behavior; the opening disclaimer at lines 17-18 is easy to discount once the concrete scenario begins.

## 3. Local Tensions

- **Fast reader versus unfamiliar reader:** the audience can absorb concepts quickly, but the current draft compresses premise, vocabulary, and composition into the opening. More terminology is not necessarily the problem; terminology arrives before the practical question that makes it useful (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:22-36`).
- **Natural narrative versus explicit taxonomy:** the usage scenario is concrete and sequential (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:82-108`), while the later representation section is taxonomic (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:133-167`). Keeping both is useful only if the second clearly announces itself as a return that names and composes what the reader has already seen.
- **Local explanation versus system synthesis:** explaining each object locally protects clarity, but a mere list of objects does not establish why a proposed group is meaningful. The claim that dispatch, attempts, participants, roles, authority, and records form the operating structure (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:160-164`) needs an explicit consequence, not just co-membership.
- **Primary product versus broader model:** the current text treats arbitrary human-machine compositions as the main frame (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:71-74,130-131`), whereas the accepted framing makes one person working through AI agents the stable subject (`2026-08-12-1757-work-overview-editorial-drift.md:27-32`). Generality can appear later as a property of the model, but not at the cost of replacing the product's protagonist.
- **Present experience versus temporal continuity:** observation explains seeing work now and later (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:169-182`), while knowledge explains responsible reuse across work (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:184-218`). They are adjacent but not composed into a single answer about continuity: how the person moves from managing this work to benefiting from prior work.

## 4. Questions for Synthesis

1. What is the shortest opening sequence that establishes, in order, the person's desired outcome, why direct agent use becomes insufficient at scale, and what relationship with the system replaces manual coordination?
2. Should the first scenario remain entirely in ordinary language, with `dispatch`, `attempt`, `role`, `authority`, and `observation` introduced only in a deliberate second pass over that same scenario?
3. Which larger compositions will the document explicitly propose, and what unique capability does each composition produce? A plausible reader-facing sequence is: controlled unit of work; observable work system; continuity between work and reusable knowledge—but the synthesis should test whether three levels are necessary.
4. Where should the document disclose that these groupings are explanatory lenses rather than uniquely determined architectural boundaries, without weakening their usefulness?
5. Can the late success criteria be reorganized as validation of the previously shown compositions—what each whole must preserve—rather than remain a separate risk catalogue (`work-and-knowledge-system-overview.md:240-261`)?
