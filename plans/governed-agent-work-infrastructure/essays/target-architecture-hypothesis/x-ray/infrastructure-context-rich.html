1 · context and bounded intention

Work begins as an intention, then becomes bounded enough to act on.

A situated concern becomes an objective, then differentiated work. Each assignment receives only the context and authority needed for its part while remaining connected to the whole.

Situated perception

A condition, opportunity, or concern becomes visible.

Declared objective

The desired change is made explicit and remains revisable.

Differentiated intention

Plans, questions, routes, and workstreams expose different needs.

Bounded assignment

Immediate objective, selected context, constraints, gates, and authority travel together.

Attempt and effect

Work changes the world and returns evidence to the larger objective.

Objective and authority

Why the work exists, what boundary applies, and who may authorize it.

Materialized context

Lineage may expose broad context; one execution receives the smallest coherent slice.

Bounded authority

Allowed work, effects, tools, budgets, stop conditions, and prohibitions narrow toward action.

Connected history

Later readers can traverse from conclusion back to question—and forward to dependants.

Context available through lineage is not context materialized for one execution. Too little context permits locally correct but globally harmful work. Too much creates distraction, conflicting instructions, excess cost, and unclear authority. The assignment boundary is therefore part of governance, not just prompt construction.

2 · problem and stakes

Decomposition increases local precision while hiding global purpose.

A workflow can execute the wrong route correctly. The failure is not missing activity; it is the loss of inspectable connections between what was wanted, what was allowed, what was assigned, what happened, and what changed.

Locally right, globally wrong

A task passes its own checks while no longer advancing the source objective.

Authority drift

A delegated step silently acts beyond the decision that authorized it.

Epistemic collapse

Independent judgments contaminate one another or disappear inside an unexplained synthesis.

Unprovable outcome

An artifact survives, but its assumptions, causes, dissent, and downstream effects cannot be reconstructed.

Five paths must remain independently inspectable

Purpose

Why this local work exists and which larger objective it serves.

Authority

Which decision allowed which work and effects, within what limits.

Assignment

Why this actor received this bounded part, context, role, and gate.

Causation

Which attempt, message, judgment, and tool act produced a result.

Realization

How outputs became effects and whether those effects advanced the objective.

None of these paths may be inferred from another. A task relationship does not prove authority; temporal order does not prove causation; an artifact does not prove realization; and successful execution does not prove purpose.

7 · dispatch modes

A mode is a bounded composition of choices, not one overloaded enum.

The saved task DAG makes modes concrete. Strategy selects dimensions; Protocol closes them into nodes, edges, gates, joins, and convergence predicates; Dispatch binds that graph for execution.

1 · Work topology

Sequence, fan-out, lens fan-out, staged DAG, tournament bracket, or bounded loop.

2 · Interaction

Sealed-independent, robot-talks deliberation, zig-zag alternation, or producer–reviewer exchange.

3 · Bias control

None, diverse lenses, replication, paired tension, or full pairwise tension.

4 · Evidence acquisition

Direct work, scouts that locate evidence, or probes that inspect through a pinned lens.

5 · Join policy

Collect all, parent synthesis, ranked selection, Pareto set, adjudication, or explicit disagreement.

6 · Convergence

Typed predicate, iteration bound, evidence threshold, unresolved-residue rule, and stop condition.

Task fan-out

Independent sibling tasks run concurrently, then join. Best when the work is separable and outputs have explicit ownership.

root → {task A | task B | task C} → join

Lens fan-out

The same target is inspected through structurally different lenses. Preserves distinct observations before synthesis.

target → {security | UX | operations} → synthesis

Tensioned views

Paired roles are intentionally given opposing pressures. Their disagreement is an evidence-bearing result, not noise to erase.

proposal → {maximize X ↔ protect Y} → tension map

Robot-talks

WORKFLOW CONTRACT Standalone, it is an audit workflow. As robot_talks:true, it is an intra-group confrontation overlay governed by Strategy.

round 1 → challenge → response → convergence check

Zig-zag

WORKFLOW CONTRACT Work alternates between stages or roles: producer, critic, revision, verification. Each return edge has a reason and limit.

produce → critique → revise → verify ↺ bounded

Tournament

Multiple proposals are evaluated against declared criteria; ranking, bracket, or Pareto selection preserves comparison evidence.

{P1 | P2 | P3 | P4} → evaluate → select

Scouts and probes

Scouts find promising evidence or locations; probes inspect through a pinned lens. Neither adjudicates truth or promotes facts.

question → scout → evidence set → bounded worker

8 · state and contracts

The architecture separates invariants, tuning, and history.

The design fails quietly when something migrates between these strata without an explicit decision—for example, when a tunable release policy becomes an unchangeable law.

Given

Seven guarantees and the single-writer rule. Changing these changes what the system is.

Optimized

Strategies, modes, protocols, lenses, tensions, templates, budgets, join/convergence rules, and release policy. These are compared and tuned.

Accumulated

Strategy decisions, task DAG versions, interpretation records, Dispatch digests, events, emissions, rendered prompts, and receipts. These grow; corrections append.

Emission contract

emission:
  recipients : who receives it + release condition
  payload    : judgment | content | empty
  return     : none | ack | reply
  binding    : prompt@version + format@version

Digest contract

Closes over hashes/versions for the strategy decision, kind-level protocol, task DAG, every skill profile and DAG fragment, interpretation manifest, capability resolution, Dispatch schema, prompts, and formats. Any change creates a new digest and requires renewed confirmation.

Protocol-instance contract

Every task DAG names the immutable protocol-kind@version it derives from, its permitted variations, and a comparable-shape signature. A unique task graph preserves history only; this contract preserves R2 comparability.

Durability contract

Record is the sole ingress for durable facts. Every act produces an event; corrections never overwrite history.

9 · kernel hypothesis

A kernel does not own every meaning; it defines what must be witnessed before independently governed meanings may compose.

FORMAL HYPOTHESIS As intention is decomposed, operational precision rises while global purpose recedes. The proposed kernel is a small acceptance and composition boundary, not a selected service or deployment unit.

B0 — Bootstrap boundary

A finite, inspectable root of trust: candidate roots, versions, owners, validators, and assumptions. It does not justify itself.

M — Meta-contract

Defines what makes a domain-kernel declaration governable: stable identity, version, owned scope, applicability, authority basis, evaluation semantics, dependencies, provenance, lifecycle, violation posture, supersession, and accepted assumptions.

M does not derive domain semantics or prove truth, consistency, usefulness, authorization, or enforcement.

G — Candidate global invariants

Only laws every accepted composition must preserve. Candidates include non-manufacture of authority by projections, non-transmission by lineage, witnessed derivations, reconstructable history, local validity not implying compatibility, and checker acceptance not implying enforcement.

Ki — Bounded domain kernel

Owns local objects, relations, permitted compositions, invariants, evidence standards, authority boundaries, lifecycle, failure vocabulary, and verification procedures for domains such as planning, evidence, execution, research, identity, or a user-defined business domain.

Q — Conformance checker

Evaluates a small family of versioned judgments over declarations and witnesses. It checks declared obligations; it does not create authority, establish empirical truth, enforce physical effects, or prove that the product objective is worthwhile.

Cij — Composition witness

A scoped, versioned record of translations, versions, preserved invariants, acceptance, assumptions, conflicts, residue, rejected alternatives, evidence, checker results, and invalidation conditions. Logical compatibility is not execution permission.

Declaration judgment

B0 ; Γ ⊢Q WellFormedM(Ki)

Plain language: under the bootstrap facts B0 and environment Γ, checker Q judges that Ki satisfies meta-contract M. This is structural acceptance, not endorsement of the domain's truth.

Compatibility judgment

B0 ; Γ ⊢Q Cij : CompatibleG(Ki, Kj)

Plain language: Q judges that witness Cij supports compatibility between two versioned domain kernels against candidate global set G, within the witness's scope.

PROPOSAL Authority, precedence, compatibility, and enforcement remain different relations. A positive Q result may inform an authority decision but cannot substitute for it.

Critical architectural review

Kernel is not automatically a service

It may be a logical contract, validators, protocol, type system, small checker, distributed boundary, or combination. Deployment topology is OPEN.

Identity must not become the semantic monolith

Identity may own stable identifiers and relation signatures; each Ki owns local meaning and valid composition.

Record single-writer needs qualification

“Single writer” may mean one logical ingress, one transactional authority, or one process. Durable factual coherence is the invariant; a physical bottleneck is not selected.

Authority remains unresolved

Q can validate the structure of an authority claim without becoming its source. D9 remains CRITICAL.

Confirmation may be profile-specific

Two confirmations over one digest is preserved as the D3 proposal, not silently promoted to a universal invariant.

Formal vocabulary earns its place through checks

Category, functor, kernel, invariant, and equivalence remain provisional unless they add a check, decision, counterexample, or explanatory distinction.

10 · user-defined domain structures

Users should express their own domain structure without forcing every domain into one universal ontology.

FORMAL HYPOTHESIS Begin with the user's vocabulary and strengthen the mathematical structure only when concrete composition obligations are admitted.

A bounded domain schema may declare object/entity kinds, operations and events, states, rules, evidence types, authority roles, primitive relations, allowed signatures, selected composition rules, local invariants, and probes. It need not begin as a category.

1 Vocabularydomain words and questions
2 Typed multigraphobjects + direct edges
3 Relation familiesexplicit signatures
4 Admitted lawsselected compositions
5 Category-likeonly where gate passes
6 Translationwitnessed interoperability

A typed multigraph becomes a category only after the relevant relation family has objects/carriers, identity arrows, closed composition, associativity, explicit source/target typing, and required coherence. Different families may instead form a tree, forest, DAG, controlled cyclic graph, preorder, enriched category, indexed family, state-transition system, or no useful categorical structure.

Potentially admissible derivation

Task --implements--> Specification
Specification --defines--> Feature
Feature --serves--> Objective

A derived task-to-objective claim exists only under an accepted, versioned composition rule with replayable direct edges.

Forbidden implicit derivation

Task --authorized-by--> Decision
Decision --part-of--> Plan
⇏ Plan --authorized--> Task

Direction reversal and relation-family change do not follow. A separate authority rule and accountable acceptance would be required.

NOT IMPLEMENTATION EVIDENCE The schema progression is a design hypothesis. No universal domain editor, admission checker, or translation runtime is claimed to exist.

11 · context-window injection

Context injection should be modeled as an attributable, budgeted projection—not as copying everything reachable into a prompt.

FORMAL HYPOTHESIS Lineage determines what may be relevant; a context manifest determines what this execution actually receives.

Eligible context E(a)

Items reachable for agent assignment a through admitted purpose, authority, assignment, dependency, and evidence relations. Eligibility is not inclusion.

Selector S

A versioned policy that ranks or filters eligible items against the task, role, risk profile, token budget, freshness, authority, and dependency closure.

Context manifest Ma

The ordered, hashed, attributable set actually selected, including item versions, provenance paths, transformations, omissions, budget charges, and conflict stance.

Renderer ρ

Materializes the manifest into messages/tool state for a specific model and window. Rendering may compress or reorder only as declared; rendered bytes are evidence, not the manifest's identity.

E(a) = ReachΠ(objective(a), authority(a), dependencies(a), evidence(a))

Plain language: admitted path policy Π computes the context eligible for assignment a. Reachability alone cannot authorize an effect.

Ma = Sv(E(a), role(a), risk(a), budget(a), freshness(a))

Plain language: selector version v chooses a bounded manifest for this role and risk under an explicit budget.

ContextItem := Instruction | DomainFact | Evidence | RuntimeState | Reference | CapabilityDescription | PriorOutput

precedencea ⊆ ContextItem × ContextItem   is a declared partial order, not an accident of concatenation.

Plain language: context kinds remain distinguishable, and instruction precedence is recorded independently from the byte order used by one model adapter. Conflicts are first-class; a lower item is not silently erased.

Γa = ρmodel,window(Ma)    with    cost(Γa) ≤ budget(a)

Plain language: the model-specific renderer produces the actual injected context Γa; the budget is checked over the materialized result.

Plain language: relevance may be optimized only after hard obligations are satisfied. A high relevance score cannot compensate for missing authority provenance, forbidden disclosure, or an unmet dependency.

B0 ; Γ ⊢Q Ma : ContextAdequate(a, Orequired, Oforbidden)

Plain language: Q can check declared required and forbidden obligations, provenance, versions, and budget. It cannot prove that no unknown useful context was omitted or that the agent will understand what it receives.

Required distinctions and typed residue

Adequacy is obligation-relative

Required items, forbidden items, conflict sets, minimum provenance paths, and freshness constraints must be named. “Enough context” is not a scalar property.

Order is semantic

The manifest records precedence and adjacency constraints. Later instructions must not silently override higher-authority material merely because of render order.

Compression creates residue

ContextResidue records omitted distinctions, lossy summaries, unresolved conflicts, stale dependencies, and items excluded by budget or policy.

Possession is not authority

Reading an authority claim, credential description, or ancestor decision does not delegate its powers. Capability binding remains an independently checked runtime relation.

OPEN Selection ownership, precedence semantics, minimum context obligations, redaction policy, freshness, summarizer trust, model-specific rendering, and manifest-to-Dispatch binding are not yet owned.

13 · progressive formalization

Formalization hypothesis: fidelity, probes, composition, context, and residue.

FORMAL HYPOTHESIS Use the weakest structure that produces a concrete check. Stronger mathematics enters only with named obligations.

Level 0 — Typed graph substrate

Gd = (Vd, Ed, src, tgt, typeV, typeE)

Vd contains domain objects; Ed contains direct attributable relations; src/tgt name endpoints; and typeV/typeE type objects and relations. Direct edges remain historical source material. Every derived edge retains its direct path, composition rule, scope, version, provenance, and acceptance stance.

Level 1 — Domain category, only when admitted

Cd   admitted only if identities, closed typed composition, associativity, and required coherence hold.

Objects are selected domain objects or types; morphisms are selected structure-preserving transformations; identities mean “do nothing”; composition is accepted chaining; associativity makes grouping irrelevant for a valid chain. The entire work graph is not claimed to be one category, and several categories may coexist in one domain.

Level 2 — Translation into the infrastructure

Fd : Cd → R

HomCd(X,Y) → HomR(FdX, FdY)

Fd translates a bounded domain category into a proposed representation category R. R remains hypothetical until its objects, arrows, identities, and composition are defined.

Faithful

The induced hom-map is injective: distinct domain morphisms are not collapsed.

Full

The induced hom-map is surjective: every represented arrow between translated objects comes from a domain arrow.

Fully faithful

HomCd(X,Y) ≅ HomR(FdX,FdY)

The bounded hom-map is bijective. This is a testable refinement target, not a global endpoint.

Limits

Full faithfulness does not imply essential surjectivity, empirical truth, execution authority, complete context, or that Cd captures reality. It may be undesirable when R is intentionally coarser.

Collapse(Fd,X,Y) = { (f,g) | f ≠ g ∧ Fd(f) = Fd(g) }

Missing(Fd,X,Y) = { h | ∄ f . Fd(f) = h }

Collapse diagnoses non-faithfulness; Missing diagnoses non-fullness. Neither alone proves the architecture wrong: the operational question is whether the lost or added distinction matters for the current objective.

Level 3 — Probes and a restricted Yoneda hypothesis

Pd ⊆ Cd

NP(X) = HomCd(-,X) | Pd

NP : Cd → SetPdop

Each X is represented by how admitted probes map into it. A poor probe family collapses distinctions; a richer one may distinguish more. Full Yoneda uses every object as a probe and is fully faithful inside a well-defined category. A restricted family is fully faithful only with an additional density or adequacy condition.

X ∼P Y   when the selected probe representations cannot distinguish X and Y.

This is observational equivalence relative to Pd, not identity in the domain. Each user domain should be able to refine its probes, while the infrastructure records collapsed distinctions, unexplained observations, and non-commuting translations as observational residue.

Level 4 — Cross-domain composition

Cij names source and target domains, proposed translation, preserved structures, lost distinctions, version, scope, acceptance authority, applicable G, and unresolved residue. Do not assume a universal functor.

Tij : Ci → Cj   is a functor only if it preserves identities and composition.

If either structure is not categorical, use the honest term: graph morphism, schema mapping, interpretation, relation-preserving translation, simulation, refinement, lens, or adapter contract.

Level 5 — Type-theoretic encoding

Dependent or indexed types may bind relation endpoints, versions, scopes, owners, evidence kinds, authority boundaries, applicable invariants, and accepted composition rules. Candidate records include DomainKernel, InvariantDeclaration, AuthorityClaim, RelationSignature, DirectEdge, DerivationWitness, CompositionWitness, ContextManifest, DispatchDigest, ExecutionReceipt, and ResidueRecord.

Level 6 — Context projection and injection

The context window can be treated as a typed, lossy projection from attributable source material into a model-specific observation surface. The key object is the manifest, not an untracked prompt concatenation.

Explanatory pseudotype — context manifest

ContextManifest (a : Assignment) := {
  selectorVersion  : SelectorVersion
  sourceClosure    : List DirectWitness
  included         : List (ContextItem × Version × Provenance)
  ordering         : PrecedenceWitness
  transformations  : List TransformationWitness
  requiredChecked  : List ContextObligation
  forbiddenChecked : List ContextObligation
  conflicts        : List ContextConflict

Injectable(a, Ma) ≡ WellFormed(Ma) ∧ Required(a) ⊆ Covered(Ma)
∧ Forbidden(a) ∩ Included(Ma) = ∅ ∧ cost(ρ(Ma)) ≤ budget(a)

This judgment checks declared coverage, exclusion, and budget. It does not establish semantic completeness, model comprehension, or permission to use tools. A separate binding must relate ContextManifest to the confirmed DispatchDigest; otherwise context can drift after confirmation.

WHAT FORMALIZATION DOES NOT PROVE

It may establish internal well-formedness, type-correct relations, preservation under declared translations, conformance with selected invariants, deterministic replay of pure reductions, bounded compatibility, declared context coverage, or absence of particular counterexamples.

It does not by itself establish empirical truth, domain completeness, objective desirability, authority legitimacy, probe adequacy, context completeness, model understanding, physical enforcement, strategic realization, or absence of unknown distinctions.
