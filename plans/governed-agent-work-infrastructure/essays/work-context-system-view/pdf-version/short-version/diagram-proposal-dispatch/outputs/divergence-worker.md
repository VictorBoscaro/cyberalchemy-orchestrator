Section and placement:  
Section 3, “When the whole becomes difficult to recover,” immediately after its final paragraph on page 2. This uses the substantial open space below the section and lets the diagram consolidate the risk before Section 4 introduces the system response.

Diagram concept:  
**Coherent Here, Unresolved as a Whole**

What it explains:  
The diagram shows the same piece of work at two scales. Within its local boundary, the work can be intelligible, consistent, and visibly productive. At the scale of the broader objective, however, its contribution and relationships to other work may be difficult to recover. The contrast makes clear that apparent local success does not by itself establish progress toward the larger objective.

Visual structure:  
- Two horizontally aligned views connected by a thin line labeled “same piece of work.”
- Left view, “Within the local boundary”: a bounded shape containing “task,” linked by solid lines to “decisions,” “artifacts,” and “criteria for success.” A small label reads “visible, internally consistent result.”
- Right view, “In relation to the whole”: “broader objective” and several distinct work pieces, with the same task highlighted. Its links to the objective and other pieces are faint, interrupted, or unresolved.
- A restrained caption beneath the right view: “contribution may be difficult to recover.”
- No success/failure icons and no directional process arrow; the figure is a comparison of perspectives, not a claim that divergence inevitably occurs.

Why this is the strongest idea:  
Section 3’s central comprehension problem is a scale mismatch: evidence available inside a task can support confidence locally while remaining insufficient to judge the composition of the whole. Prose explains this sequentially, but a paired view lets the reader inspect both conditions simultaneously and recognize that they can coexist. It also creates the clearest conceptual bridge to Section 4, where recoverable relationships become the proposed response.

Evidence from the essay:  
- Page 2, end of Section 2: a local task carries some, but never all, of the larger effort’s purpose, assumptions, and prior decisions; autonomy enables focused work while allowing its broader connection to weaken without becoming visible locally.
- Page 2, Section 3: context becomes distributed across tasks and artifacts; individual parts may remain understandable even as their relation to the broader objective becomes harder to see.
- Page 2, Section 3: completed tasks, documents, and implemented decisions may appear successful within their own boundaries, yet completing parts is insufficient without their contributions composing into the objective.
- Page 2, Section 3: local success can conceal failure of the larger effort, and increasing local precision can coincide with decreasing global visibility.

Document fit:  
A shallow, full-width horizontal figure fits the open lower half of page 2. Thin rust and gray strokes, outlined circles, serif labels, and generous whitespace would echo the title-page motif without repeating or competing with it. The two-view structure remains legible at the existing text width.

Risks or uncertainties:  
Broken or fading links must mean “difficult to recover,” not “certainly absent.” The diagram must not imply an omniscient global verdict, a fixed hierarchy, or that every locally coherent task has diverged.
