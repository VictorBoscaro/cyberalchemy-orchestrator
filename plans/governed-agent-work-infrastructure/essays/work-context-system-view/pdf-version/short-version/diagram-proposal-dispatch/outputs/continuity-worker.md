Reviewer verdict: NOT APPLICABLE

Section and placement:  
Section 4, “A system for work,” immediately after the section’s final sentence. The diagram can occupy the open lower portion of page 3 without disrupting the current reading sequence.

Diagram concept:  
**Recoverable Continuity Through Revision**

What it explains:  
The diagram shows that change should add a recoverable transition rather than replace the past. A reader can inspect an earlier configuration of objective, decision, and contributing work; see what later understanding changed and why; and reach the current configuration while retaining access to the earlier one.

Visual structure:  
- Two compact, side-by-side state boundaries labeled **Earlier state** and **Current state**.
- The earlier state contains three linked entities: **Objective**, **Decision**, and **Work contribution**.
- A central transition labeled **Later understanding**, with two subordinate labels: **What changed** and **Why**.
- A directional path runs from the earlier state through the transition to the current state.
- The current state contains corresponding entities, qualified as possible outcomes: **Revised objective**, **Overturned decision**, and **Work no longer contributing**.
- A thin backward line labeled **Earlier state remains recoverable** connects the current state to the earlier state.
- Changed relations use a broken or terminated line rather than erasing the earlier relation. The three outcomes should be presented as possible forms of revision, not as changes that necessarily occur together.

Why this is the strongest idea:  
Section 4 states the continuity requirement clearly but leaves its temporal structure implicit. Prose can name earlier state, change, reason, and present understanding; a diagram can make their simultaneous recoverability immediately inspectable. This directly clarifies the essay’s distinctive claim that preserving work context includes preserving superseded states and reasons, not merely keeping current relationships connected.

Evidence from the essay:  
On page 1, section 2 says composition is not fixed: new parts emerge, existing parts change meaning, become unnecessary, or reveal dependencies. On page 3, section 4 says later understanding may revise an objective, overturn a decision, or reveal that work no longer contributes. It then defines recoverable continuity as keeping the earlier state visible alongside what changed and why. The section’s closing questions also ask what changed and which work should change with it.

Document fit:  
A shallow horizontal diagram fits the unused lower area of page 3 at the existing text width. Thin gray and rust lines, restrained circular nodes, serif labels, and generous spacing would echo the title-page motif without repeating its looping form or competing with it.

Risks or uncertainties:  
A clean before-and-after structure could falsely imply a settled or complete history. Labels must preserve “may,” avoid presenting the current state as final, and allow reasons or relationships to remain uncertain, disputed, or missing, as section 4 explicitly requires.
