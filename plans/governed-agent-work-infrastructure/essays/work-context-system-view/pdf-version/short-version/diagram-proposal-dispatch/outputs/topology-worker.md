Role: topology worker  
Assigned lens: Fong, Brendan; formal network methodology - model recoverable relationships without forcing a hierarchy.

Reviewer verdict: NOT APPLICABLE

Section and placement:  
Section 4, “A system for work,” immediately after its second paragraph, where the essay rejects a single fixed hierarchy. This lets the reader inspect the proposed relational form before the prose turns to continuity through change.

Diagram concept:  
**A Recoverable Context Network**

What it explains:  
Context consists of typed, traversable relationships among work and what explains it. The reader can move from either objective to contributing tasks, or from a task back to objectives, dependencies, decisions, evidence, and artifacts. The network also shows that some relationships may remain unavailable or uncertain.

Visual structure:  
Arrange seven nodes without vertical tiers: **Objective A**, **Objective B**, **Task X**, **Task Y**, **Decision**, **Evidence**, and **Artifact**. Connect Task X to both objectives with links labeled **contributes to**. Connect Task X and Task Y with **depends on**. Connect Decision and Task X with **shapes**; Evidence and Decision with **basis for**; Artifact and Task X with **accumulates with**. Use lines without causal arrowheads and state in a small key that labeled relationships can be traversed in either direction. Include one short broken connector that ends without a destination, keyed **relationship uncertain or missing**. Different outline treatments may distinguish objectives, work, and contextual records, but no node should appear categorically superior.

Why this is the strongest idea:  
The essay’s key structural claim is difficult to hold in prose: parts compose a whole, yet their relations cannot be reduced to one tree. A typed network makes multiple contribution, cross-work dependency, reverse traversal, and incomplete knowledge visible at once. It clarifies that the proposed system preserves explanatory relationships rather than merely collecting tasks or documents.

Evidence from the essay:  
Page 1, section 2 says parts compose the whole rather than sit beneath it; parts may become objectives or tasks and later reveal dependencies elsewhere. Page 2 defines context as recoverable relationships and says bounded work accumulates decisions and artifacts. Section 3 says whole-context becomes distributed across tasks and artifacts. Page 3, section 4 requires movement from an objective to its contributing work and from a task back to purpose, decisions, and conditions. It explicitly rejects a fixed hierarchy because one task may serve several objectives or depend on other work. The section’s closing questions connect decisions to evidence, while its qualification requires uncertain or missing relationships to remain visible.

Document fit:  
A text-column-width network about 55-65 mm high could fit on page 3 after the second paragraph while preserving the remaining reading sequence. Thin curved lines, open nodes, and the existing rust, black, and pale-gray palette would echo the title-page motif without reproducing its prominent symmetrical form.

Risks or uncertainties:  
Label the network **representative, not complete**. “Shapes” must not imply that a decision determines a task mechanically, and “basis for” must not imply uncontested evidence. The broken connector should denote unavailable relationship knowledge, not a known unnamed entity.
