Section and placement:  
Section 3, immediately after its final paragraph, in the open lower portion of page 2.

Diagram concept:  
**One Objective, Changing Structure**

What it explains:  
The diagram shows how a situated perception may give rise to intention and an initially undifferentiated objective; how effort reveals a changing composition of outcomes, constraints, questions, objectives, and tasks; and how bounding those parts enables focused work while distributing the context needed to understand the whole. It explains a qualified development, not a fixed production process.

Visual structure:
- Three horizontally related states, reusing one recognizable objective form throughout.
- **Situated objective**: “situated perception” connects to “intention + objective” with the label **influences what becomes visible; does not determine**. Experience, knowledge, biological constitution, social position, and cultural context appear as a compact supporting list, not separate causal inputs.
- **Evolving composition**: the objective contains outcomes, constraints, questions, possible routes, composing objectives, and tasks connected laterally rather than hierarchically.
- A return curve within and around this state reads **new parts emerge; meanings and dependencies change as work proceeds**.
- **Bounded work**: several local boundaries contain tasks or objectives plus decisions, artifacts, and success criteria. Each carries only partial purpose, assumptions, and prior decisions.
- Connections from the bounded parts back to the broader objective remain visible but vary in completeness, labeled **may weaken or become harder to recover**.
- Transition captions read **often first appears as** and **as time, effort, and bounded attention are applied**, preventing the arrows from implying universal or mechanical stages.
- A closing tension beneath the third state reads **focused work becomes possible / context of the whole becomes distributed**.

Why this is the strongest idea:  
The essay’s central explanatory problem is that differentiation and bounded attention are simultaneously necessary for pursuit and capable of obscuring purpose. A diagram can keep the objective visually continuous while its internal and contextual relations change, making that double effect inspectable at once. The worker’s five-state pipeline should be compressed because it risks presenting contingent, revisable development as a mechanism with fixed stages.

Evidence from the essay:  
Page 1, Section 1 says perception is situated and influential without mechanically determining a conclusion; intention can precede a precise account of the objective. Page 1 and page 2, Section 2 describe distinctions emerging through effort, parts composing rather than subordinating to the whole, composition changing during work, and bounded attention carrying only partial context. Page 2, Section 3 explains that division distributes whole-context and can leave locally coherent results disconnected from broader progress.

Document fit:  
A shallow three-state diagram fits page 2’s lower whitespace. Open nodes, thin gray and muted red lines, serif labels, and generous spacing can echo the title motif without reproducing or competing with it.

Risks or uncertainties:  
Arrows may still imply inevitability. The qualifiers must remain legible, and incomplete connections must indicate a possibility, not certain loss. The composition must not resemble a hierarchy, and the diagram must not imply that all contextual influences or meanings can be enumerated.
