Role: Divergence reviewer  
Assigned lens: Gigerenzer, Gerd; adversarial bounded-rationality methodology.

Reviewer verdict: REVISE

Section and placement:  
Section 3, “When the whole becomes difficult to recover,” immediately after its final paragraph on page 2. The available lower-page space allows the figure to conclude the diagnosis before Section 4 presents a response.

Diagram concept:  
**Locally Clear, Composition Uncertain**

What it explains:  
The same task can be understandable and apparently successful within its own boundary while the available view of how it contributes to the broader objective remains incomplete. The figure distinguishes local evidence from recoverable cross-work context; it does not claim access to an objectively known global state.

Visual structure:  
- Two side-by-side frames joined by a small repeated task marker labeled “same task.”
- Left frame, “Inside the task boundary”: the task connects by solid lines to “decisions,” “artifacts,” and “local success criteria,” ending in “visible result.”
- Right frame, “Across divided work”: the repeated task appears among a “broader objective” and a few “other work pieces.”
- Some cross-work links are solid and labeled “recoverable”; others terminate openly or use restrained dotted strokes labeled “uncertain or difficult to recover.”
- A short baseline statement spans both frames: “Local evidence can be sufficient for action without being sufficient to judge composition.”
- No failure icon, verdict badge, causal arrow, or complete enclosing “whole.”

Why this is the strongest idea:  
Section 3 asks the reader to hold two compatible judgments at once: work may be coherent locally, yet its contribution may remain hard to assess. A paired view makes that bounded-information problem immediately inspectable. It also prevents “global failure” from appearing to be an omniscient diagnosis: the diagram shows limits in recoverable context, which is the essay’s actual concern. Additional prose would repeat the argument without making the simultaneous scale contrast as clear.

Evidence from the essay:  
- Page 2, end of Section 2: a bounded task carries only some of the larger effort’s purpose, assumptions, and prior decisions; local autonomy enables action while divergence may remain invisible locally.
- Page 2, Section 3: parts can remain understandable while their relationships to the broader objective become harder to see.
- Page 2, Section 3: visible outputs can appear successful within their boundaries, although completed parts do not necessarily compose into progress.
- Page 3, Section 4: relationships may be uncertain, disputed, or missing, so the system should expose gaps rather than invent coherence.

Document fit:  
A shallow, full-width figure fits page 2’s open lower region. Thin rust and gray strokes, outlined nodes, serif labels, and generous whitespace would echo the title motif without reproducing its central symbol. Limiting each frame to four or five labels preserves legibility at the existing text width.

Risks or uncertainties:  
The worker’s faint or broken links could be read as proof that relationships are absent or objectively weakened. Open-ended, explicitly qualified links should instead represent what cannot currently be recovered. “Local success” must remain an appearance supported by local criteria, not a definitive judgment.
