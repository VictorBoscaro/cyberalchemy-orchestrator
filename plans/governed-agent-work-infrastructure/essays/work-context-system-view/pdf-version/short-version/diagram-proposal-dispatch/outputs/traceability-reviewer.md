Section and placement:  
Section 4, “A system for work,” immediately after its final paragraph, in the open lower half of page 3.

Diagram concept:  
**Recoverable Paths of Inquiry**

What it explains:  
The diagram shows two complementary movements: from an objective toward work pursued on its behalf, and from a bounded task back toward the purpose, decisions, conditions, dependencies, and evidence that explain it. It presents traceability as navigation through recoverable relationships, not as a workflow or complete representation of meaning.

Visual structure:  
- One central **Task** node.
- Connected nodes for **Objective**, a second **Objective**, **Dependent work**, **Decision**, **Condition**, **Evidence**, and **Artifact**.
- An emphasized route from **Objective** through the central **Task** toward its **Artifact**, labeled **Follow contributing work**.
- Emphasized reverse routes from **Task** to **Objective**, **Decision**, and **Condition**, grouped under **Return to what gives the task meaning**.
- The second objective demonstrates that one task may contribute to several objectives.
- A dependency line connects the task to work elsewhere.
- A dotted link between **Decision** and **Evidence** is labeled as a question, **What evidence informed this?**, rather than asserting that evidence is always recorded.
- One interrupted relationship is marked **uncertain, disputed, or missing**.
- Arrowheads indicate direction of inquiry only. They must not imply causation, execution sequence, or containment.

Why this is the strongest idea:  
Section 4 defines the proposed system primarily by movements and questions: following an objective into its contributing work, returning from a task to its context, and inspecting what shaped or blocks work. A diagram makes those bidirectional paths simultaneously visible. This improves comprehension more than additional prose because the essay’s central claim concerns traversability across relationships. The revision removes the worker proposal’s stronger suggestion of a prescribed objective-to-work-to-result pipeline.

Evidence from the essay:  
- Page 2, Section 2 defines context as recoverable relationships connecting bounded work to what explains it; it also says local work accumulates decisions and artifacts.
- Page 3, Section 4 explicitly describes movement from an objective through contributing work and from a task back to purpose, decisions, and conditions.
- Page 3 states that a task may contribute to several objectives or depend on work elsewhere, rejecting a single fixed hierarchy.
- Page 3 requires uncertain, disputed, and missing relationships to remain visible.
- Section 4’s closing questions connect decisions with evidence and ask what is completed, unfinished, or blocking a task.

Document fit:  
A shallow, full-column network fits comfortably below the page-3 prose. Thin rust and warm-gray lines, black emphasis, restrained serif labels, and open-circle nodes would echo the title-page motif without repeating its interlocking-loop composition.

Risks or uncertainties:  
Even a small network could be mistaken for a canonical data model. Caption it as an example of recoverable inquiry paths. The artifact connection and decision-evidence link must remain qualified; the essay supports their relevance but does not specify mandatory records or fixed relationship types.
