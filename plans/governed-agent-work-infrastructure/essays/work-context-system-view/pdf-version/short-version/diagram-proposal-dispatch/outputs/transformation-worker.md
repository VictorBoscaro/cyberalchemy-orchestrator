Diagram concept:  
**How an Objective Becomes Divided Work**

What it explains:  
The diagram maps one objective across successive structural states: situated perception gives rise to intention and an initially monolithic objective; effort reveals distinctions; distinctions become composing objectives, questions, or tasks; bounded attention makes each part pursuable while carrying only partial context; division then distributes the context of the whole and permits locally coherent work to diverge from the larger objective. This makes the motivation for Section 4 visible.

Visual structure:  
- A left-to-right sequence of five compact states connected by thin directional lines.
- **Situated perception**: “experience,” “knowledge,” “biological constitution,” “social position,” and “cultural context” connect to perception under the label **influence, not determine**.
- **Intention + monolithic objective**: one undivided form with an indistinct interior.
- **Distinctions emerge**: the same form now contains “outcomes,” “constraints,” “questions,” and “possible routes.” The transition is labeled **time and effort**.
- **Composing parts**: objectives, questions, and tasks connect laterally within the whole, not as a hierarchy. A note states **composition changes as work proceeds**.
- **Bounded, divided work**: several local boundaries contain decisions, artifacts, and success criteria. Connections back to the broader objective vary in visibility. The carried connection is labeled **some purpose, assumptions, and prior decisions - never all**.
- Beneath the final transition: **pursuable locally / harder to recover as a whole**.
- Direction shows the essay’s argumentative development, not a deterministic or irreversible process.

Why this is the strongest idea:  
The essay’s key transformation spans a page break and several paragraphs: the same division that enables focused work also makes the whole difficult to recover. Reusing the objective across visual states lets readers perceive that relational continuity and tension at once. More prose would restate individual claims; this diagram exposes how those claims compose and why a system for work becomes necessary.

Evidence from the essay:  
Page 1, Section 1 describes situated perception as influential but non-mechanical, followed by intention toward a still-formless objective. Page 1, Section 2 explains that effort reveals distinctions and that resulting parts compose the whole rather than sit beneath it; their meaning and dependencies continue changing. Page 2’s continuation of Section 2 says bounded attention makes work pursuable, carries only part of the larger context, and enables local autonomy while introducing divergence risk. Page 2, Section 3 explains that division distributes whole-context across tasks and artifacts, allowing local success to conceal failure of the larger effort.

Document fit:  
A shallow horizontal diagram fits page 2’s large lower whitespace without reflowing prose. Thin black, gray, and muted red lines, serif labels, and restrained open forms would echo the title-page motif while remaining subordinate to it.

Risks or uncertainties:  
A pipeline appearance could imply mechanical causation, fixed stages, inevitable divergence, or irreversible loss. Labels must preserve influence, ongoing revision, partial context, and risk rather than certainty. Composing parts must not appear hierarchical.
