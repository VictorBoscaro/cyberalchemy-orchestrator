Section 4, “A system for work,” immediately after the paragraph ending with the requirement to keep the earlier state visible alongside what changed and why. Diagram concept:  
**Revision Without Erasure**

What it explains:  
The diagram shows that later understanding can change how an objective, decision, or work contribution is understood without making the earlier understanding disappear. It also shows that revision need not be final, complete, or uncontested: the later view remains open to further change, while missing or disputed reasons remain visible as gaps.

Visual structure:  
- Three horizontal, independent rows labeled **Objective**, **Decision**, and **Work contribution**.
- Each row begins inside an **Earlier understanding** boundary and continues through a central **Revision record** containing **What changed** and **Why**.
- Each row ends in a **Later understanding** boundary, using qualified labels: **may be revised**, **may be overturned**, and **may no longer contribute**.
- A thin return path from each later item to its earlier counterpart is labeled **Earlier state remains recoverable**.
- The three rows must not be joined into one synchronized event; a small label should state **Possible revisions; not necessarily concurrent**.
- The later boundary has an open right edge or continuation mark labeled **Further revision remains possible**, avoiding a terminal “current state.”
- A dashed revision link, keyed **uncertain, disputed, or missing**, demonstrates that the diagram records gaps rather than inventing coherence.

Why this is the strongest idea:  
The essay states the continuity requirement but leaves its temporal and epistemic consequences implicit. This diagram makes preservation through change inspectable while correcting the worker proposal’s strongest risk: a clean before-and-after composition could imply that revision produces a settled replacement state. Independent rows, qualified transitions, visible gaps, and an open continuation preserve the essay’s claim that work context remains revisable and incomplete.

Risks or uncertainties:  
Three rows may still be read as an exhaustive model or as changes that occur together. The “possible revisions” qualifier and visual separation are essential. “Why” must allow an unresolved or disputed value; otherwise the diagram would promise more recoverability than the essay supports.
