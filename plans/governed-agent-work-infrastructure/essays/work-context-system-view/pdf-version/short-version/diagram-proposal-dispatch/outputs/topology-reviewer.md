Section and placement:  
Section 4, “A system for work,” immediately after the second paragraph, following the rejection of a single fixed hierarchy.

Diagram concept:  
**Relationships Without a Fixed Hierarchy**

What it explains:  
The diagram shows that work context is recoverable through multiple typed relationships rather than one parent-child tree. A task may contribute to several objectives, depend on other work, and remain connected to decisions, conditions, evidence, and artifacts. Those paths can be inspected from either endpoint without making the underlying relation semantically symmetric.

Visual structure:  
Use a compact, non-tiered network with eight representative nodes: **Objective A**, **Objective B**, **Task X**, **Task Y**, **Decision**, **Condition**, **Evidence**, and **Artifact**.

Use directed, labeled relations whose direction matches the prose:

- Task X to both objectives: **contributes to**
- Task X to Task Y: **depends on**
- Decision to Task X: **shapes / gives meaning to**
- Condition to Task X: **gives meaning to**
- Evidence to Decision: **evidence for**
- Task X to Artifact: **accumulates**

Add a small note: **Each recorded relation can be inspected from either endpoint.** This expresses navigability without falsely making “depends on” or “contributes to” bidirectional. Show one dashed contribution link labeled **uncertain or disputed**, plus a separate open gap marker labeled **known relationship missing**. State that the network is **representative, not complete**.

Why this is the strongest idea:  
Section 4’s central structural claim is easy to misread as a richer hierarchy. A typed network makes the decisive difference immediately inspectable: one task can participate in several objectives and cross-work dependencies while retaining explanatory links to contextual records. It also prevents the essay’s “recoverable relationships” from being mistaken for mere document storage. A diagram communicates this topology more efficiently than another paragraph because multiplicity, crossing paths, and qualified gaps must be perceived together.

Evidence from the essay:  
Page 1, section 2 says parts compose the whole rather than sit beneath it and may later reveal dependencies elsewhere. Page 2 defines context as recoverable relationships and says bounded work accumulates decisions and artifacts; section 3 says whole-context becomes distributed across tasks and artifacts. Page 3, section 4 explicitly supports objective-to-work and task-to-purpose, decisions, and conditions traversal; states that a task may serve several objectives or depend on other work; connects decisions with evidence through its closing questions; and requires uncertain, disputed, or missing relationships to remain visible.

A column-width network approximately 50-60 mm high can occupy the substantial open space on page 3 while preserving the section’s sequence. Risks or uncertainties:  
The worker’s undirected-line proposal would blur semantic direction, and its free-ended broken connector conflates uncertainty with missing information. “Evidence for” must not imply decisive proof, while the sample topology must not appear exhaustive or prescribe a concrete data model.
