# Research Initial Definitions: External Composition Precedents

## Context

Cyberalchemy Orchestrator develops infrastructure intended to keep work connected to the
objectives, decisions, assumptions, actions, and evidence that give it meaning. The Composition
Lab supports that purpose by treating composition as an unresolved subject before turning it into
an architecture, interface, or executable tool.

The project wants to support ways of working formed from heterogeneous materials such as skills,
interfaces, artifacts, tasks, knowledge, and agents. It does not yet have a justified account of
what makes such formation compositional, how composition differs from neighboring phenomena, or
which distinctions remain valid across domains. Resolving that uncertainty matters because an
unsupported generalization could turn one local practice into an inappropriate model for the
whole project.

## Purpose

This document establishes the informational starting point for an external line of research on
composition. Its eventual evidence will inform later comparison with the repository's internal
uses and later decisions about how composition may be understood, represented, evaluated, and
treated in the Composition Lab.

This document does not select a theory of composition, establish a definition, or authorize a
product or architecture decision.

## Research Question (Can be refined)

How do external accounts treat composition, distinguish it from neighboring
phenomena, and delimit what their accounts could legitimately contribute to understanding the
composition of skills, interfaces, artifacts, knowledge, work, agents, and lenses in this project?

## Confirmed Product Constraints

- Composition in general is the object of the Composition Lab; it must not be reduced in advance
  to lens composition or to the design of a particular tool, workflow, runtime, or interface.
- Lens composition is the first empirical case because the repository already preserves relevant
  practices and records. It is not the definition or presumed universal model of composition.
- External concepts do not have authority to reclassify internal repository evidence merely by
  being imported into the project.
- Similar terminology across domains does not by itself establish a shared mechanism or justify
  transfer into the project.
- Later claims must remain no stronger than their evidence and must preserve unresolved
  differences and limits.
- Research findings remain distinct from later product, architecture, interface, runtime, or
  governance decisions.

## Current Evidence Baseline

- The Composition Lab is an incubating research space. It has no accepted theory, defined
  interface, or executable tool, and its central question treats the meaning and treatment of
  composition as open. ([Composition Lab README](../../README.md))
- The broader research program states that the project does not yet know what separates
  composition from aggregation, sequence, configuration, integration, coordination, or a later
  interpretation that parts form a whole. It permits either a common mechanism, a family of
  mechanisms, or continued separation among some uses of the term.
  ([Research program](../../research-program.md))
- The program's initial definitions establish heterogeneous materials—including skills,
  interfaces, ways of working, artifacts, tasks, and knowledge—as relevant to the subject, while
  explicitly withholding any settled theory or formalism.
  ([General initial definitions](../research-initial-definitions.md))
- Existing project context reports multiple local uses of composition language and a rich initial
  case involving lenses. That establishes a reason to seek external context, but it does not show
  that those uses share a mechanism or fit any external account.
  ([General initial definitions](../research-initial-definitions.md))
## Known Gaps

- It is not known whether composition is governed by one common account, by several
  domain-specific accounts, or by a family of partially related phenomena.
- It is not known whether external accounts refer to the same phenomenon when they use language
  translated or interpreted as composition.
- The boundaries between composition and neighboring phenomena remain unclear, including whether
  those boundaries are stable or domain-dependent.
- It is unclear which commitments external accounts make about parts, wholes, relations,
  operations, processes, purposes, environments, observers, or outcomes.
- It is unknown whether the identity of parts must precede composition or can itself change through
  composing.
- It is unknown which external distinctions concern the process of composing, the resulting whole,
  judgments about that whole, or some combination of these.
- The roles of order, compatibility, transformation, preservation, loss, closure, emergence, and
  failure remain unresolved across domains.
- It is unclear when an external concept explains an observed difference and when it merely gives
  that difference a new name.
- The conditions under which a concept can transfer from its original setting to this project's
  heterogeneous materials are not established.
- It is not yet known which external accounts are compatible, complementary, in tension, or
  incommensurable.
- The current project baseline contains no external evidence establishing which, if any,
  properties observed in lens composition also characterize composition more generally.
