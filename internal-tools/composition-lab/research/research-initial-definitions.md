# Research Initial Definitions: Composition as a Phenomenon

## Context

Cyberalchemy Orchestrator develops infrastructure intended to keep work connected to the
objectives, decisions, assumptions, actions, and evidence that give it meaning. The repository
already depends on composition in several senses: work is divided and recombined, capabilities are
assembled, structures interact through interfaces, and local results are related back to larger
purposes.

The project may eventually support people in composing skills, interfaces, ways of working,
artifacts, tasks, and knowledge. Before treating that possibility as a product architecture,
however, the project needs a better account of composition itself. Without it, a system may merely
place parts beside one another, encode one favored workflow, or call arbitrary aggregation
“composition” without knowing what was formed or whether the result works as a whole.

## Purpose

This document establishes the informational starting point for research into composition. That
research is intended to inform later decisions about how composition should be represented,
supported, evaluated, and governed in the project, including—but not limited to—possible internal
tools and external coordination mechanisms.

It does not establish a theory of composition, select a formalism, or authorize a product design.

## Research Question (Can be refined)

How does composition happen, what makes an occurrence genuinely compositional, and how should
composition itself be understood and treated in systems that let people form ways of working from
skills, interfaces, artifacts, tasks, and knowledge?

## Confirmed Product Constraints

- Composition itself is the object of investigation; it must not be reduced in advance to the
  design of an `internal_tool`, workflow engine, or orchestration interface.
- The project does not yet know the answer. Existing definitions and formalisms are evidence or
  candidates, not settled foundations.
- Lens composition is the first empirical case because the repository already contains relevant
  practices and records. It is not the definition, scope boundary, or presumed model of
  composition in general.
- The eventual subject includes heterogeneous materials: skills, interfaces, ways of working,
  artifacts, work, and knowledge.
- Any later system-level proposal must remain connected to objectives, decisions, assumptions,
  actions, and evidence, consistent with the repository's governing purpose.

## Current Evidence Baseline

- The repository currently describes its objective as preserving licensed connections between
  work and the reasons for relying on it. It also distinguishes what currently runs from broader
  research theses. ([Repository README](../../../README.md))
- A provisional operational-language document distinguishes composition from compatibility,
  equivalence, and compression. It currently describes composition as connecting typed
  differences through a valid interface or rule, while explicitly leaving dependencies such as
  formation judgments, closure, identity, and associativity unresolved.
  ([Operational Knowledge Language](../../../docs/temps/operational-knowledge-language/README.md))
- A draft essay on textual composition treats composition as more than the presence and arrangement
  of parts: dependencies, emphasis, movement, traversal, and contribution to a whole may matter.
  The essay explicitly limits its scope and disclaims completeness and empirical validation.
  ([Evaluating Text as Composition](../../../vault/essays/evaluating-text-as-composition.md))
- Existing repository artifacts use “composition” across workflows, graphs, prose, software
  interfaces, knowledge structures, and multi-agent work. This establishes multiple local usages,
  but not that they share one mechanism or admit one general theory.
- The existing Milestone 1 program and repository inventory define a detailed study of lens
  composition. They remain useful as the first case history, but their lens-specific question and
  deliverables do not govern the broader Composition Lab program.
  ([Milestone 1 integrated program](../orchestration/milestone-1-strategy/04-integrated-program.md);
  [Lens inventory initial definitions](milestone-1/01-repository-inventory/research-initial-definitions.md))

## Known Gaps

- It is not yet known what distinguishes composition from aggregation, sequencing, containment,
  configuration, integration, coordination, or mere coexistence.
- The necessary participants in composition are unclear: parts, interfaces, constraints, an
  orienting purpose, an environment, an operator, an observer, or some combination.
- It is unclear whether composition is best understood as an operation, process, relation,
  judgment, achievement, emergent event, or a family of phenomena that should not be unified.
- The roles of compatibility, typing, ordering, interaction, transformation, preservation, and
  closure are unresolved.
- It is unknown when properties of a whole are produced by composition, merely selected by an
  observer, inherited from parts, or imposed by an evaluation criterion.
- It is unclear which aspects of composition transfer across software, work practices, knowledge,
  interfaces, organizations, texts, music, and other domains, and which are domain-specific.
- There is no established observational vocabulary for recording a composition as it forms,
  changes, succeeds, fails, or decomposes.
- There is no established empirical basis for comparing alternative compositional arrangements or
  for attributing an outcome to the composition rather than to the quality of its individual parts.
- It is not known how user authorship, system constraints, and emergent effects divide authority
  over a composition.
- The relationship between composing a way of working and externally coordinating multiple
  compositions remains open.
- It is not known which observations from the lens-composition case are specific to multi-agent
  inquiry and which, if any, recur in composition of skills, workflows, interfaces, artifacts, or
  knowledge.
