# Research Initial Definitions: Internal Uses of Composition

## Context

Cyberalchemy Orchestrator develops infrastructure that keeps agent work connected to the
objectives, decisions, assumptions, actions, and evidence that give it meaning. Across the
repository, heterogeneous elements are assembled, related, sequenced, transformed, and described
as contributing to larger wholes, including agents and lenses, skills, workflows, interfaces,
artifacts, and knowledge.

The repository already contains both claims about composition and records of systems or practices
that may instantiate it, but it is not yet known whether those occurrences share a mechanism or
whether some are better understood as aggregation, sequencing, configuration, integration,
coordination, or interpretation after the fact.

## Purpose

This document establishes the informational starting point for a comparative internal study of
composition in the repository. The resulting understanding will inform later refinement of the
Composition Lab's account of composition, the interpretation of the lens-composition case, and
decisions about which repository practices warrant comparison with external precedents.

It does not define composition, establish a repository-wide vocabulary, or authorize a product or
architecture decision.

## Research Question (Can be refined)

What forms of composition does the repository claim or realize across lenses, skills, workflows,
artifacts and knowledge, and interfaces; what, if anything, recurs across those domains; and which
occurrences are adequately explained by neighboring phenomena such as aggregation, sequencing,
configuration, integration, coordination, or retrospective interpretation?

## Confirmed Product Constraints

- Composition in general is the object of the Composition Lab; it must not be reduced to lens
  composition or to the design of a particular tool, workflow engine, or orchestration interface.
- Lens composition is the first case and empirical anchor because the repository already preserves
  relevant practices and records. It is not the definition or presumed universal model of
  composition.
- Lens-specific terms and observations must remain local to that case unless evidence supports
  their relevance elsewhere.
- Claims, prescriptions, configurations, executions, and observed effects must not be treated as
  equivalent kinds of evidence.
- Internal evidence does not by itself establish that a recurring repository pattern is a general
  property of composition.
- Any later system-level proposal must remain connected to objectives, decisions, assumptions,
  actions, and evidence, consistent with the repository's governing purpose.

## Current Evidence Baseline

- The Composition Lab is an incubating research space whose object is composition itself, including
  how heterogeneous materials form ways of working. It explicitly treats lens composition as its
  first empirical case rather than as the boundary of the field.
  ([Composition Lab README](../../README.md))
- The research program records repository uses of compositional language and practices across
  agent perspectives, capabilities, artifacts, interfaces, and knowledge structures. It also
  records that this multiplicity does not demonstrate a shared mechanism.
  ([Research program](../../research-program.md))
- The general initial definitions establish that the project may eventually support composition of
  skills, interfaces, ways of working, artifacts, tasks, and knowledge, while leaving the nature of
  composition unresolved.
  ([General initial definitions](../research-initial-definitions.md))
- The repository already treats lens composition as a comparatively rich local case involving
  distributed perspectives, information boundaries, confrontation of results, and synthesis. This
  establishes an empirical anchor, not proof that those features characterize composition in other
  domains. ([Research program — “Por que começar com lentes”](../../research-program.md))

## Known Gaps

- It is not known which repository occurrences are compositions rather than uses of compositional
  language or instances of neighboring phenomena.
- It is unclear whether the repository's uses across lenses, skills, workflows, artifacts and
  knowledge, and interfaces share any relations, transformations, or conditions.
- It is not known which features of the lens case are specific to multi-agent inquiry and which, if
  any, recur outside it.
- The boundaries among composition, aggregation, sequencing, configuration, integration,
  coordination, and retrospective interpretation are not established in the repository's actual
  uses.
- It is unclear how often the repository preserves evidence of an arrangement being instantiated,
  as distinct from described or configured.
- It is unclear when repository records support an observed effect of a whole, rather than only the
  presence or quality of its parts.
- It is not known whether similar terminology in different repository domains refers to the same
  phenomenon, related phenomena, or unrelated practices.
- It is unclear whether practices that do not use the word “composition” instantiate relevant
  forms that named examples omit.
- The evidential relationship between prescribed structures, runtime behavior, preserved
  artifacts, and attributed outcomes remains unresolved.
- It is not yet known which internal observations are suitable for comparison with external
  accounts of composition without importing a local category as a universal one.
