## Key Findings

- The central question combines four different judgments: possible strengthening, territory outside
  Cyberalchemy's proposals, Cyberalchemy-only capabilities, and reuse. Those judgments do not share
  one comparison boundary: the first two concern relevance and scope, the third requires defensible
  negative claims, and the fourth additionally concerns transferability. They must remain distinct
  in the later comparison rather than collapse into a feature checklist
  (`../dialogue.md:26-32`).
- The current wording is state-asymmetric: external projects are asked what they "provide," while
  Cyberalchemy is partly characterized by what it is "proposing." The session itself requires a
  Cyberalchemy baseline split into built, proposed, open, or contested and rejects README claims as
  proof. A fair boundary must therefore state the evidence status on both sides before comparing
  capabilities (`../dialogue.md:28-30`, `../dialogue.md:38`, `../dialogue.md:47-48`).
- The eight repositories are a nominated candidate set, not yet an established peer class. The
  preserved record lists their names while explicitly challenging whether they are comparable; it
  does not define a common system boundary, lifecycle stage, or unit of comparison
  (`../dialogue.md:15-24`, `../dialogue.md:36`).
- "Strengthen" and "gap" currently lack a settled normative anchor. Governance/evidence-first and
  repository-local are assumptions under challenge, while absence may represent a deliberate
  boundary. Until Cyberalchemy's intended boundary is explicit, feature presence cannot by itself
  establish either value or deficiency (`../dialogue.md:37`, `../dialogue.md:39`).
- Reuse is already bounded into concepts, patterns, and code, with code subject to licensing and
  implementation fit. The preserved context supplies no license, provenance, dependency, or
  architectural-compatibility evidence for any candidate, so code-level reusability remains an
  unknown rather than an inferred benefit (`../dialogue.md:40-41`).

## Gaps or Inconsistencies

- The agent prompt says to use already-established candidate summaries, but the preserved session
  contains only repository identifiers. The summaries, their sources, and their evidence status are
  absent, so they cannot support repository-level claims from this record
  (`../dialogue.md:15-24`, `../dialogue.md:71-77`).
- No boundary distinguishes a repository's implemented open-source surface from a broader hosted
  product, documentation promise, example application, or ecosystem integration. "Project
  provides" is therefore ambiguous (`../dialogue.md:28-30`, `../dialogue.md:38`).
- No evidentiary meaning is defined for a negative claim such as "they do not have X." The rule that
  README claims are insufficient addresses positive claims but does not make absence observable
  (`../dialogue.md:29`, `../dialogue.md:38`).
- No revision or time boundary is recorded for either Cyberalchemy or the eight external
  repositories. Without one, "has," "proposes," and "does not have" can refer to different states
  (`../dialogue.md:28-30`; the session records only its own date at `../dialogue.md:3-5`).
- "Repository-local" is not defined here: it could describe storage, execution, authority,
  governance records, or all four. Challenging that assumption without resolving its meaning risks
  comparing unlike boundaries (`../dialogue.md:37`).

## Local Tensions

- The session demands stronger evidence than README claims, yet the comparative-framing prompt
  relies on candidate summaries that are not preserved with evidence. The intended input is less
  auditable than the standard the later research is expected to enforce
  (`../dialogue.md:38`, `../dialogue.md:71-77`, `../dialogue.md:90-92`).
- The central question invites identification of missing and unique capabilities, while the
  assumptions warn that absence may be intentional. This creates a direct tension between feature
  difference and warranted deficiency: the former does not establish the latter
  (`../dialogue.md:28-30`, `../dialogue.md:39`).
- Governance/evidence-first and repository-local appear both as candidate identity constraints and
  as assumptions to challenge. Treating them as fixed would prejudge the comparison; treating them
  as disposable would erase the current project boundary before it has been evaluated
  (`../dialogue.md:34-41`).
- The session is expressly limited to informational definitions, but its central question uses
  decision-bearing terms such as "strengthen" and "reusable." At this stage those terms can only be
  bounded, not answered (`../dialogue.md:10-13`, `../dialogue.md:26-32`).

## Questions for Synthesis

- Are the eight repositories intended to be peers, or references from different system layers whose
  differences must not be scored as if they offered the same product boundary?
- Which Cyberalchemy properties are current facts, provisional design commitments, and assumptions
  still open to challenge?
- What must "repository-local" and "governance/evidence-first" mean for the later comparison to
  preserve Cyberalchemy's objective without assuming the conclusion?
- What evidence boundary can justify "does not have" rather than the weaker and more supportable
  "not evidenced within the inspected public surface"?
- Which repository revisions and which artifact surfaces are in scope when state-bearing claims are
  eventually made?
