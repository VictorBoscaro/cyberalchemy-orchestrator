## Scope and evidence discipline

This is a comparison between the frozen Phase-A developmental/educational map and the initial definitions. It is not an essay draft. It does not treat the order of headings, questions, or examples as a developmental sequence. It makes no novelty claim.

The native comparison is typed by:

`content or competence × bearer × attribution criterion × timescale × mechanism × setting × persistence or failure mode × normative standard × evidence`

Without those coordinates, “knowledge formation” is too broad to support a single causal or developmental account.

Only four external boundary searches were added. They address testimony, affect and motivation, tacit knowing, and cultural transmission. The first three produced useful supplements. The transmission search did not yield a sufficiently direct, stable primary source and is therefore not used as evidence.

## Executive verdict

The initial definitions already avoid several familiar errors: they do not make an explicit question the universal beginning of knowledge; they refuse a compulsory linear sequence; they do not equate learning, memory, belief, skill, testimony, and knowledge; and they leave the bearer open beyond the isolated individual. Those are substantive confirmations.

The central unresolved problem is not another missing item in the list. It is that four different targets remain folded into “knowledge acquiring form”:

1. a capacity, representation, strategy, practice, or coordination changes;
2. an observer, learner, or community recognizes or attributes knowledge;
3. a claim or practice meets some epistemic or institutional norm;
4. an achievement is stabilized, transmitted, or preserved.

These targets can coincide, but the developmental literature supplies counterexamples to their equivalence. A framing that does not split them will repeatedly mistake a change in evidence for a change in competence, public acceptance for epistemic warrant, or preservation of a statement for preservation of understanding.

## 2. Vocabulary that the framing may be imposing

### 2.1 “Movement”

“Experiential, cognitive, social, and epistemic movements” is plural and cautious, but *movement* still suggests continuity, direction, and a process that travels. Some cases are better described as reorganization, selection among strategies, coordination, stabilization, participation, or a changed attribution. The term should not be the sole generic.

**Correction:** use “changes, relations, practices, and attribution conditions” unless a source warrants a directional process.

### 2.2 “Knowledge acquiring form”

This phrase can imply that knowledge exists as a preformed substrate awaiting shape, or that there is one target form. Developmental accounts instead concern changes in representations, inferential reach, skills, strategies, participation, coordination, and public artifacts. Whether any of these changes counts as knowledge is a further attribution or normative question.

**Correction:** separate what changes from the rule by which the result is called knowledge.

### 2.3 “Phenomenological”

A phenomenological entry can discipline attention to lived experience, but it can also privilege first-person availability and an individual experiential field. Distributed accomplishment, testimony, institutional memory, and knowledge that exceeds any participant’s representation do not fit automatically inside that register.

**Correction:** treat phenomenology as one evidential perspective, not as the universal container or origin of every bearer type.

### 2.4 “Receiving information”

This phrase carries a container or transfer metaphor: a stable item passes into a recipient. It can conceal interpretation, source evaluation, reconstruction, and changes produced by prior knowledge.

**Correction:** specify whether the event is exposure, uptake, encoding, interpretation, reliance on testimony, or successful learning.

### 2.5 “Transmission”

Transmission can suggest that content remains invariant across persons or generations. A statement may be copied while its inferential role is lost; a practice may persist while its rationale changes; a learner may reconstruct rather than receive an understanding.

**Correction:** report separately the fidelity of expression, use, justification, skill, and surrounding enabling conditions.

### 2.6 “Subject or unit”

The phrase combines at least four questions: who or what bears the achievement, what the unit of analysis is, where the relevant process occurs, and to what target an observer attributes knowledge.

**Correction:** keep **bearer**, **unit of analysis**, **causal locus**, and **attribution target** distinct.

### 2.7 “Accepted under recorded conditions”

This is appropriate as a local lifecycle status. Outside that register it risks equating acceptance with knowledge and privileging mature propositional, documentary, and institutional forms over practical or tacit competence.

**Correction:** label it explicitly as an architectural status predicate, not a general definition of knowledge.

### 2.8 “Boundaries among” the listed terms

The wording can imply that each term occupies a clean partition. The definitions correctly deny mutual exclusivity, but the spatial metaphor can restore the separation it disclaims.

**Correction:** investigate relations, attribution criteria, and failure cases rather than presume fixed boundaries.

## 3. Material omissions

### 3.1 An explicit attribution test

For each example, the framing needs to ask:

- What content or competence is attributed?
- To which bearer?
- By which observable criterion?
- Under what task, support, and cultural conditions?
- Which alternative explanation has been excluded?

Object-permanence research is the canonical warning: looking-time, anticipatory response, and manual search need not yield the same attribution at the same age. A discrepancy may reflect response demands, inhibition, memory, or action planning rather than the presence or absence of a single all-purpose concept.

### 3.2 Competence versus performance

Failure on a task does not directly demonstrate absent knowledge, and success does not by itself show a stable, general competence. Support, cueing, language, inhibition, familiarity, and response modality can change performance.

**Required addition:** every developmental example should state whether its evidence concerns spontaneous performance, supported performance, retained competence, or transfer.

### 3.3 Culture and task meaning

Tasks are not culturally neutral windows. School arithmetic and market arithmetic can recruit different routines, artifacts, and meanings. A result may fail to transfer without showing that nothing was learned.

**Required addition:** record setting, cultural practice, task interpretation, and the population across which the attribution is meant to generalize.

### 3.4 Timescale

Microgenetic strategy change, developmental reorganization, apprenticeship, institutional stabilization, and intergenerational transmission occur on different timescales. They should not be narrated as phases of one process without evidence.

**Required addition:** attach a timescale to every claimed change.

### 3.5 Change architecture

The framing names revision but does not distinguish:

- replacement;
- reorganization;
- enrichment or differentiation;
- redistribution among competing strategies;
- coexistence with contextual selection;
- inhibition or suppression without elimination;
- coordination across persons and artifacts.

Conceptual-change evidence often supports coexistence rather than eradication. Strategy research likewise shows overlapping procedures rather than one clean succession.

### 3.6 Context and transfer

Knowledge displayed in one setting may not appear in another. Transfer varies with content, context, temporal distance, functional relation, and prompting.

**Required addition:** specify whether a claim is local performance, near transfer, far transfer, or context-independent competence.

### 3.7 Formation, attribution, warrant, and stabilization

These four targets are presently adjacent but not cleanly separated.

| Target | Core question | Typical evidence | Characteristic error |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formation or reorganization | What changed in capacity, representation, strategy, practice, or coordination? | Longitudinal, microgenetic, learning, process, or intervention evidence | Inferring an inner change from one successful response |
| Attribution or recognition | Why does an observer or community count the bearer as knowing? | Task criteria, discourse, assessment, source evaluation | Treating the criterion as the phenomenon itself |
| Warrant | What makes the belief, claim, or practice epistemically acceptable? | Reasons, reliability, evidence, norms, defeaters | Equating social acceptance with justification |
| Stabilization or preservation | What continues across time, persons, or settings? | Retention, transfer, replication, records, practice continuity | Treating statement persistence as understanding persistence |

### 3.8 Causal versus normative vocabulary

“Epistemic movement” can mix a causal description of change with a normative judgment that the result is justified or reliable.

**Required addition:** mark whether each claim is descriptive, causal, attributional, or normative.

### 3.9 Public knowledge versus individual learning

A body of public claims can be preserved while no individual understands the whole; an individual can learn while no public artifact changes. Distributed systems can achieve outcomes no single member can reproduce.

**Required addition:** do not infer change in one bearer from persistence or performance at another level.

### 3.10 Error modes in the observer’s account

The framing should name false positives and false negatives in knowledge attribution:

- prompted success mistaken for stable competence;
- verbal failure mistaken for conceptual absence;
- copied language mistaken for understanding;
- fluent performance mistaken for justified belief;
- group output attributed to each participant;
- persistence of records mistaken for preservation of usable orientation.

### 3.11 Power, access, and legitimacy

Participation and institutional acceptance are shaped by authority, access to tools, and whose testimony or competence is recognized. These conditions affect visibility and attribution without automatically determining truth or warrant.

**Required addition:** distinguish social authorization from epistemic justification while examining their interaction.

### 3.12 Affect and motivation

The known-gaps list includes affect, but it remains untyped. Pintrich, Marx, and Boyle show that goals, values, self-efficacy, control beliefs, and classroom context mediate conceptual change. Treating cognition as “cold” can misdescribe both engagement with anomalous evidence and persistence through revision.

**Required addition:** type affect and motivation as possible mediators or constituents in particular mechanisms, not as a decorative contextual list.

### 3.13 Testimony as selective reliance

Testimony is more than transferred content. Children evaluate source knowledge and prior accuracy when deciding whom to trust. Koenig and colleagues show that young children selectively rely on knowledgeable or previously accurate speakers for new information.

**Required addition:** distinguish exposure to testimony, comprehension, source evaluation, reliance, retention, and warranted acceptance.

### 3.14 Tacit knowing

Tacit knowing is not merely an unrecorded proposition. It can pervade practical, propositional, and experiential knowledge, and may be expressed in discrimination or skilled action without exhaustive verbalization.

**Required addition:** do not use explicit articulation or recorded provenance as the universal criterion for knowledge attribution.

## 4. False equivalences to block explicitly

| Do not equate | Reason |
|---|---|
| Becoming discernible with arising | Better evidence may reveal a pre-existing capacity. |
| Acquiring form with being attributed | A phenomenon and a criterion for recognizing it are different objects. |
| Formation with justification | A belief can form without being warranted; a warranted public claim can be learned poorly. |
| Social acceptance with epistemic warrant | Acceptance may reflect authority, convention, or institutional procedure. |
| Participation with internalization | Coordinated success need not imply an equivalent representation in every participant. |
| Transmission with preserved understanding | Expressions can persist while use, rationale, or competence decays. |
| Learning with knowledge | Learning is a change process; knowledge is an attribution or normative status under some criterion. |
| Memory with knowledge | Retention can preserve error; knowing can rely on external resources. |
| Confidence with justification | Metacognitive assurance and evidential warrant can diverge. |
| Individual, group, institution, and distributed system as equivalent bearers | Their composition, evidence, responsibility, and persistence conditions differ. |
| Stage-like aggregate pattern with a universal stage | Task and support effects can generate apparent discontinuities. |
| Correct response with stable competence | Guessing, cueing, imitation, or narrow task learning remain alternatives. |

## 5. Proposed corrections to the research frame

### 5.1 Replace the provisional question

Current form: a search for “movements” participating in “knowledge acquiring form.”

Recommended research question:

> Which changes, relations, practices, and attribution conditions are described as knowledge forming; with respect to what content or competence, bearer, criterion, timescale, mechanism, context, persistence or failure mode, normative standard, and evidence? Where do translations among these descriptions fail?

This is a research-frame correction, not proposed essay prose.

### 5.2 Split the object of inquiry

Maintain four linked but non-identical registers:

1. **formation/reorganization** of capacities, representations, strategies, practices, or coordination;
2. **discernibility/attribution** by the learner, observer, or community;
3. **warrant/acceptability** under an explicit norm;
4. **stabilization/preservation** across time, people, and settings.

Any later synthesis should state which register it occupies and justify crossings between them.

### 5.3 Add a claim-typing template

For every general sentence, record:

```text
content_or_competence:
bearer:
attribution_criterion:
timescale:
mechanism:
setting_and_culture:
persistence_or_failure_mode:
normative_standard:
evidence_and_alternatives:
```

### 5.4 Add four mandatory counterexample tests

1. **Criterion sensitivity:** infant expectation succeeds while manual search fails.
2. **Context sensitivity:** competence appears in market arithmetic but not school-format arithmetic, or conversely.
3. **Coexistence:** scientific reasoning is available while intuitive explanations remain active.
4. **Distributed accomplishment:** coordinated navigation succeeds although no individual contains the whole procedure or state.

A general formulation that cannot state what it predicts in all four cases is underspecified.

### 5.5 Type testimony

Replace a generic “testimony/transmission” relation with:

`speaker/source × content × recipient × source-evaluation cue × uptake × retention × warrant × later use`

This prevents reliable source selection from being reduced to passive reception.

### 5.6 Type persistence

Ask separately whether the following persist:

- wording or inscription;
- retrievable belief;
- inferential understanding;
- practical skill;
- coordinated practice;
- access to artifacts;
- provenance and defeaters;
- institutional capacity;
- reliable orientation in a setting.

### 5.7 Constrain the architectural definition

Use the operational definition only in the form:

> Within this architecture, an item receives the lifecycle status “knowledge” when it is accepted under recorded conditions of provenance, evidence, review, scope, version, and state.

Do not infer that this predicate exhausts ordinary, developmental, practical, social, or epistemological uses of knowledge.

## 6. Boundary decisions

### Decision A — no universal first movement

The developmental map does not justify choosing perception, information reception, explicit questioning, memory, social recognition, or justification as the universal beginning. Any such choice must be labeled as an expository entry point.

### Decision B — phenomenology is a perspective, not a bearer theory

Lived experience can organize an introduction, but it does not settle whether the relevant bearer is an individual, dyad, practice, institution, or distributed system.

### Decision C — architecture and development use different status predicates

Recorded acceptance is a lifecycle predicate. Developmental competence, practical knowing, and epistemic warrant require other criteria. Translation is possible only after the predicates are stated.

### Decision D — local ordered dependencies remain admissible

Rejecting a universal stage sequence does not require rejecting every prerequisite relation. A source may establish a local dependency for a specified task, population, mechanism, and support condition.

### Decision E — “knowledge” may appear early only with a typed local use

An early occurrence is defensible if the bearer and attribution criterion are named and the text does not silently universalize that use.

## 7. Collapse tests for the corrected frame

These are not novelty claims; they are tests of whether the proposed distinctions add analytical value.

- **Formation/attribution split collapses** if every change in the available evidence is shown to coincide with a change in the relevant competence across tasks and supports. Existing criterion-sensitive findings argue against that collapse.
- **Bearer typing collapses** if individual, group, institutional, and distributed attributions have identical evidence, composition, responsibility, and persistence conditions. Existing distributed-cognition cases argue against that collapse.
- **Persistence typing collapses** if preserved wording reliably entails preserved understanding, skill, practice, access, provenance, and orientation. The initial definitions themselves anticipate failures of this entailment.
- **Context typing collapses** if competence and transfer are invariant across task format, setting, cultural practice, and support. Existing transfer and situated-cognition evidence argues against that collapse.

## 8. Dissent and residual uncertainty

### Dissent

The initial definitions are careful enough to resist a stage narrative, but their central phrase still invites a single-process reading. Merely expanding the inventory of factors would not fix that problem. The inquiry needs a typed grammar of claims before synthesis.

### Residual uncertainty

- The boundary between tacit competence and knowledge depends on the normative standard adopted; the developmental literature alone cannot settle it.
- Distributed accomplishment does not automatically entail distributed epistemic responsibility.
- Selective trust shows source evaluation, but not that every accepted testimonial belief is justified or understood.
- Affect and motivation can mediate conceptual change; whether they partly constitute particular forms of knowing requires a more specific claim and evidence base.
- Cultural transmission remains an evidence gap in this supplemental pass. No claim about fidelity or reconstructive transmission here relies on the discarded search result.
- If the project intends only a local editorial lexicon, the full scientific typing template may be heavier than necessary. In that case, it should say that the register is local and refrain from general claims about formation.

## 10. Final recommendation

Retain the initial definitions’ anti-origin, anti-stage, anti-synonymy, and anti-isolated-subject constraints. Before synthesis, revise the governing question and add the four-register split plus the nine-field claim type. The highest-priority correction is:

> Before asking how knowledge forms, state whether the inquiry concerns a change in capacity or practice, its recognition or attribution, its epistemic warrant, or its stabilization and preservation.

No one of these should stand in for the others without explicit evidence.
