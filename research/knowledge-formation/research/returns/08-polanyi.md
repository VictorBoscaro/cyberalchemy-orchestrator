The existing work essay and its PDF were excluded as conceptual evidence; the sections of the systems-and-categories essay that connect knowledge to work were also excluded.

**Verdict:** the repository contains a strong native protocol for *making explicit representations governable*: orient a distinction, contact a case, preserve a readout, interpret it under a criterion, keep the mismatch, revise without erasing history, and transmit exact records with provenance. It does **not** yet contain a positive account of embodied skill, tacit integration, learning as a change in a knower, or transmission as another person coming to understand. Architecture silently narrows those phenomena whenever it treats inspectability, persistence, exact transport, or accepted state as sufficient proxies for knowing.

No claim in this return is Lean-proved. `proved` is therefore intentionally absent from the claim ledger.

## Repository protocol for claims that may become reusable

The protocol implicit across the artifacts is:

1. Preserve the source occurrence and distinguish it from later interpretation.
2. State the lens, expectation, relevance criterion, scope, and contact by which a readout matters.
3. Separate signal, evidence, witness, judgment, and represented residue.
4. Keep descriptions provisional until evidence, review, acceptance, and authority are independently recorded.
5. Record direct relations and their witnesses; do not infer meaning from folder proximity, chronology, summaries, or lineage.
6. Append corrections and supersessions so the earlier account remains recoverable.
7. Promote only supported claims into a new artifact with its own contract; do not promote a notebook wholesale.
8. Transport content without silent rewriting, and keep the receiver’s interpretation attributable as a new act.

This is a coherent protocol for repository truthfulness. It becomes an experiential theory only by an unsupported translation: replacing “what can be retrieved and audited” with “what is known,” or replacing “what can be executed from an instruction artifact” with “what someone knows how to do.”

## Drift, gaps, and inconsistencies

### 1. “Knowledge machine” → governed record

The superseded seed Plan proposed a single T0 engine in which probing and residue enrich a knowledge model. The later architecture essays retreat to a safer account: append-only facts, hypothesis descriptions, acceptance boundaries, and inspectable provenance. This is a justified demotion, but the vocabulary still occasionally inherits the old ambition. “Knowledge store,” “knowledge graph,” and “learning” often name storage/promotion/revision mechanisms even where no account of a knower survives.

### 2. Learning → model revision or curriculum order

Two meanings dominate: (a) revising a representation after residue and (b) ordering concepts for reader comprehension. Neither addresses retention, embodied capacity, skilled performance, changed perception, or social apprenticeship. The repository should not infer that these are smaller instances of the same generic `learn` operation without a bridge.

### 3. Skill → instruction artifact

The protocol-compilation experiment preserves a skill’s “stable meaning” by versioning instructions, topology, parameters, and review obligations. That is useful engineering, but it silently replaces practical mastery with an executable text. A person following a skill file, an agent producing a compliant trace, and a practitioner who can discern what the instructions do not say are presently indistinguishable unless outcome evidence exposes the difference.

### 4. Observation → accepted event

The conceptual notebook makes observation situated and contact-dependent. Runtime architecture makes observations candidates for validation, journaling, aggregation, and replay. The translation is legitimate only if the accepted event keeps the contact, observer, lens, scope, and transformation that produced it. Otherwise the architecture preserves a payload while deleting the conditions under which it became a readout.

### 5. Interpretation is necessary conceptually and prohibited operationally

The notebook needs interpretation to turn signal into evidence. The fabric forbids reinterpretation in transit. This separation is sound for provenance, but there is no fully owned receiving layer where situated understanding is reconstructed, contested, or learned. The aggregator may interpret outputs, yet its product is another artifact/judgment, not evidence that recipients acquired the same sense.

### 6. Explicitness is both safeguard and distortion risk

The architecture repeatedly says that unrecorded distinctions cannot be recovered. The exploratory essay explicitly warns that some tacit, unstable, or contested distinctions should remain informal because explicitness can falsely stabilize them. The repository has no policy for preserving a *known non-explicit remainder* except open questions and prose caveats.

## Strongest conflict with operational containment

The strongest conflict is between **R5/D6 lossless transport** and the repository’s own **lens/contact account**.

- Operational containment correctly requires that the fabric not author, summarize, reinterpret, or rank content.
- Experiential inquiry says that a readout becomes evidence only through a lens, expectation, relevance criterion, and judgment.
- Therefore content can be transmitted without the understanding that made it evidence being transmitted.

If the system treats exact payload + provenance + accepted state as preserved knowledge, it commits the very category error the notebook avoids: it collapses occurrence, readout, interpretation, warrant, and residue into a transportable record. The repair is not to let the fabric interpret. It is to represent the recipient-side act of interpretation as a distinct, attributable, revisable occurrence and to keep open the possibility that sense was not transmitted.

## Reversal evidence

1. **T0/self-similarity reversal:** the archived seed claims one root loop across epistemic, categorical, and engineering faces, then records that no close row enriches the model and marks the current self-similar instance falsified. The Plan is now superseded.
2. **“Laws” → heuristics:** the operational notebook says earlier notes called compression/composition/refinement statements “laws”; it demotes them to conditional heuristics with domains, costs, exceptions, and falsifiers.
3. **Predictive grammar → accepted negative:** the notebook records `confirmed-kill early-stop`; its grammar remains editorial/integrative but loses incremental predictive status.
4. **Minimal kernel → dependency hypothesis:** the notebook prohibits calling orientation, distinction, relation/transformation, and composition minimal, sufficient, independent, or primitive until counterexamples and dependency analysis support those words.

These reversals are evidence of a healthy demotion protocol. They are also evidence that the architecture’s current containment vocabulary should not be mistaken for the mature epistemology the older “knowledge machine” name anticipated.

## Coherence test

**Test case:** transmit one identical readout, with identical source provenance, to two recipients who have different prior lenses or practical capacities.

The native account is coherent only if the repository can record all of the following without forcing equality:

1. one payload and one transmission history;
2. two recipient-side interpretations with their own lens/context;
3. possibly different evidence judgments and confidence;
4. possibly different abilities to use the result;
5. later revision of either interpretation without rewriting the original readout; and
6. an explicit “uptake unknown” state when no evidence of understanding or ability exists.

**Failure condition:** byte identity, provenance identity, or shared acceptance automatically produces one shared knowledge state. No launch-snapshot artifact demonstrates that the test passes. The test is therefore proposed, not implemented.

## Tensions and translation failures

| Translation attempted by the repository | What survives | What is lost or unowned |
|---|---|---|
| experience → artifact | attributable statement, scope, timestamp, status | felt salience, attention, bodily orientation, unarticulated discrimination |
| practice → skill file/protocol | repeatable instruction and execution boundary | connoisseurship, improvisation, knowing when the rule misfits |
| observation → event/readout | payload, observer metadata if supplied, replay | perceptual availability and the learned capacity to notice |
| interpretation → judgment | explicit claim, scale, rationale, acceptance state | pre-predicative integration and transformation of the interpreter |
| learning → revision | before/after versions, residue, new distinctions | retention, transfer, skilled action, changed perception |
| testimony/transmission → exact delivery | content identity, source, provenance, acknowledgement | recipient uptake, trust calibration, preservation of sense |
| memory → append-only history | recoverable prior records | living recollection, forgetting, salience, embodied readiness |

## Rejected synthesis move

Reject the smooth sequence:

`experience → observation → information → interpretation → learning → understanding → acceptance → knowledge → transmission`

It is unsupported for four independent reasons: the notebook says registers are not stages; its motifs may be projections; the learning-order artifact is pedagogical rather than epistemic; and architecture acceptance governs reuse/authority rather than truth or understanding. A polished sequence here would convert repository convenience into an origin claim.

## Closing assessment

The repository’s strongest native contribution is negative and architectural: do not erase the difference between a readout and its interpretation, a claim and its evidence, acceptance and authority, exact transport and authorship, or correction and historical rewrite. Its weakest area is the positive phenomenon Polanyi would force into view: skillful knowing often exceeds what can be stated, and explicit records can depend on capacities they do not contain. Until that remainder has an owner, the system should describe itself as governing explicit epistemic artifacts and their reuse—not as containing, transmitting, or producing knowledge without qualification.

**Dissent:** the launch corpus is more coherent than a simple “repository equals database” critique would suggest; it repeatedly marks its own boundaries and accepted negatives. My dissent is narrower: those caveats have not yet been carried into an operational representation of recipient uptake, tacit competence, or interpretive transformation, so the architecture still defaults to containment when experiential evidence is absent.
