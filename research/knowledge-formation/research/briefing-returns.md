### Native problem map

1. **No primitive epistemic given.** The 1868 cognition series denies immediate introspection, intuition as unmediated cognition, thought without signs, and a conception of the absolutely incognizable. Cognition is determined by prior cognition; self-knowledge is inferred through outward facts and testimony. This raises the problem of object-constraint without foundational givenness.

2. **Cognition and selfhood are sign-processes.** Every thought-sign is interpreted by a subsequent thought. Meaning is virtual and prospective. Persons and external symbols reciprocally educate one another, so attribution cannot be reduced to a punctual state inside an organism.

3. **Inquiry begins with disruption, not a universal origin.** Genuine doubt irritates an already established habit. Inquiry moves toward settled belief, but it presupposes prior habits. Many disturbances never become inquiry.

4. **Persistence is habitual and socially ambivalent.** Beliefs persist as action-guiding dispositions. Tenacity and authority can settle and preserve error by controlling exposure, teaching, and expression. Community is not automatically epistemically virtuous.

5. **Scientific correction is methodological, not automatic.** Science is distinctive because it makes present opinion answerable to external constraint, replication, prediction, experiment, criticism, and revision. Self-correction is neither quick nor guaranteed for every actual community. The first rule of logic adds an ethical-affective condition: one must desire to learn and not block inquiry.

6. **Discovery, consequence, and testing are irreducible.** Mature inquiry differentiates abduction (conjecture), deduction (consequences), and induction (testing). Early `hypothesis` was a syllogistic evidencing form and overlapped functionally with induction; mature abduction became the novelty-introducing methodological stage. Abduction is not inference to the best explanation and does not by itself warrant acceptance.

7. **Perception is inferential, but experience constrains interpretation.** Peirce rejects an uncontaminated perceptual foundation. Mature semeiotic nonetheless distinguishes immediate and dynamical objects, while collateral experience supplies familiarity a sign alone cannot furnish. This blocks both foundational givenness and frictionless interpretivism.

8. **Meaning-growth is not knowledge-growth.** Interpretants may be emotional, energetic, or logical; they may clarify, redirect, mislead, or reproduce a habit. Not every sign-effect adds information, and not every informational change is knowledge. Scholars disagree over whether infinite semiosis survives unchanged into the mature theory.

9. **Truth is not actual consensus.** Peirce links truth to the opinion indefinitely continued inquiry would reach, but finite agreement cannot constitute truth. Interpretations divide among literal convergence, counterfactual/regulative settlement, assertional commitment, and realist correspondence pragmatically clarified by inquiry. Buried or unrecoverable truths pressure literal convergence.

10. **Belief, confidence, and hope are not synonyms.** The 1877 doubt-to-belief model is complicated by the 1898 claim that belief has no place in pure science. Scientific inquiry may require confidence or hope rather than action-guiding settled belief. Vital matters cannot await indefinite inquiry and instead depend partly on inherited sentiment and instinct.

11. **Critical common-sensism gives starting commitments, not infallible foundations.** Inquiry begins from currently indubitable, vague, inherited habits. Changed experience can make them genuinely dubitable. This modifies Reid while resisting both total skepticism and Cartesian certainty.

12. **Attribution is distributed but not a simple group mind.** Persons bear habits and make assertions, yet signs, practices, objects, future interpretants, and an indefinite community help constitute epistemic significance. The community is temporally open and object-constrained, not a present consensus-agent.

### Established positions and owners

- Anti-intuitionism, inferential self-knowledge, thought in signs: Peirce's 1868 cognition series.
- Belief as habit and inquiry as genuine doubt-resolution: Peirce's 1877–78 *Illustrations of the Logic of Science*.
- Pragmatic clarification through conceivable practical bearings: Peirce, 1878 and 1905.
- Truth and reality related to indefinite inquiry but independent of finite opinion: Peirce; divergent reconstructions by Cheryl Misak and Christopher Hookway.
- Triadic semiosis and interpretant-development: Peirce; major reconstructions by Savan, Liszka, Short, Atkin, and Campos.
- Collateral experience as a boundary on interpretation: mature Peirce; emphasized by Mats Bergman.
- Differentiated abductive, deductive, and inductive functions: mature Peirce; clarified by Burks, Frankfurt, Fann, Anderson, Daniel McAuliffe, and Douglas Walton.
- Critical common-sensism and rational self-control: mature Peirce; developed by Hookway.
- Scientific confidence or hope versus belief and vital commitment: 1898 Peirce; reconstructed by Hookway and Kasser.

### Semantic changes to preserve

- In 1868 `intuition` means cognition not determined by a previous cognition of the same object, not an intelligent hunch.
- `Hypothesis` develops from a syllogistic inference form into abduction, retroduction, or presumption as a conjectural methodological function.
- `Belief` changes from the 1877 terminus of inquiry to something Peirce sometimes excludes from pure science in 1898.
- `Community` can mean actual mutual influence, coercive authority, an indefinite community of investigators, or an ideal future reference point; these are not interchangeable.
- `Interpretant` is not simply a human interpretation; it may be a further sign, effect, habit-change, or normatively specified outcome.
- `Information` can grow in persons and words without becoming warranted knowledge.
- `Pragmatism` becomes `pragmaticism` partly to distinguish Peirce from Jamesian and Schillerian uses.

### Disagreements and countertraditions

1. Cartesian foundationalism versus Peircean anti-intuitionism; a counter-reading argues that the latter is not as anti-Cartesian as standard historiography claims.
2. James's will to believe versus Peirce's will to learn; Peirce partly approaches James on vital matters.
3. Actual-consensus readings versus realist, regulative, and assertion-based interpretations of truth.
4. Infinite-semiosis readings versus Short's claim that infinity is chiefly characteristic of the early theory and mature habits/final interpretants provide qualified closure.
5. Abduction as IBE versus historically differentiated Peircean abduction.
6. Psychological doubt-resolution versus logic as normative self-control.
7. Ubiquitous semiosis versus Secondness and collateral experiential resistance.
8. Individualist epistemology versus the opposite overreach of treating the community as a literal present group mind.

### Concepts resisting unification

Cognition, belief, knowledge, confidence, hope, opinion, habit, information, interpretant, and truth; individual habit and communal settlement; actual consensus and ideal-limit convergence; emotional, energetic, and logical interpretants; abductive novelty, deductive explication, and inductive testing; perceptual judgment and collateral experience; common-sense indubitability and certainty; scientific inquiry and vital decisions; cessation of doubt and truth; semiotic development and warrant; human learning and cosmological habit-taking.

### Coherence verdict on `knowledge formation`

`Knowledge formation` is coherent only as an external and explicitly disjunctive umbrella over prior cognition becoming further cognition, genuine doubt becoming inquiry, abductive proposal undergoing deductive and inductive testing, interpretation changing habits, private uncertainty entering public criticism, and current confidence becoming corrigible. It is incoherent if it implies a substance produced at one moment, a sequence beginning in ignorance, settlement entailing truth, or one subject owning the result.

### Strongest expected cross-domain conflict

Epistemic attribution cannot be made adequate by identifying an individual organism's internal state at a time. For Peirce, cognitively significant content and warrant are diachronic, sign-mediated, environmentally constrained, and answerable to a temporally indefinite community. This is expected to conflict with individualist cognitive psychology, neuroscience, bounded Bayesian-agent models, and some machine-learning accounts. Peirce does not deny individual cognition; he denies its sufficiency.

### Reversal evidence

The strong anti-individualist claim would be reversed or materially weakened if manuscript-level chronology showed that Peirce abandoned the man-sign, external-sign, or indefinite-community theses; made mature final interpretants consistently private occurrent states; treated community only as a contingent aid to fully individual warrant; made collateral experience an immediately self-authenticating given; or rendered the 1898 belief/science distinction merely rhetorical. The wider map would weaken if `knowledge` proved to be a stable Peircean technical kind subsuming belief, information, inquiry, and interpretants across periods.

### Rejected synthesis move

Rejected: **Peirce offers a universal cycle in which surprise causes abduction, deduction, induction, communal consensus, and therefore knowledge.** Not all doubt becomes inquiry; inquiry need not follow a clean temporal cycle; abduction proposes but does not warrant; deduction need not add information; induction remains fallible; tenacity and authority also settle belief; consensus can be false; science may require hope rather than belief; vital matters differ; semiosis is broader than knowledge; and collateral experience prevents a closed, self-producing semiotic cycle.

### Confirmations

The boundary document correctly:

- refuses to assume an explicit question as knowledge's universal beginning; Peirce requires genuine doubt but also prior cognition and habit, and many epistemic changes are not inquiries;
- separates an expository beginning from an ontological or psychological origin;
- refuses to turn information, learning, understanding, belief, memory, skill, justification, confidence, testimony, and knowledge into synonyms or a staged ladder;
- keeps individual, relational, social, institutional, and distributed attribution unresolved rather than presuming an isolated subject;
- treats local operational knowledge as architectural rather than as a settled epistemology;
- leaves permanence disjunctive; Peirce likewise distinguishes habit-persistence, sign-records, community reaffirmation, and survival under future testing;
- recognizes that transmission of a statement may lose the conditions under which it was understood; Peirce's distinction among sign, collateral experience, object, and interpretant directly supports this warning;
- refuses to infer conceptual order from narrative order.

### Imposed vocabulary

The boundary's `formation`, `movement`, and `knowledge acquiring form` are not stable Peircean technical terms. They can organize the investigation only as external umbrella language. Peirce's native terms are cognition, thought-sign, habit, belief, genuine doubt, inquiry, inference, abduction, deduction, induction, experience, interpretant, information, self-control, truth, reality, and indefinite community.

`Discernible in experience, practice, and social life` is broad enough for Peirce but risks underdescribing the object-side compulsion of Secondness and the normative dimension of logic. `Accepted under recorded conditions` is legitimate architectural language but does not translate directly into Peircean belief, warrant, or truth.

### Omissions relative to the frozen Peircean map

1. **External constraint:** the document foregrounds distinctions and conditions but not sufficiently the dynamical object, Secondness, surprise, resistance, and collateral experience by which signs are constrained.
2. **Bad persistence:** social and institutional locations are named, but not Peirce's warning that authority can preserve error by controlling attention and expression.
3. **Normative inquiry:** provenance and review are listed, but the ethical requirement to desire learning and avoid blocking inquiry is absent.
4. **Temporal openness:** recorded conditions capture past pedigree, whereas Peircean warrant also looks forward to criticism, experiment, successor formulations, and indefinite inquiry.
5. **Hypothesis-testing articulation:** the boundary does not yet distinguish hypothesis generation from consequence derivation and testing.
6. **Science/vital-matters split:** the boundary does not note Peirce's late refusal to let scientific suspension govern every urgent practical decision.
7. **Historical semantic change:** it properly anticipates genealogy but does not yet exhibit changes such as hypothesis to abduction or belief to confidence/hope in science.
8. **Semiotic attribution:** language and records are named as possible losses, but signs are not yet treated as participants in cognition that can reciprocally alter persons and public meanings.

Memory and skill are not errors in the boundary; they are justified open questions. The Peircean corpus supplies habit as a nearby term but does not license the equivalence memory = habit = skill = retained knowledge.

### False equivalences to prevent

- `acceptance = belief`: acceptance may be architectural status; Peircean belief is an action-guiding habit, and scientific confidence may fall short of it.
- `acceptance = warrant or truth`: tenacity, authority, and a priori fashion can secure acceptance without truth.
- `provenance/review = Peircean inquiry`: these can support criticism but do not replace experiential testing, object-constraint, or future corrigibility.
- `community = consensus`: an actual community can enforce error; the indefinite community is not a majority vote.
- `record persistence = knowledge persistence`: a record may survive while interpretive capacity, collateral experience, practice, or warranted orientation disappears.
- `transmission = knowledge transfer`: a sign can be copied without the relevant interpretant or object-familiarity.
- `semiosis = knowledge formation`: sign-action is broader than warranted cognition.
- `habit = knowledge`: habits can be irrational, coercively stabilized, or merely practical.
- `settlement = truth`: the irritation of doubt can cease under epistemically defective methods.
- `formation = one process`: the corpus supplies heterogeneous changes and no universal cycle.

### Corrections and refinements to the boundary

1. Keep `knowledge formation` explicitly marked as a provisional family-name, not a process-kind.
2. Add object-side resistance/collateral experience alongside experiential, cognitive, social, and epistemic movements.
3. Type acceptance as an architectural status and explicitly deny that it alone entails belief, warrant, or truth.
4. Add future corrigibility to provenance: conditions should record not only why something was accepted but what could unsettle it and how successors remain linked.
5. Treat institutions as both epistemic supports and possible mechanisms of authoritative fixation.
6. Add confidence and hope as states that may sustain inquiry without settled belief.
7. Distinguish the preservation of a sign from preservation of its interpretive and experiential conditions.
8. Preserve the difference between generating a candidate, deriving its implications, and testing it, without forcing these into a universal temporal sequence.
9. Keep urgent practical orientation outside any claim that indefinite scientific inquiry is the sole route for all matters.

### Phase B verdict

The boundary is unusually well aligned with the negative constraints supplied by Peirce. It already blocks the most damaging synthesis errors: universal origin, isolated subject, definition-first exposition, and a smooth ladder of epistemic stages. Its remaining risk is subtler: `formation` and `movement` may still invite a single generative narrative, while the architectural phrase `understanding accepted under recorded conditions` may quietly collapse acceptance, belief, warrant, and truth.

A Peircean correction is not to replace the boundary with a doubt–inquiry–belief story. It is to retain a plural map of sign-mediated, habitual, experiential, inferential, social, and normative changes; require object-side resistance and future corrigibility; and treat every present attribution as revisable without making truth equivalent to revision or consensus.

The strongest Phase B confirmation remains the frozen Phase A claim: no bounded present subject or record is sufficient by itself for epistemic attribution. The strongest Phase B caution is that this does not authorize a literal group mind or a universal semiotic production cycle.

Inclusion: primary theory altering the bearer, production, warrant, transmission, or persistence of knowledge; ethnographic mechanisms; primary counterpositions defending truth or universal appraisal; authoritative reference works; stable publisher/journal/DOI/library records.

Exclusion: generic knowledge-management and DIKW pipelines; machine-learning uses except where directly relevant to testimony/distribution; cognitive-developmental and animal social-learning corpora; popular summaries; theology; most legal epistemology; and unsupported generalizations about “Asian,” “African,” or “Indigenous” epistemology. These are coverage limits, not irrelevance verdicts.

Stopping rationale: conceptual saturation was reached around the recurring axes individual/system, discovery/construction, truth/authorization, archive/repertoire, translation/equivocation, and understanding/ideology. Later searches added metadata rather than a new mechanism. This was a structured map, not a systematic review or corpus-closure claim.

### Established positions and owners

1. **Understanding as event.** Gadamer's *Verstehen* occurs within effective history. Prejudgments enable and constrain intelligibility; application belongs to understanding; a fusion of horizons changes interpreter and matter rather than depositing content in an unchanged subject. Hans-Georg Gadamer, *Truth and Method* (1960; revised English trans. Joel Weinsheimer and Donald G. Marshall, Bloomsbury/Continuum, 2004/2013), https://www.bloomsbury.com/us/truth-and-method-9781780936246/. Habermas's counterposition: tradition-mediated agreement can reproduce domination and systematic distortion; dialogical openness alone may not distinguish truth from ideology. SEP: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/win2022/entries/hermeneutics/.

2. **Social location.** Karl Mannheim, *Ideology and Utopia* (1929/1936), https://www.routledge.com/Ideology-and-Utopia/Mannheim/p/book/9781315002828, treats thought as socially and historically located, creating the reflexive problem that sociology of knowledge is located too. Sandra Harding, *Whose Science? Whose Knowledge?* (Cornell UP, 1991), ISBN 9780801425134, https://www.cornellpress.cornell.edu/book/9780801425134/whose-science-whose-knowledge/, makes marginalized standpoint a critical achievement, not an automatic possession of identity.

3. **Collective stabilization of facts.** Ludwik Fleck, *Genesis and Development of a Scientific Fact* (1935; Chicago UP ed. 1979/1981), ISBN 9780226253251, https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/G/bo25676016.html: facts develop in thought collectives/styles under historical conditioning and worldly resistance. Thomas Kuhn, *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions* (1962; 4th ed. Chicago UP, 2012), https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/S/bo13179781.html: normal science, anomaly, crisis, and paradigm reorganization replace monotonic accumulation. David Bloor, *Knowledge and Social Imagery* (1976; 2nd ed. Chicago UP, 1991), https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/K/bo3684600.html: explain accepted and rejected beliefs symmetrically and reflexively.

4. **Construction counterpositions.** Ian Hacking, *The Social Construction of What?* (Harvard UP, 1999), https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Social_Construction_of_What.html?id=C3ILEAAAQBAJ, separates constructions of classifications, people, ideas, objects, and facts. Paul Boghossian, *Fear of Knowledge* (OUP, 2006), DOI https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199287185.001.0001, rejects strong constructivism and relativism. Bruno Latour, “Why Has Critique Run out of Steam?”, *Critical Inquiry* 30.2 (2004): 225–248, DOI https://doi.org/10.1086/421123, warns that indiscriminate debunking can empower denial and redirects attention to matters of concern. “Socially made” does not decide truth, warrant, imposition, robustness, or fragility.

5. **Competent participation.** Jean Lave and Etienne Wenger, *Situated Learning* (Cambridge UP, 1991), DOI https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511815355: learning changes participation, identity, access, and skilled practice through legitimate peripheral participation. Exclusion from legitimate participation is a failure mechanism. The model does not cover every guarded, secret, complementary, or rupture-driven practice.

6. **Distributed bearer.** Edwin Hutchins, *Cognition in the Wild* (MIT Press, 1995/1996), ISBN 9780262581462, https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262581462/cognition-in-the-wild/: teams, artifacts, procedures, and environments can compose a cognitive system. Ronald Giere and Barton Moffatt, “Distributed Cognition,” *Social Studies of Science* 33.2 (2003): 301–310, DOI https://doi.org/10.1177/03063127030332017, dissolves a simple cognitive/social boundary. Individual, team, institution, and coupled system remain rival bearers.

7. **Dependence and criticism.** John Hardwig, “Epistemic Dependence,” *Journal of Philosophy* 82.7 (1985): 335–349, DOI https://doi.org/10.2307/2026523: advanced knowledge can depend on evidence no individual possesses. Alvin Goldman, *Knowledge in a Social World* (OUP, 1999), ISBN 9780191519284, https://books.google.com/books/about/Knowledge_in_a_Social_World.html?id=lQdREAAAQBAJ: assess social practices by truth-conducive outputs, not consensus alone. Helen Longino, *Science as Social Knowledge* (Princeton UP, 1990) and *The Fate of Knowledge* (2002): objectivity is achieved through organized criticism, uptake, shared standards, and tempered equality of authority. Sociality may transmit, generate, distribute, or constitute warrant; those theses differ.

8. **Epistemic cultures and cooperation without consensus.** Karin Knorr Cetina, *Epistemic Cultures* (Harvard UP, 1999), DOI https://doi.org/10.4159/9780674039681: high-energy physics and molecular biology use distinct object, instrument, empirical, and warranting arrangements. Susan Leigh Star and James Griesemer, “Institutional Ecology, ‘Translations’ and Boundary Objects,” *Social Studies of Science* 19.3 (1989): 387–420, DOI https://doi.org/10.1177/030631289019003001: heterogeneous communities coordinate through locally adaptable yet cross-site stable objects. Cooperation need not culminate in shared meaning or fusion.

9. **Classification, memory, archive, repertoire.** Geoffrey Bowker and Susan Leigh Star, *Sorting Things Out* (MIT Press, 1999), ISBN 9780262024617, https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262024617/sorting-things-out/: standards make entities visible/actionable while producing residues and exclusions. Geoffrey Bowker, *Memory Practices in the Sciences* (MIT Press, 2005/2006), ISBN 9780262025898, https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262025898/memory-practices-in-the-sciences/: recording infrastructures shape scientific memory and forgetting. Maurice Halbwachs, *On Collective Memory*, ed./trans. Lewis Coser (Chicago UP, 1992), ISBN 9780226115962, https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/O/bo3619875.html: groups reconstruct rather than merely retrieve the past. Diana Taylor, *The Archive and the Repertoire* (Duke UP, 2003), https://www.dukeupress.edu/the-archive-and-the-repertoire: gesture, movement, speech, and performance store and transmit differently from written archives.

10. **Attribution and failure.** Robert Merton, “The Matthew Effect in Science,” *Science* 159.3810 (1968): 56–63, DOI https://doi.org/10.1126/science.159.3810.56: prestige and cumulative advantage distort credit allocation. Miranda Fricker, *Epistemic Injustice* (OUP, 2007), DOI https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780198237907.001.0001: testimonial injustice produces credibility deficits; hermeneutical injustice reflects unequal participation in shared interpretive resources. Kristie Dotson, “Tracking Epistemic Violence,” *Hypatia* 26.2 (2011): 236–257, DOI https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1527-2001.2011.01177.x: pernicious ignorance can make hearers fail speakers' vulnerabilities. Shannon Sullivan and Nancy Tuana, eds., *Race and Epistemologies of Ignorance* (SUNY Press, 2007), ISBN 9780791471029, https://sunypress.edu/Books/R/Race-and-Epistemologies-of-Ignorance: ignorance can be socially produced and maintained.

### Comparative limits and translation-resistant concepts

Fredrik Barth, “An Anthropology of Knowledge,” *Current Anthropology* 43.1 (2002): 1–18, DOI https://doi.org/10.1086/324131, compares substantive corpus, media of representation, and social organization. Even this heuristic may over-unify knowing that cannot be detached from doing, being, land, or obligation.

Helen Verran, *Science and an African Logic* (Chicago UP, 2001), ISBN 9780226853895, https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/S/bo3631540.html, shows Yoruba numbering and certainty enacted through distinct social-material practices. Linda Tuhiwai Smith, *Decolonizing Methodologies*, 3rd ed. (Zed/Bloomsbury, 2021; orig. 1999), ISBN 9781350346086, https://www.bloomsbury.com/us/decolonizing-methodologies-9781350346086/, analyzes research as an imperial regime of extraction and authorization. Shawn Wilson, *Research Is Ceremony* (Fernwood, 2008), ISBN 9781552662816, https://cup.columbia.edu/book/research-is-ceremony/9781552662816/, joins ontology, epistemology, methodology, and axiology through relational accountability. Kim TallBear, *Native American DNA* (Minnesota UP, 2013), DOI https://doi.org/10.5749/minnesota/9780816665853.001.0001, shows that genetic evidence does not determine political belonging. Marisol de la Cadena, *Earth Beings* (Duke UP, 2015), DOI https://doi.org/10.1215/9780822375265, uses partial connections without reducing earth-beings to cultural belief about universal nature. Eduardo Viveiros de Castro, “Perspectival Anthropology and the Method of Controlled Equivocation,” *Tipití* 2.1 (2004), DOI https://doi.org/10.70845/2572-3626.1010, preserves equivocation rather than assuming shared referents.

Coverage does not license an “Indigenous worldview,” an Africa-wide claim, or a general “non-Western epistemology.” Asian, Islamic, South Asian, much Pacific and African scholarship remained outside the search.

Concepts resisting unification: *Wirkungsgeschichte* versus causal belief history; fusion versus consensus; thought style versus paradigm; participation versus information transfer; distributed cognition versus collective belief; epistemic culture versus worldview; boundary-object coordination versus semantic unity; archive versus repertoire; testimony versus group knowledge versus certification; relational accountability versus an added ethics constraint; controlled equivocation versus failed translation; hermeneutical injustice versus missing vocabulary; produced ignorance versus absent knowledge; political belonging versus genetic information.

### Principal disagreements

- Bearer: individual, group, institution, tradition, practice, or coupled system.
- Norm: truth, justification, reliability, competent performance, intelligibility, relational accountability, or authorization.
- Mechanism: interpretation, participation, testimony, criticism, inscription, classification, translation, or struggle.
- World relation: discovery, construction, resistance-conditioned articulation, enactment, or partial connection.
- Change: accumulation, apprenticeship, horizon transformation, paradigm rupture, migration, or refusal.
- Persistence: personal memory, collective reconstruction, archive, embodied repertoire, standards, or routine.
- Failure: falsity, unreliability, exclusion, silence, loss, mistranslation, incommensurability, or organized ignorance.
- Comparison: universal appraisal, local criteria, controlled equivocation, or ontological difference.

Martin Hollis and Steven Lukes, eds., *Rationality and Relativism* (MIT Press, 1982), ISBN 9780262580618, https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262580618/rationality-and-relativism/, demonstrates that the rationality debate yields multiple positions rather than “universalism versus anything goes.”

### Strongest expected cross-domain conflict

In many central cases, knowledge does not first form inside an individual and later become social. Historically inherited language, participation, testimony, artifacts, classifications, authority, and infrastructures partly constitute the epistemic capacity. This conflicts with individual-cognitive, computational, or formal models that treat those conditions as external inputs or implementation detail. It does not make truth, evidence, perception, or individual cognition dispensable; it makes them insufficient as a general account.

### Reversal evidence

The anti-unification position should be reversed if evidence establishes a single operational process that: predicts change across interpretive, scientific, embodied, Indigenous, institutional, and distributed cases; identifies one bearer criterion without ad hoc redefinition; distinguishes knowledge from error and organized ignorance without importing one tradition's validation regime; survives removal of social-material organization; preserves inferential, practical, ontological, and normative roles in translation; and displays an invariant longitudinal sequence.

Locally, Gadamer would be weakened if interpreters reliably suspended historically inherited prejudgments from an external methodical standpoint; distributed accounts if system performance reduced to individual states without explanatory loss; power accounts if credibility, classification, and access were causally irrelevant under strong controls.

### Rejected synthesis move

Rejected: `data → information → understanding → justified knowledge → collective memory`.

It assumes neutral data before interpretation; treats practice as content conversion; ignores instruments and thought styles; presumes an individual bearer; mistakes coordination for semantic convergence; collapses archive into repertoire; overlooks warranted contributions denied recognition; and can destroy relational, political, or ontological conditions by extracting detachable information.

The acceptable synthesis is a controlled comparison of differently constituted epistemic achievements, not a common sequence.

## Phase B — boundary comparison

Boundary read: `research/knowledge-formation/research-initial-definitions.md` only. No other internal project artifact was consulted. No supplemental external search was required because every comparison below is directly tested by sources already frozen in phase A.

### Confirmations

1. **No universal origin or question-first beginning.** This agrees with Gadamer's absence of a pre-interpretive start and with practice/distribution accounts in which inquiry need not begin as an explicit question.
2. **Narrative order is not ontological order.** This directly blocks the rejected pipeline.
3. **Non-equivalence of information, learning, understanding, belief, memory, skill, testimony, justification, and knowledge.** This matches the frozen map's central distinction.
4. **No isolated universal subject.** Individual, relational, practical, social, institutional, and distributed possibilities align with Hutchins, Hardwig, Lave/Wenger, and Knorr Cetina.
5. **Expository entry is not an origin claim.** This is a precise and necessary safeguard.
6. **Statement preservation may lose understanding conditions.** Bowker, Taylor, Halbwachs, and practice theory strongly confirm this.
7. **Internal vocabulary is not privileged over scholarship.** This is methodologically sound if evidence types remain distinguished.
8. **The operational baseline is explicitly demoted.** Calling “understanding accepted under recorded conditions” an architectural proposal rather than an account of formation correctly limits its authority.

### Imposed or potentially loading vocabulary

- **“Knowledge acquiring form.”** This metaphor suggests a prior formless substrate and a directed result. Some traditions concern changed participation, stabilized facts, disclosure, recognition, or maintained relations—not form acquisition.
- **“Movements.”** Useful phenomenologically, but it may smuggle temporal continuity across discontinuous paradigm rupture, attribution events, or infrastructural persistence.
- **“Phenomenological introduction.”** This assigns one tradition editorial priority. It must not become the classifier through which social epistemology, STS, or Indigenous accounts are redescribed.
- **“Conditions.”** In the baseline it gathers provenance, evidence, review, scope, version, and state. These differ as warrant, accountability, administrative state, and reuse metadata.
- **“Internal and external evidence.”** Equal non-privilege is right, but the two are not the same evidence type: local definitions evidence project use; scholarship supports descriptive, historical, empirical, or normative claims.

### Omissions

1. **Power and failure:** organized ignorance, testimonial silencing, hermeneutical marginalization, and epistemic violence.
2. **Recognition and credit:** who caused, contributed to, knows, is authorized, and receives credit can diverge.
3. **Classification and standards:** infrastructures do not only preserve; they constitute visibility and produce residual cases.
4. **Embodied repertoire:** the gaps mention embodiment and skill but not performance as a knowledge-preserving medium distinct from archive.
5. **Cooperation without consensus:** boundary objects show that shared work need not require common understanding.
6. **Comparative translation:** equivocation, partial connection, and ontological difference are absent. Without them, “same investigation” may impose a common object.
7. **Truth/realism countertradition:** causal genesis, social acceptance, justification, and truth must remain separate questions.
8. **Relational accountability and refusal:** knowledge practices may carry obligations and limits on extraction, not just alternate content.
9. **Worldly resistance:** social conditioning does not entail unconstrained construction.

### False equivalences and category risks

- **“Subject or unit”** conflates at least bearer, cognitive site, contributor, object of attribution, certifying authority, beneficiary, and accountable party.
- **“Permanence” options** mix persistence mechanisms (memory, repeatable skill, record) with social statuses (acceptance) and functional outcomes (reliable orientation).
- **“Understanding accepted under recorded conditions”** would be false as a general epistemic equivalence. Acceptance is neither necessary nor sufficient for knowledge; recorded provenance supports governance but does not guarantee truth or understanding.
- **“Transmission”** should not be one operation. Statement copying, testimonial warrant, skill apprenticeship, embodied reenactment, translation, and institutional reproduction have different identity and loss conditions.
- **“Formation” and “learning”** cannot serve as mutual proxies. A practice or institution may gain an epistemic capacity while no person undergoes the corresponding learning, and a person may learn without producing knowledge.

### Corrections recommended

1. Replace the singular research object with **epistemic achievements and transformations sometimes called knowledge**.
2. Require each account to state: achievement, bearer/site, contributors, attribution target, practice/media, warrant/authority regime, temporal-material support, and failure mode.
3. Keep **formation, learning, stabilization, recognition, attribution, authorization, transmission, preservation, revision, and loss** distinct, while allowing co-constitution.
4. State explicitly that agreement and acceptance can stabilize error or domination; neither is an epistemic success criterion by itself.
5. Add a comparison safeguard: similar terms do not guarantee shared referents; record equivocation and untranslatable residue.
6. Add archive/repertoire and boundary-object cases so persistence and coordination are not modeled only through statements.
7. Add a power question: who can speak, be heard, classify, preserve, refuse, authorize, and receive credit?
8. Type the architectural baseline as **governed-reuse eligibility**, not knowledge simpliciter.
9. If the essay opens phenomenologically, label understanding as one situated entrance and introduce its collapse-test inline: if other traditions can only appear after translation into changes of understanding, the opening has become an imposed universal frame.

### Boundary-level conclusion

The initial definitions are unusually careful and are largely confirmed. They already prevent the most damaging universal-sequence and isolated-subject errors. Their remaining weakness is not overt conflation but an under-typed umbrella: “formation,” “movement,” “subject or unit,” “conditions,” “transmission,” and “permanence” still gather mechanisms that the external corpus keeps separate. The correction is a comparison grammar, not a replacement master narrative.

Neither establishes a single architecture. | Perceived things show profiles against horizons; the lived body is a tacit “I can,” not normally an inspected object. | Ecological/direct perception and enactivism locate intelligibility in organism–environment coupling; predictive processing invokes hierarchical generative models. | Conflating sensitivity, conscious appearance, object recognition, and justified perceptual knowledge; treating direct and inferential vocabularies as trivially intertranslatable. |
| Attention | How is a theme selected and organized against context and margin? | Inattentional blindness is robust but heterogeneous; perceptual/cognitive load modulates misses. Attention’s necessity for consciousness remains disputed. | Attention reorganizes theme–field–margin rather than merely turning up a spotlight; background remains operative without being focal. | Attention can be epistemically enabling (selection, inquiry) and distorting (exclusion, capture). | Equating unattended with unprocessed, unconscious, unknown, or unavailable; individualizing what joint attention or institutions organize. |
| Embodied skill | How does competent responsiveness become flexible, reliable, teachable, or fragile? | Practice predicts performance only partially; expert variance is not exhausted by deliberate practice. Implicit-learning/pressure results support some automaticity claims, but reflective control can also aid experts. | Skilled action often feels absorbed, solicitational, and non-deliberative; breakdown can make body/tool conspicuous. | Rylean/anti-intellectualist ability views conflict with propositional intellectualism; Montero argues expert action can remain thoughtful. | “Tacit = nonconceptual = bodily = infallible”; skill performance does not by itself settle knowledge-how, attribution, or justification. |
| Affect | How do concern, salience, valence, and felt possibility shape what matters and what is retained? | Arousal and valence selectively enhance some memory details and impair others; affect shapes encoding, consolidation, retrieval, and distortion. | Moods and existential feelings disclose a field of possibilities globally; emotions are bodily orientations, not detachable labels. | Enactive affect is constitutive of sense-making; affective-scaffold accounts distribute regulation across artifacts/others. | “Emotion enhances memory” is too coarse; vividness/confidence are not accuracy, and neural modulation is not an epistemic warrant. |
| Memory | How do past achievements stabilize, transform, reactivate, and remain available? | Consolidation is real at cellular scales; systems-level handoff is disputed. Reactivation can induce lability under boundary conditions. Episodic detail can transform toward gist. | Remembering presents absence as past and often includes felt familiarity, ownership, and temporal perspective. | Constructive, extended, and distributed approaches reject archive metaphors; critics deny that external storage literally is memory. | Persistence is not immutability; retrieval is not simple replay; neural persistence, personal remembering, public record, and collective memory are different explananda. |
| Recognition | How does something/someone show up as “again,” familiar, identifiable, or socially visible? | Recollection/familiarity dissociations have substantial support, but dual-process, single-process, and global-matching models remain live. Interaction dynamics can improve detection of another agent. | Familiarity can occur without contextual recollection; social invisibility can coexist with literal visual detection. | Honnethian recognition concerns affirmative social acknowledgement; recognition-memory concerns prior occurrence. They are homonyms with possible interaction, not one mechanism. | A feeling of knowing can be false; social uptake can fail despite accurate cognition; pattern match is not acknowledgement. |
| Enaction | How do autonomous agents enact domains of significance through regulated engagement? | Dynamical and minimalist interaction experiments support coupling-sensitive cognition in limited tasks; they do not prove all cognition is interaction-constituted. | Subject and world appear co-defined in lived activity; agency is experienced through possibilities and resistance. | Autopoietic enactivism emphasizes autonomy/adaptivity; sensorimotor enactivism emphasizes mastery; radical enactivism rejects content for basic minds; participatory sense-making allows interaction-level organization. | Treating “enaction” as one thesis; scaling from basic sense-making to propositional knowledge; sliding from causal coupling to constitution. |
| Prior orientation | How do sedimented habits, horizons, expectations, and social prejudices make some possibilities salient before reflection? | Predictive/context effects, schema-guided memory, and attentional expectancy show history-sensitive processing. | Husserlian horizon/passive synthesis, Heideggerian fore-structure, and Merleau-Pontian sedimentation describe prethematic orientation differently. | Hermeneutics treats all explicit understanding as situated within prior interpretive possibilities; predictive processing offers a computationally different account. | Translating horizon into Bayesian prior erases normativity, history, and lived givenness; translating prior into prejudice erases neutral/necessary anticipation. |
| Conceptual change | How can a learner gain genuinely new expressive or inferential resources? | Educational/developmental data support restructuring but fit competing granularities: framework theories, knowledge-in-pieces, categorization shifts, bootstrapping. | Disorientation can precede reconfiguration of what is thinkable or salient, but no invariant lived sequence is established. | Posner-style accommodation, Carey’s Quinean bootstrapping, diSessa’s recoordination, and transformative learning disagree about the unit and depth of change. | Circularity: explaining a new concept by resources that already contain it; replacement narratives ignore coexistence/context-dependence; rational reflection accounts underplay embodiment and power. |
| Attribution and circulation | How does an achievement count as *someone’s* or *a group’s* knowledge and receive uptake? | Transactive/collective-memory and distributed-recall research show that groups allocate, cue, converge, and distort remembering. Qualitative health/incident studies document patterned credibility hierarchies. | Being heard, ignored, or rendered socially invisible changes the lived possibility of speaking as a knower. | Fricker: testimonial/hermeneutical injustice; Dotson/Medina: contributory, agential, and oppressive structures; distributed cognition: system-level accomplishment. | Occurrence, possession, credit, authority, uptake, and ownership can come apart. A true contribution may fail to become attributable knowledge. |

## 4. Process families: arise, change, persist, attribute, fail

This is a **plural map**, not a sequence.

| Question | Candidate process families | What would count as failure |
|---|---|---|
| Arise | perceptual attunement; sensorimotor mastery; affective salience; passive synthesis; testimony/joint attention; conceptual bootstrapping or recoordination | no discrimination; no grip on affordances; no uptake; circular “new” concept; false familiarity |
| Change | prediction-error learning; equilibration; habit reorganization; reconsolidation; reflective critique; interactional repair; conceptual replacement/restructuring | rigidity; catastrophic interference; context-bound change mistaken for general change; coercive reframing |
| Persist | bodily habit; neural/cellular stabilization; hippocampal–cortical reorganization; rehearsal; artifact and institutional scaffolding; social distribution | forgetting; decontextualized gist; scaffold loss; skill decay; institutional erasure |
| Become attributable | first-person avowal; successful performance; testimony and credibility uptake; authorship/credit norms; group role differentiation | testimonial injustice; appropriation; credit inflation; achievement without ability or ability without acknowledged authorship |
| Fail as knowledge | perceptual illusion; inattentional miss; affect-driven distortion; choking; false recognition; memory misinformation; conceptual incommensurability; hermeneutical gap | the failure types are non-equivalent and cannot be merged into one “formation error” variable |

## 6. Disagreements and countertraditions

1. **Representation vs. world-involving dynamics.** Predictive-processing models explain perception through hierarchical generative models; radical ecological/enactive approaches reject representations as unnecessary or distorting. Hybrid vocabulary does not resolve the ontological dispute.
2. **Direct perception vs. constructive inference.** Ecological psychology treats specifying information and affordances as directly perceived; constructivist traditions emphasize ambiguity, prior expectations, and inference.
3. **Skill as absorbed coping vs. thoughtful control.** Dreyfusian/Rylean readings stress non-deliberative expertise; Stanley–Williamson and Montero retain propositional or conscious guidance. Choking evidence supports only a conditional interference claim.
4. **Attention necessary for consciousness vs. dissociable.** Mole and colleagues argue attention is necessary but insufficient; Koch/Tsuchiya/Lamme-style lines report consciousness without attention. Paradigm definitions partly drive the dispute.
5. **Memory consolidation as transfer vs. transformation.** Standard consolidation predicts decreasing hippocampal dependence; multiple-trace/trace-transformation views retain hippocampal dependence for detailed episodic recollection and distinguish it from gist/semanticization.
6. **Recognition as one strength signal vs. separable familiarity/recollection.** Evidence supports dissociations, but formal identification of processes from judgments is contested.
7. **Conceptual change as framework replacement vs. piecemeal recoordination.** Posner/Carey traditions posit deep restructuring; diSessa emphasizes many smaller elements and process-level reorganization. Fodorian critics deny that bootstrapping explains new primitives.
8. **Extended cognition vs. embedded causation.** Clark–Chalmers/Barnier–Sutton allow constitutive extracranial resources; Adams–Aizawa/Rupert/Heersmink-style objections distinguish causal dependence from cognitive constitution and biological memory from external storage.
9. **Individual epistemic virtue vs. structural oppression.** Fricker’s hearer-focused corrective is criticized as too individual and too narrow; Dotson/Medina foreground persistent exclusion from shared resources and group agency.
10. **Interaction as support vs. constituent.** Mainstream social cognition lets interaction supply inputs to individual mechanisms; participatory sense-making claims interaction dynamics can sometimes constitute part of the cognition.

## 7. Concepts that resist translation

- **Leib / lived body** ≠ physiological body-state or body representation.
- **Body schema** ≠ body image; the former names prereflective sensorimotor organization, the latter a family of perceptual/attitudinal representations.
- **Affordance** ≠ subjective feature label, action association, or interface usability cue; Gibsonian affordances are relational opportunities in an ecological system.
- **Horizon** ≠ statistical prior. A horizon includes implicit possibilities of further givenness and context; Bayesian priors are formal probability assignments.
- **Sedimentation/habit** ≠ fixed motor routine. Phenomenological habit reorganizes a field of possible action.
- **Sense-making** ≠ semantic representation or mere stimulus response; in autonomous enactivism it is norm-governed organism–environment regulation.
- **Autonomy** ≠ independence or isolation; it concerns operationally closed self-constitution under ongoing dependence.
- **Familiarity** ≠ recollection, truth, or justified confidence.
- **Recognition (Anerkennung)** ≠ recognition-memory; social acknowledgement cannot be reduced to matching a stored representation.
- **Knowing-how** ≠ motor skill, procedural memory, ability, or propositional knowledge without further argument.
- **Hermeneutical injustice** ≠ any vocabulary gap; the gap must connect to structural marginalization and unfair disadvantage.
- **Collective memory** ≠ identical memories in many heads; it can name socially organized resources, convergence, narratives, or a group-level system, depending on the theory.
- **Conceptual change** ≠ belief revision or added facts; strong accounts require change in available inferential/expressive resources.

## 8. Coherence test for “knowledge formation”

Use the umbrella only if a proposed case supplies answers to all six questions without equivocating:

1. **Achievement:** What capacity, discrimination, true commitment, reliable performance, or shared understanding is claimed?
2. **Bearer:** Is the relevant bearer an organism, person, dyad, group, practice, institution, or coupled system?
3. **Mechanism/register:** Is the claim empirical, phenomenological, epistemological, or an explicitly licensed bridge between them?
4. **Norm:** What distinguishes successful formation from adaptation, conditioning, familiarity, confidence, consensus, or mere persistence?
5. **Diachrony:** What changes, what persists, and by which evidence can transformation be distinguished from replacement or retrieval?
6. **Attribution:** Who can avow, exercise, transmit, challenge, and receive credit for the achievement—and what social conditions enable uptake?

**Pass condition:** the case names a local phenomenon and its norm without assuming that all nine domains share a process.  
**Fail condition:** “knowledge formation” merely redescribes any state change that later supports successful behavior. Under that usage it is too indiscriminate to be explanatory.

## 9. Strongest expected cross-domain conflict

**Conflict:** enactive/phenomenological accounts treat knowing as world-involving, normatively situated, and sometimes interaction-constituted, while much empirical cognitive science operationalizes learning as changes in internal performance variables or neural states. The conflict is not “body vs. brain”; it concerns the **unit of analysis and the location of norms**. A neural account may explain discrimination changes without explaining why they count as knowledge, whose achievement they are, or why testimony receives uptake. Conversely, a phenomenological/social account may describe sense and authority without supplying a causal learning mechanism.

**Collapse-test:** if the allegedly world-involving or social contribution can be removed while a fully specified individual mechanism preserves the same discrimination, transfer, flexibility, phenomenology, and attribution profile, the constitutive (not merely causal) cross-domain claim collapses.

## 10. Evidence that would reverse this map’s position

The present position is pluralist and anti-reductionist. It would be reversed toward a unified formation theory by:

- a single independently specified model that predicts, before data fitting, learning and failure across perceptual attunement, embodied skill, episodic/semantic transformation, conceptual change, and social attribution;
- invariant temporal markers showing the same ordered process across those domains, including transfer and failure profiles;
- intervention evidence showing that manipulating the proposed common variable changes not only performance but also first-person availability, flexible reason-giving, social uptake, and group-level distribution;
- successful reduction of horizon, affective salience, affordance, skill, memory transformation, and credibility attribution to the same construct without loss of their native contrasts;
- cross-cultural and non-individual replication showing the model does not silently assume an isolated adult subject.

Evidence that would reverse the stronger world-involving interpretation toward internalism would be repeated demonstrations that bodily, environmental, artifact, and interactional structures never improve prediction beyond input variables to an autonomous intracranial mechanism, together with successful causal models reproducing the phenomenological and attribution profiles.

## 11. One rejected synthesis move

**Rejected:** “Knowledge forms when embodied prediction error, affectively weighted attention, memory reconsolidation, and social recognition converge into an enactive model update.”

**Why rejected:** it compresses incompatible explanatory registers; assumes a universal sequence; treats horizon as prior, affect as scalar weight, remembering as generic reconsolidation, social recognition as feedback, and enaction as predictive-model updating. It also has no criterion separating knowledge from adaptive but false or oppressive stabilization. This is a vocabulary merger, not a supported synthesis.

## 14. Phase-A verdict

- “Knowledge formation” is coherent only as a **typed family of local achievements plus explicit bridges**, not as one process.
- Strongest supported commonality: history-sensitive, selective, norm-laden change in organism–environment–social relations.
- Strongest unsupported leap: that this commonality identifies a single mechanism, bearer, sequence, or definition of knowledge.
- Highest-priority downstream gate: require every cross-domain synthesis to declare bearer, evidence register, success norm, persistence criterion, and attribution rule before comparing mechanisms.

**Dissent:** Embodied/enactive pluralism itself can become a permissive umbrella. Its anti-reductionist vocabulary does not automatically supply operational tests, causal mechanisms, or epistemic norms; predictive/internalist models may explain more of some tasks than enactivist rhetoric admits.

## 2. Confirmations from the native map

### 2.1 Confirmed constraints

1. **No explicit-question origin.** Perceptual attunement, passive synthesis, affective salience, bodily habit, recognition, and testimony can precede explicit inquiry. The prohibition is supported.
2. **Expository entry is not ontological origin.** Phenomenology can begin with a salient appearance while acknowledging tacit horizons, sedimented capacities, and social histories that make it possible.
3. **No universal sequence.** Phase A found multiple non-equivalent families: attunement, mastery, consolidation, reconsolidation, conceptual reorganization, testimonial uptake, and collective distribution.
4. **No term pipeline.** Information → learning → understanding → belief → justification → knowledge is not supported as a general order. The initial definition correctly refuses it.
5. **No isolated knower by default.** Participatory sense-making, socially distributed memory, testimony, epistemic dependence, and credibility economies all supply established alternatives.
6. **Permanence is heterogeneous.** Neural stabilization, retained episodic detail, semantic gist, repeatable ability, artifact persistence, public accessibility, and institutional capacity can vary independently.
7. **Statement preservation is insufficiently diagnostic.** A sentence can remain while sensorimotor mastery, contextual understanding, reasons, provenance, or the community able to interpret it disappear.
8. **Operational vocabulary is not automatically explanatory.** The baseline correctly demotes its architectural definition to a proposal for governed reuse rather than a theory of how knowing arises.
9. **“Formation” itself is revisable.** The provisional research question already permits rejecting its own main noun; this is consistent with the Phase-A coherence test.

### 2.2 Confirmed open questions

| Initial open question | Native-map confirmation |
|---|---|
| Where may exposition begin? | No native domain supplies a neutral universal start. Perception, affect, breakdown, testimony, and remembered absence are all defensible local entries. |
| What counts as learning or contributing to knowledge? | Skill, memory, conceptual change, and testimony use different success measures; behavioral change alone is insufficient. |
| Must knowledge be a person-owned state? | Distributed cognition and group epistemology keep this open; participation, storage vehicle, bearer, and attribution can come apart. |
| What does persistence mean? | Phase A distinguished habit, stabilization, reorganization, rehearsal, artifact support, and social distribution. |
| Does transmission preserve understanding? | Social epistemology contains an active dispute; testimony may generate or enable understanding without passing an invariant object intact. |
| Can a statement survive while knowledge is lost? | Yes as a live possibility, but “knowledge loss” must be typed by bearer and capacity rather than inferred from statement survival alone. |

## 3. Vocabulary imposed by the initial definitions

The file is cautious, but its nouns and verbs still orient the field.

| Imposed term | What it makes visible | What it silently risks |
|---|---|---|
| **formation / acquiring form** | emergence and change rather than static possession | presupposes a bounded item that progressively takes shape; suggests one cross-domain process |
| **movement** | non-stasis, transition, and possible plurality | blurs empirical process, lived transition, social reclassification, and epistemic status change |
| **phenomena later called knowledge** | attribution can occur after an achievement | “called” can reduce knowledge to labeling or defer the success norm indefinitely |
| **discernible in experience, practice, and social life** | preserves lived, practical, and relational dimensions | “discernible” may privilege later reflection over prereflective availability and nonconceptual skill |
| **information / evidence / acceptance / provenance / knowledge** | useful architectural distinctions | visually and conceptually invites a staged pipeline even while the prose denies one |
| **understanding accepted under recorded conditions** | makes scope and accountability explicit | acceptance plus records can be mistaken for truth, reliability, ability, grasp, or knowledge simpliciter |
| **subject or unit of knowledge** | opens individual and collective candidates | conflates bearer, vehicle, participant, locus of evaluation, and unit of attribution |
| **state possessed** | exposes the internalist default for criticism | still frames the alternative against possession rather than beginning from activity or relation |
| **permanence** | raises retention and loss | suggests all relevant persistence is one magnitude; ignores accessibility, exercise, revisability, and identity through transformation |
| **transmission** | foregrounds testimony and preservation | suggests a conserved object moving from source to recipient; understates hearer contribution, repair, and transformation |
| **statement** | identifies a persistable public artifact | privileges propositional products over skills, orientations, recognitional capacities, and shared practices |
| **conditions** | supports qualified, scoped acceptance | can mix evidential conditions, enabling causes, social permissions, constitutive relations, and governance metadata |

## 4. Omissions relative to the native problem map

### 4.1 Phenomena omitted or only implicit

| Missing item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Perception–action coupling** | A world can become available through active exploration before information is explicitly received or a belief is formed. |
| **Attention as theme–field–margin organization** | What becomes available depends on foreground/background organization; attention is not merely a later filter. |
| **Affordances and solicitations** | Practical intelligibility may be relational and action-scaled, not an internal representation of neutral input. |
| **Recognition/familiarity** | “This again” can precede recollection and can mislead; social recognition is distinct from recognition memory. |
| **Autonomy, adaptivity, and sense-making** | Enactivist accounts locate minimal normativity in self-maintaining regulation; naming embodiment without these leaves it as a modifier of internal cognition. |
| **Interaction-level constitution** | Dyadic dynamics may sometimes perform cognitive work, not merely deliver social input to individuals. |
| **Horizon, sedimentation, and prior orientation** | The framing mentions prior knowledge but not the broader background of habits, expectations, and interpretive possibilities that precede explicit knowledge. |
| **Breakdown and repair** | Skill, testimony, and understanding often become explicit when smooth coping fails; failure can reorganize the field rather than merely stop formation. |
| **Conceptual change mechanisms** | “Making new distinctions available” needs competing accounts: replacement, bootstrapping, recoordination, and context-sensitive coexistence. |
| **Epistemic affect** | Curiosity, doubt, surprise, wonder, anxiety, and felt certainty can orient inquiry without guaranteeing truth. |

### 4.2 Normative and social omissions

- **Truth, reliability, aptness, grasp, and answerability** are not offered even as competing norms. Without a norm, adaptive or socially accepted error can count as formation.
- **Credibility and uptake** are absent. Testimony can be accurate yet fail to become attributable knowledge because a hearer discounts the speaker.
- **Epistemic injustice and oppression** are absent. Hermeneutical resources and testimonial standing are unevenly distributed.
- **Appropriation and credit** are absent. A contribution may circulate while its originator loses authority or attribution.
- **Ignorance, refusal, and willful non-uptake** are absent. Not all failure is missing information or failed learning.
- **Group acceptance vs. group belief/knowledge** is not separated. A formally accepted proposition can be action-guiding without being believed by members or qualifying as group knowledge.
- **Division of epistemic labor** is implicit but unnamed. Some knowledge acquisition depends on others in ways no individual can fully reconstruct.

### 4.3 Diachronic omissions

- Stabilization vs. **transformation**: persistence can preserve gist while losing episodic specificity.
- Retention vs. **access**: stored material may be unavailable or cued only in restricted contexts.
- Possession vs. **exercise**: retained know-how can outlast current ability, and ability can occur without articulate reasons.
- Record survival vs. **interpretability**: symbols require practices, languages, and trust relations.
- Forgetting vs. **unlearning**: loss may be accidental, inhibitory, adaptive, imposed, or intentionally cultivated.
- Individual forgetting vs. **socially shared retrieval-induced forgetting**: selective conversation can align what multiple people omit.
- Revisability vs. **instability**: openness to correction is not the same as absence of durable orientation.

### 4.4 Coverage risks not resolved by the file

- Cross-cultural and decolonial epistemologies are not named; a general external corpus could otherwise reproduce an Anglophone cognitive-science/European-phenomenology center.
- Nonhuman cognition and minimal cognition remain outside the stated bearer list despite their importance to life–mind continuity disputes.
- Neurodivergent, disabled, and clinical phenomenologies are not named; these can test whether tacit norms of perception, communication, and credibility have been universalized.
- The relation between first-person description and third-person measurement is not specified.

## 5. False equivalences and category slides to block

| Candidate slide | Why false or unearned | Required correction |
|---|---|---|
| accepted understanding = knowledge | Acceptance can be pragmatic, coerced, mistaken, or merely procedural; records do not add truth or grasp by themselves. | Name it **governed acceptance for reuse** unless an independent epistemic norm is supplied. |
| formation = any relevant change | Conditioning, increased confidence, social conformity, and oppressive stabilization are changes without thereby being knowledge. | Require a typed achievement and failure norm. |
| information received = testimony learned | Developmental evidence shows selective trust and inferential work by hearers; reception is active and socially conditioned. | Separate signal availability, communicative uptake, belief change, and knowledge. |
| transmission = conservation | Understanding may be generated with hearer contribution; speaker knowledge may be neither a simple necessary nor sufficient condition in all theories. | Decompose source state, communicative act, hearer uptake, transformation, and resulting status. |
| memory = persistence | Memory may transform, reconsolidate, distort, or remain inaccessible; skills and public records persist differently. | Type the carrier, retained organization, access conditions, and fidelity criterion. |
| social acceptance = collective knowledge | Group-belief literature disputes whether non-summative group stances are belief or acceptance and what further conditions knowledge requires. | Keep group acceptance, group belief, distributed competence, and collective knowledge separate. |
| distributed subject = external storage | A notebook, record, group, or institution may causally scaffold cognition without constituting a knower. | State the constitution criterion and answer the causal–constitution objection. |

| embodiment = brain plus bodily input | Enactive embodiment concerns autonomous, affective, sensorimotor, and social organization. | Specify which dimension of embodiment does explanatory work. |

## 6. Corrections proposed

### 6.1 Replace the load-bearing question

**Current form:** asks what movements participate in knowledge acquiring form.

**Corrected form:**

> Which locally distinguishable changes in perception, attention, embodied skill, affect, memory, recognition, conceptual resources, testimony, and social coordination can support epistemic achievements; for each change, what is the bearer, evidence register, success norm, mode of persistence, and rule of attribution—and which proposed bridges among them fail?

Why this is narrower and stronger:

- “locally distinguishable” blocks a universal formation process;
- “support” does not prejudge causal vs. constitutive status;
- “epistemic achievements” requires an explicit norm without prematurely defining all knowledge;
- the five typing questions implement the Phase-A coherence test;
- “which bridges fail” makes counterexamples first-class results.

### 6.2 Add a mandatory typing grid

Every candidate phenomenon should record:

| Field | Required question |
|---|---|
| **Bearer** | organism, person, dyad, group, practice, institution, coupled system, or unresolved? |
| **Register** | empirical finding, phenomenological description, epistemological interpretation, or licensed bridge? |
| **Achievement** | discrimination, reliable orientation, ability, true commitment, understanding, justified belief, shared competence, or other? |
| **Norm** | what makes success epistemic rather than merely adaptive, accepted, familiar, or persistent? |
| **Role** | enabling cause, constitutive component, evidence, storage vehicle, cue, scaffold, social uptake, or attribution? |
| **Diachrony** | acquisition, stabilization, transformation, access, exercise, revision, loss, or reattribution? |
| **Failure** | illusion, miss, distortion, rigidity, choking, false recognition, conceptual circularity, exclusion, appropriation, or other? |

### 6.3 Qualify the architectural baseline

- Retain the existing operational formula only under a local label such as **governed reusable acceptance**.
- Do not treat provenance, evidence, review, scope, version, and state as jointly sufficient for knowledge.
- Ask separately whether the item is true/reliable, understood, usable, revisable, and attributable.
- Permit the architecture to preserve conditions of acceptance without settling the epistemology.

### 6.4 Decompose “transmission”

Use at least five fields:

1. source competence/status;
2. public expression or artifact;
3. channel and access conditions;
4. recipient uptake, reconstruction, and defeaters;
5. resulting ability/belief/understanding and attribution.

This prevents the preserved statement from standing in for the entire epistemic relation.

### 6.5 Decompose “permanence”

Track separately:

- trace or inscription survival;
- retrievability/accessibility;
- repeatable performance;
- contextual fidelity;
- interpretability by a community;
- institutional capacity to act;
- revisability without identity loss;
- continued attribution and authority.

### 6.6 Add explicit negative cases

The research should test at least:

- fluent but false skill-like performance;
- vivid false recognition;
- accurate testimony denied uptake;
- accepted consensus produced by conformity;
- preserved statement with lost interpretive practice;
- distributed success with disputed bearer;
- conceptual change that is only vocabulary substitution;
- durable oppressive orientation;
- collective remembering that coordinates forgetting.

| Tempting synthesis | Collapse test |
|---|---|
| all relevant changes are “formation” | If the same term cannot distinguish true flexible achievement from durable adaptive error, it contributes no explanation. |
| governed acceptance constitutes knowledge | If a fully documented, reviewed, scoped, accepted claim can still be false or misunderstood, the sufficiency claim collapses. |

| institutions know through records | If records persist but no member/system can interpret or appropriately act from them, record-based knowing collapses. |
| social interaction is constitutive | If removing real-time coupling while preserving inputs leaves the same discrimination, flexibility, experience, and attribution profile, constitution collapses to causal support. |

- **Decision:** qualified confirmation with required vocabulary corrections.
- **Retain:** anti-universalism; non-equivalence constraints; plural bearers; expository/ontological-order distinction; open permanence and testimony questions; demotion of the architectural definition.
- **Revise:** the load-bearing “formation/movement/acquiring form” wording; the operational use of “knowledge”; the undifferentiated terms “transmission,” “permanence,” “subject/unit,” and “conditions.”
- **Add:** explicit evidence registers; local epistemic norms; perception, attention, recognition, enaction, prior orientation, conceptual change; credibility/power/appropriation; negative cases; typed persistence and loss.

## 1. Scope and controlling distinctions

### Question decomposed

Phenomena called *knowing*, *knowledge*, *learning*, *development*, *competence*, *expertise*, or *participation* were mapped under five non-equivalent questions:

1. **Arising:** What was absent before, and what counts as its first appearance?
2. **Changing:** Does change mean enrichment, reorganization, replacement, redescription, strategy redistribution, proceduralization, or changed participation?
3. **Persisting:** What remains stable—representation, disposition, performance, routine, identity, public artifact, or system organization?
4. **Attribution:** Who or what is licensed to count as knowing—an individual, a dyad, a community, or a person–artifact system?
5. **Failing:** Is failure absence, inaccessible competence, contextual non-transfer, interference, misconception, loss of participation, or an attribution error by the observer?

### Initial result

“Knowledge formation” is not yet a coherent natural-kind label. It is usable as a **typed comparison problem** if every claim specifies:

`content/competence × bearer × attribution criterion × timescale × mechanism × setting × persistence/failure mode × normative standard × evidence`

Without those fields, apparent agreement is frequently equivocation.

Saturation was declared after five independent chains (genetic epistemology; contemporary developmental mechanisms; conceptual change; skill/transfer; sociocultural/situated learning) and four disconfirming searches produced no new **type** of answer to the controlling distinctions. - the strength of Piaget’s universality claim;
- cultural and methodological qualifications;
- degrees of guidance in instruction;

## 4. Established positions and ownership

1. **Action-centered constructivism is owned by Piaget, not by generic “active learning.”** Piaget explicitly treats knowledge as organized activity and rejects both passive copying and fully preformed structures [S1–S4].
2. **A global staircase is not the only Piaget-compatible descendant.** Fischer retypes stages as domain- and support-sensitive skill hierarchies; Siegler replaces single-state descriptions with distributions of strategies [S7–S9].
3. **Early competence evidence is criterion-sensitive.** Baillargeon’s looking-time results attribute hidden-object representation earlier than Piagetian search tasks, while Munakata models the search failure through competing graded traces [S5–S6]. The inference “looked longer, therefore possesses the full adult concept” remains stronger than the data alone.
4. **Domain-specific starting systems are owned by core-knowledge accounts.** Spelke argues for early, task-limited systems; Carey adds representational discontinuity and bootstrapping [S11–S12].
5. **Emergence without preset stages is owned by dynamic-systems, connectionist, and neuroconstructivist accounts.** Stable behavior may be an attractor generated by coupled constraints [S10, S14–S15].
6. **Conceptual change is a family of incompatible mechanisms.** Posner et al. own accommodation conditions; Vosniadou owns framework-theory restructuring; Chi and colleagues own ontological-category shift; diSessa owns knowledge-in-pieces; Smith et al. own the constructive critique of “misconceptions” as simply defective beliefs [S25–S29].
7. **Persistence through coexistence has direct evidence.** Shtulman and Valcarcel found slower and less accurate adult verification where scientific answers conflict with naïve theories across ten domains, supporting suppression/coexistence rather than erasure [S31].
8. **Proceduralization is not identical to understanding.** Anderson owns knowledge compilation; Chi, Feltovich, and Glaser show domain organization around deep principles; Ericsson et al. emphasize deliberate practice; Macnamara et al. delimit its explanatory reach [S32–S35].
9. **Situated and participation accounts change the explanandum.** Lave and Wenger do not merely add social input to an individual acquisition model; they treat learning as changing participation and identity [S19]. Sfard makes the acquisition/participation distinction explicit and rejects choosing only one metaphor [S23].
10. **Distributed cognition changes the bearer.** Hutchins attributes navigation-relevant cognition to a coordinated person–artifact system, not merely to parallel individual minds [S21].
11. **Community knowledge is not the sum or mean of member beliefs.** Scardamalia and Bereiter treat public idea improvement as a communal epistemic object [S43].
12. **Epistemic cognition adds normativity.** Hofer and Pintrich map beliefs about knowledge and knowing; Chinn and colleagues require epistemic aims, values, sources, justification, virtues/vices, and reliable processes [S44–S45]. A causal learning mechanism alone cannot determine when a belief is justified.

## 5. The universal-stage question

### What must be separated

| Claim often bundled as “Piagetian stages” | Phase-A verdict |
|---|---|
| Children actively construct organizations rather than passively copy inputs | Retained; widely inherited, though reformulated [S1–S4, S25, S28] |
| Some developmental achievements show ordered dependencies | Retained as a local possibility; cross-cultural reviews report meaningful qualitative regularities [S8, S38] |
| One age-indexed, domain-general stage describes a child across tasks | Not sustained; variability across domain, support, strategy, and task is central in alternatives [S7–S10] |
| Failure on a canonical task proves absence of the underlying competence | Rejected; looking-time, graded-memory, language, context, and motor demands change attribution [S5–S6, S36] |
| The same tasks have equivalent meaning across cultures | Rejected as a default; must be established locally [S37–S39] |
| Formal operations are an inevitable universal endpoint | Not sustained in a strong age-fixed form; schooling, cultural valuation, and task ecology matter [S37–S38] |
| Stage-like discontinuity requires a stored stage program | Rejected; dynamic systems can generate discontinuous behavior from continuous coupled change [S10, S14] |

### Attack on the universal narrative, with retained counterevidence

- **Task/criterion attack:** Baillargeon and DeVos found looking-time evidence of hidden-object expectations in 3.5-month-olds, earlier than search-based object-permanence attribution [S5]. Munakata shows how search errors can coexist with weaker representations [S6]. These results undermine a single behavioral criterion; they do not by themselves establish a fully adult object concept.
- **Within-person variability attack:** Siegler’s overlapping-waves account treats old and new strategies as co-present, with development expressed as changing distributions rather than abrupt replacement [S9].
- **Domain/support attack:** Fischer predicts different demonstrated levels depending on domain and environmental support [S7]. A person cannot be assigned one context-free cognitive altitude without loss.
- **Mechanism attack:** Thelen and Smith show that nonlinear, stage-like transitions can emerge from self-organizing multicomponent dynamics [S10]. Discontinuity in performance does not identify a stage mechanism.
- **Cultural/task-meaning attack:** Brazilian street vendors solved arithmetically equivalent problems better in commercial contexts than in school or context-free formats [S37]. The inference from school-task failure to lack of mathematical knowledge is invalid.
- **Sampling attack:** Henrich, Heine, and Norenzayan show that WEIRD samples are often outliers across cognitive and social domains [S41]. Broesch and colleagues require culturally grounded, community-engaged developmental design rather than export of a supposedly neutral task [S42].
- **Counterevidence retained:** Ashton’s and Dasen’s reviews report some cross-cultural qualitative sequences even with major differences in timing, attainment, and culturally valued domains [S37–S38]. Therefore the defensible conclusion is not “all sequence is fiction”; it is “ordered local dependencies do not license a global universal staircase.”
- **Textual correction:** Piaget’s 1956 formulation explicitly says *formation des connaissances* in the plural, denies an absolute beginning, and distinguishes heredity/maturation, physical experience, social transmission/interaction, and equilibration [S1]. The common four-stage classroom synopsis is narrower than the primary program.

## 6. Major disagreements and countertraditions

### A. Structure versus variability

- **Structuralist claim:** later organizations integrate earlier ones and acquire necessity through equilibration [S1–S4].
- **Counterposition:** performance is a distribution of strategies or context-supported skills [S7–S9].
- **Unresolved:** whether local ordered dependencies aggregate into structures with explanatory power beyond task families.

### B. Constructed knowledge versus early core systems

- **Constructivist claim:** no fixed cognitive structures are present at the start; action organizes experience [S1–S4].
- **Core-knowledge claim:** object, number, agency, and spatial systems emerge early with domain/task limits [S11].
- **Intermediate alternatives:** Karmiloff-Smith/Elman-style emergent specialization and neuroconstructivism reject both a blank slate and richly prespecified adult modules [S14–S15]. Carey accepts core systems but requires bootstrapped discontinuities for later concepts [S12].
- **Unresolved:** which infant responses require conceptual content versus perceptual/graded predictive organization.

### C. Coherent theories versus knowledge in pieces

- **Framework-theory claim:** naïve knowledge is organized by entrenched ontological and epistemological presuppositions [S26].
- **Ontological-shift claim:** difficulty is especially high when a scientific concept requires reassignment from matter-like to process-like categories [S27].
- **Knowledge-in-pieces claim:** intuitive knowledge is composed of many small, context-sensitive resources whose reorganization can support expertise [S28–S29].
- **Persistence finding:** old and scientific responses can coexist for years [S31].
- **Unresolved:** coherence may vary by person, domain, task, and measurement grain; one universal storage architecture is not warranted.

### D. Acquisition versus participation

- **Acquisition family:** knowledge is a representation, concept, procedure, or justified belief that an individual comes to possess [S12–S13, S25–S35, S41–S42].
- **Participation family:** learning is changing membership, identity, responsibility, and access within practice [S17–S20, S23].
- **Distributed extension:** some accomplishments belong to a coordinated system [S21].
- **Sfard’s warning:** either metaphor alone hides phenomena; acquisition handles individual continuity and transfer questions, while participation handles identity, practice, and constitutive social relations [S23].
- **Unresolved:** a principled rule for moving attribution between person, group, and socio-technical system.

### E. Situated specificity versus abstraction and transfer

- **Situativity claim:** knowledge is partly constituted by activity, context, tools, and culture; schooling can produce inert knowledge [S20, S22].
- **Counterposition:** Anderson, Reder, and Simon argue that strong claims against transfer, abstraction, and direct instruction are overstated [S24].
- **Taxonomic correction:** Barnett and Ceci show that “far transfer” varies across multiple content and context dimensions; transfer is not one binary outcome [S36].
- **Unresolved:** which representational and practice conditions yield robust transfer without erasing context.

### F. Discovery versus guidance

- **Strong unguided-discovery position:** learners should construct without substantial guidance.
- **Counterevidence:** Alfieri et al.’s meta-analysis favors explicit instruction over unassisted discovery, while enhanced/assisted discovery outperforms comparison conditions [S46].
- **Scaffolding counterposition:** problem- and inquiry-based learning need not be minimally guided; scaffolds can reduce load while preserving epistemic practices [S47].
- **Active-learning evidence:** Freeman et al. find average gains over lecture-dominant instruction in undergraduate STEM [S48].
- **Verdict:** “constructivism implies minimal guidance” is a category error. Epistemic constructivism does not select a single pedagogy.

### G. Practice sufficiency versus multicausal expertise

- **Deliberate-practice claim:** prolonged, effortful, feedback-rich practice is a central cause of expert performance [S33].
- **Limit:** Macnamara et al. find substantial but domain-variable explained variance, very low in professions and education; the 2018 corrigendum does not restore sufficiency [S34].
- **Verdict:** practice is an enabling mechanism under constraints, not a universal origin or sufficient account of expertise.

## 7. How knowing arises, changes, persists, and fails — typed synthesis

### Arising

| Type of “newness” | Mechanism candidates | What would count as evidence |
|---|---|---|
| First task success | maturation, improved motor control, cue use, strategy discovery, stronger active trace | Cross-task convergence and process measures, not one score [S5–S9] |
| New organization of prior activity | assimilation/accommodation/equilibration; skill construction | Novel coordination and necessity across related tasks [S1–S4, S7] |
| New representational power | bootstrapping; representational redescription; ontological change | Ability to express/infer relations unavailable in prior system [S12, S15, S27] |
| New causal model | intervention-sensitive structure learning | Generalized intervention/counterfactual predictions [S13] |
| New fluent procedure | compilation, proceduralization, schema induction, practice | Speed/accuracy gains plus retention and selective application [S32–S36, S49] |
| New participation status | guided participation, legitimate peripherality, identity change | Increasing responsibility, access, and competent contribution [S17–S19] |
| New public knowledge | progressive discourse and improvable conceptual artifact | Community-visible idea improvement beyond individual recall [S43] |

### Changing

No single verb is adequate:

- **Accretion:** adding propositions or cases.
- **Enrichment:** increasing detail while retaining the same framework.
- **Reorganization:** changing relations among existing elements.
- **Replacement:** adopting an incompatible conception; empirically less clean than classic descriptions imply.
- **Suppression/prevalence shift:** old intuition persists but loses control in some contexts [S31].
- **Redescription:** making implicit procedural information more explicit and flexible [S15].
- **Proceduralization:** embedding declarative information into productions [S32].
- **Strategy redistribution:** changing frequency and adaptiveness of coexisting strategies [S9].
- **Participation/identity transformation:** changing relations to a practice [S19, S23].
- **System reconfiguration:** changing coordination and information flow [S21].

### Persisting

Persistence may reside in:

- scheme or operation [S1–S4];
- graded memory traces/attractor dynamics [S6, S10, S14];
- core systems [S11–S12];
- naïve theories or resources that remain suppressible [S28–S31];
- compiled procedures and domain schemas [S32–S36, S49];
- recurrent social practices, identities, and access relations [S17–S23];
- artifacts, representational media, and information-flow routines [S21, S43];
- epistemic standards and justificatory habits [S44–S45].

These are not interchangeable stores.

### Becoming attributable

Attribution requires an explicit rule:

| Rule | Licensed statement | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| Correct answer | “The person knows X” | Guessing, cueing, and brittle performance |
| Looking-time violation | “The infant expects X” | Inflating expectation into adult-like concept [S5–S6] |
| Explanation/justification | “The learner understands or knows why X” | Verbal skill and schooling bias |
| Transfer | “The learner can use X beyond the trained setting” | “Distance” underspecified [S36] |
| Reliable skilled action | “The performer knows how” | Tacit competence may not support verbal report or far transfer [S32–S36, S49] |
| Responsible participation | “The newcomer is becoming knowledgeable in practice” | Conformity can be mistaken for epistemic warrant [S19, S23] |
| System success | “The team/artifact system knows how to navigate” | Obscuring individual accountability and local ignorance [S21] |
| Public idea improvement | “The community has advanced knowledge” | Public artifact may not be understood by every member [S43] |
| Justified reliable process | “The belief/claim is epistemically warranted” | Norms can vary by domain and context [S45] |

### Failing

1. **True absence:** the needed representation, skill, or relation has not formed.
2. **Performance blockage:** competence is masked by language, memory, motor, attentional, or social demands [S5–S7].
3. **Miscuing:** a context activates an unproductive resource or old intuition [S28–S30].
4. **Synthetic model:** learner reconciles incompatible information without revising a framework [S26].
5. **Ontological misclassification:** process is treated as substance [S27].
6. **Non-transfer:** organization remains tied to surface features or training context [S20, S24, S36–S37].
7. **Practice exclusion:** learner lacks legitimate access, responsibility, or guidance [S17–S19].
8. **Coordination breakdown:** system fails despite locally competent individuals [S21].
9. **Normative failure:** belief is held but poorly justified or produced unreliably [S45].
10. **Observer attribution error:** assessment imports culture-specific expectations and mislabels competent activity as deficit [S37–S42].

## 8. Concepts that resist translation

| Native concept | Tempting translation | Why the translation fails |
|---|---|---|
| Piagetian **scheme** (*schème*) | production rule or stored schema | A scheme is an organized repeatable action structure with assimilation/accommodation, not merely a declarative template or compiled rule |
| **Equilibration** | error minimization / Bayesian updating / homeostasis | It includes compensatory reorganization and the genesis of structures that may become necessary; no agreed scalar objective function is specified [S3–S4] |
| **Stage** | age band | Piagetian stage denotes structural organization/order; age is an empirical index. Later accounts retype stages as local skill levels or emergent transitions [S7–S10] |
| **Core knowledge** | prior knowledge | Core systems are early, domain- and task-specific representational systems, not any belief held before instruction [S11–S12] |
| **Conceptual change** | learning | It may mean accommodation, framework revision, ontological reassignment, resource reorganization, or prevalence shift [S25–S30] |
| **Knowledge in pieces** | fragmented misinformation | Pieces can be productive intuitive resources from which expert understanding is reorganized [S28–S29] |
| **Proceduralization** | understanding | Faster rule-governed execution may occur without flexible explanation or broad transfer [S32, S35] |
| **Legitimate peripheral participation** | beginner stage | It is a relational condition of access and membership, not a competence rung inside an individual [S19] |
| **Guided participation** | direct instruction | Guidance may be embedded in shared activity, observation, responsibility transfer, and nonverbal coordination [S17–S18] |
| **Distributed cognition** | group knowledge as summed beliefs | The system’s organization and artifact-mediated transformations can realize an accomplishment no member individually realizes [S21] |
| **Knowledge building** | collaborative learning | Its target is improvement of public conceptual artifacts, not merely each participant’s learning gain [S43] |
| **Epistemic cognition** | metacognition about confidence | It includes aims, values, standards, sources, justification, virtues/vices, and reliability [S45] |

## 9. Coherence test for any use of “knowledge formation”

### Required tuple

For claim `X forms`, supply:

1. **X — content:** proposition, concept, operation, strategy, procedure, practice, identity, artifact, or norm.
2. **B — bearer:** individual, dyad, community, or socio-technical system.
3. **A — attribution criterion:** answer, action, anticipation, explanation, transfer, participation, public improvement, or justification.
4. **T — timescale:** trial, lesson, microgenetic series, ontogenesis, career, community generation, or historical change.
5. **M — mechanism:** named and discriminated from rivals.
6. **C — context:** task, tools, language, culture, instruction, and support.
7. **P — persistence/failure:** retention, coexistence, suppression, context dependence, exclusion, or coordination breakdown.
8. **N — norm:** correctness, usefulness, fluency, communal legitimacy, or epistemic justification.
9. **E — evidence:** observation that could distinguish formation from temporary performance.

### Pass conditions

A cross-domain synthesis passes only if it:

- keeps B, A, T, and N explicit;
- states whether terms are identical, analogous, nested, causal, or merely correlated;
- predicts at least one differential outcome between candidate mechanisms;
- preserves known non-transfer and coexistence effects;
- does not infer an individual representation from system success, or communal knowledge from average individual scores;
- allows culturally different developmental pathways unless measurement equivalence is established.

### Fail examples

- “The infant forms object knowledge at 3.5 months” — bearer is clear, but content and criterion are inflated if only looking-time expectation is measured.
- “The community internalizes knowledge” — shifts bearer mid-sentence; communities do not literally internalize as individuals do.
- “Practice creates knowledge” — leaves practice type, feedback, content, transfer, and attribution criterion unspecified.
- “Participation is accommodation” — erases a relational change in access/identity by translating it into an individual scheme change.
- “All learning is Bayesian updating” — lacks a demonstrated mapping for bootstrapped representational change, participation, public artifacts, and epistemic norms.

## 10. Strongest expected cross-domain conflict

**Conflict:** *Where is knowing, and what makes the attribution true?*

- Representation-centered developmental and skill theories locate the relevant change primarily in the individual and test it through anticipation, inference, explanation, speed, accuracy, or transfer [S5–S15, S25–S35].
- Participation and distributed traditions locate at least some knowing in relations among persons, practices, artifacts, histories, and identities [S17–S23, S43].
- Epistemic-cognition traditions add a normative dimension: successful prediction or participation is not sufficient for justified knowledge [S44–S45].

**Why strongest:** This is not a dispute about which input produces the same internal product. It changes the product, bearer, success criterion, and failure mode. A learner can:

- possess a correct proposition but fail to participate;
- participate competently without being able to state the proposition;
- belong to a system that succeeds while misunderstanding a local relation;
- produce a correct answer through an unreliable process;
- contribute to a public idea that exceeds any one member’s grasp.

No scalar “amount of knowledge” preserves all five cases.

## 11. Rejected synthesis move

**Rejected:** “All traditions describe one generic cycle of assimilation, prediction error, and accommodation.”

**Reason for rejection:**

- It converts Piaget’s historically specific mechanism into a loose metaphor.
- It treats Bayesian model updating, ontological change, proceduralization, strategy selection, guided participation, identity transformation, and public idea improvement as if they shared a state space and bearer.
- It cannot explain why old intuitions coexist with scientific responses [S31], why street and school arithmetic diverge [S37], or why a navigation system can succeed without any individual possessing the system-level organization [S21].
- It makes no differential prediction and is therefore not a mechanism-level synthesis.

## 12. Evidence that would reverse or materially weaken the phase-A position

Current position: **typed pluralism**—several formation phenomena are real, but unification is licensed only after bearer, criterion, timescale, mechanism, context, persistence, and norm are aligned.

The position would be reversed or materially weakened by:

1. **Strong universal-stage evidence:** preregistered longitudinal studies across culturally diverse sites showing measurement invariance, cross-domain synchrony, fixed order, low within-person variability, and task-robust transitions not explained by schooling/support.
2. **Criterion convergence in infancy:** looking, reaching, search, explanation, and transfer measures converging on the same latent construct with predictive continuity into later explicit reasoning.
3. **Clean replacement evidence:** longitudinal and process evidence that incompatible naïve theories are erased rather than suppressed, with no conflict cost under speeded, stressful, or novel conditions.
4. **General transfer evidence:** learned abstractions transferring robustly across the Barnett–Ceci dimensions without contextual supports, deep-structure cues, or retraining.
5. **Bearer-reduction theorem plus evidence:** a principled, predictive reduction from participation/distributed-system descriptions to individual representational states that preserves identity, access, artifact, and coordination effects.
6. **Normative reduction:** evidence that predictive success or stable participation reliably entails justified belief across domains, making separate epistemic aims and standards redundant.
7. **Practice sufficiency:** prospective random-assignment evidence that amount/quality of deliberate practice explains nearly all stable expertise differences across multiple domains after opportunity, selection, prior ability, coaching, and measurement are controlled.

## Scope and evidence discipline

This is a comparison between the frozen Phase-A developmental/educational map and the initial definitions. It is not an essay draft. It does not treat the order of headings, questions, or examples as a developmental sequence. It makes no novelty claim.

The native comparison is typed by:

`content or competence × bearer × attribution criterion × timescale × mechanism × setting × persistence or failure mode × normative standard × evidence`

Without those coordinates, “knowledge formation” is too broad to support a single causal or developmental account.

Only four external boundary searches were added. They address testimony, affect and motivation, tacit knowing, and cultural transmission. The first three produced useful supplements. The transmission search did not yield a sufficiently direct, stable primary source and is therefore not used as evidence.

## Executive verdict

The initial definitions already avoid several familiar errors: they do not make an explicit question the universal beginning of knowledge; they refuse a compulsory linear sequence; they do not equate learning, memory, belief, skill, testimony, and knowledge; and they leave the bearer open beyond the isolated individual. Those are substantive confirmations.

The central unresolved problem is not another missing item in the list. It is that four different targets remain folded into “knowledge acquiring form”:

1. a capacity, representation, strategy, practice, or coordination changes;
2. an observer, learner, or community recognizes or attributes knowledge;
3. a claim or practice meets some epistemic or institutional norm;
4. an achievement is stabilized, transmitted, or preserved.

These targets can coincide, but the developmental literature supplies counterexamples to their equivalence. A framing that does not split them will repeatedly mistake a change in evidence for a change in competence, public acceptance for epistemic warrant, or preservation of a statement for preservation of understanding.

## 2. Vocabulary that the framing may be imposing

### 2.1 “Movement”

“Experiential, cognitive, social, and epistemic movements” is plural and cautious, but *movement* still suggests continuity, direction, and a process that travels. Some cases are better described as reorganization, selection among strategies, coordination, stabilization, participation, or a changed attribution. The term should not be the sole generic.

**Correction:** use “changes, relations, practices, and attribution conditions” unless a source warrants a directional process.

### 2.2 “Knowledge acquiring form”

This phrase can imply that knowledge exists as a preformed substrate awaiting shape, or that there is one target form. Developmental accounts instead concern changes in representations, inferential reach, skills, strategies, participation, coordination, and public artifacts. Whether any of these changes counts as knowledge is a further attribution or normative question.

**Correction:** separate what changes from the rule by which the result is called knowledge.

### 2.3 “Phenomenological”

A phenomenological entry can discipline attention to lived experience, but it can also privilege first-person availability and an individual experiential field. Distributed accomplishment, testimony, institutional memory, and knowledge that exceeds any participant’s representation do not fit automatically inside that register.

**Correction:** treat phenomenology as one evidential perspective, not as the universal container or origin of every bearer type.

### 2.4 “Receiving information”

This phrase carries a container or transfer metaphor: a stable item passes into a recipient. It can conceal interpretation, source evaluation, reconstruction, and changes produced by prior knowledge.

**Correction:** specify whether the event is exposure, uptake, encoding, interpretation, reliance on testimony, or successful learning.

### 2.5 “Transmission”

Transmission can suggest that content remains invariant across persons or generations. A statement may be copied while its inferential role is lost; a practice may persist while its rationale changes; a learner may reconstruct rather than receive an understanding.

**Correction:** report separately the fidelity of expression, use, justification, skill, and surrounding enabling conditions.

### 2.6 “Subject or unit”

The phrase combines at least four questions: who or what bears the achievement, what the unit of analysis is, where the relevant process occurs, and to what target an observer attributes knowledge.

**Correction:** keep **bearer**, **unit of analysis**, **causal locus**, and **attribution target** distinct.

### 2.7 “Accepted under recorded conditions”

This is appropriate as a local lifecycle status. Outside that register it risks equating acceptance with knowledge and privileging mature propositional, documentary, and institutional forms over practical or tacit competence.

**Correction:** label it explicitly as an architectural status predicate, not a general definition of knowledge.

### 2.8 “Boundaries among” the listed terms

The wording can imply that each term occupies a clean partition. The definitions correctly deny mutual exclusivity, but the spatial metaphor can restore the separation it disclaims.

**Correction:** investigate relations, attribution criteria, and failure cases rather than presume fixed boundaries.

## 3. Material omissions

### 3.1 An explicit attribution test

For each example, the framing needs to ask:

- What content or competence is attributed?
- To which bearer?
- By which observable criterion?
- Under what task, support, and cultural conditions?
- Which alternative explanation has been excluded?

Object-permanence research is the canonical warning: looking-time, anticipatory response, and manual search need not yield the same attribution at the same age. A discrepancy may reflect response demands, inhibition, memory, or action planning rather than the presence or absence of a single all-purpose concept.

### 3.2 Competence versus performance

Failure on a task does not directly demonstrate absent knowledge, and success does not by itself show a stable, general competence. Support, cueing, language, inhibition, familiarity, and response modality can change performance.

**Required addition:** every developmental example should state whether its evidence concerns spontaneous performance, supported performance, retained competence, or transfer.

### 3.3 Culture and task meaning

Tasks are not culturally neutral windows. School arithmetic and market arithmetic can recruit different routines, artifacts, and meanings. A result may fail to transfer without showing that nothing was learned.

**Required addition:** record setting, cultural practice, task interpretation, and the population across which the attribution is meant to generalize.

### 3.4 Timescale

Microgenetic strategy change, developmental reorganization, apprenticeship, institutional stabilization, and intergenerational transmission occur on different timescales. They should not be narrated as phases of one process without evidence.

**Required addition:** attach a timescale to every claimed change.

### 3.5 Change architecture

The framing names revision but does not distinguish:

- replacement;
- reorganization;
- enrichment or differentiation;
- redistribution among competing strategies;
- coexistence with contextual selection;
- inhibition or suppression without elimination;
- coordination across persons and artifacts.

Conceptual-change evidence often supports coexistence rather than eradication. Strategy research likewise shows overlapping procedures rather than one clean succession.

### 3.6 Context and transfer

Knowledge displayed in one setting may not appear in another. Transfer varies with content, context, temporal distance, functional relation, and prompting.

**Required addition:** specify whether a claim is local performance, near transfer, far transfer, or context-independent competence.

### 3.7 Formation, attribution, warrant, and stabilization

These four targets are presently adjacent but not cleanly separated.

| Target | Core question | Typical evidence | Characteristic error |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formation or reorganization | What changed in capacity, representation, strategy, practice, or coordination? | Longitudinal, microgenetic, learning, process, or intervention evidence | Inferring an inner change from one successful response |
| Attribution or recognition | Why does an observer or community count the bearer as knowing? | Task criteria, discourse, assessment, source evaluation | Treating the criterion as the phenomenon itself |
| Warrant | What makes the belief, claim, or practice epistemically acceptable? | Reasons, reliability, evidence, norms, defeaters | Equating social acceptance with justification |
| Stabilization or preservation | What continues across time, persons, or settings? | Retention, transfer, replication, records, practice continuity | Treating statement persistence as understanding persistence |

### 3.8 Causal versus normative vocabulary

“Epistemic movement” can mix a causal description of change with a normative judgment that the result is justified or reliable.

**Required addition:** mark whether each claim is descriptive, causal, attributional, or normative.

### 3.9 Public knowledge versus individual learning

A body of public claims can be preserved while no individual understands the whole; an individual can learn while no public artifact changes. Distributed systems can achieve outcomes no single member can reproduce.

**Required addition:** do not infer change in one bearer from persistence or performance at another level.

### 3.10 Error modes in the observer’s account

The framing should name false positives and false negatives in knowledge attribution:

- prompted success mistaken for stable competence;
- verbal failure mistaken for conceptual absence;
- copied language mistaken for understanding;
- fluent performance mistaken for justified belief;
- group output attributed to each participant;
- persistence of records mistaken for preservation of usable orientation.

### 3.11 Power, access, and legitimacy

Participation and institutional acceptance are shaped by authority, access to tools, and whose testimony or competence is recognized. These conditions affect visibility and attribution without automatically determining truth or warrant.

**Required addition:** distinguish social authorization from epistemic justification while examining their interaction.

### 3.12 Affect and motivation

The known-gaps list includes affect, but it remains untyped. Pintrich, Marx, and Boyle show that goals, values, self-efficacy, control beliefs, and classroom context mediate conceptual change. Treating cognition as “cold” can misdescribe both engagement with anomalous evidence and persistence through revision.

**Required addition:** type affect and motivation as possible mediators or constituents in particular mechanisms, not as a decorative contextual list.

### 3.13 Testimony as selective reliance

Testimony is more than transferred content. Children evaluate source knowledge and prior accuracy when deciding whom to trust. Koenig and colleagues show that young children selectively rely on knowledgeable or previously accurate speakers for new information.

**Required addition:** distinguish exposure to testimony, comprehension, source evaluation, reliance, retention, and warranted acceptance.

### 3.14 Tacit knowing

Tacit knowing is not merely an unrecorded proposition. It can pervade practical, propositional, and experiential knowledge, and may be expressed in discrimination or skilled action without exhaustive verbalization.

**Required addition:** do not use explicit articulation or recorded provenance as the universal criterion for knowledge attribution.

## 4. False equivalences to block explicitly

| Do not equate | Reason |
|---|---|
| Becoming discernible with arising | Better evidence may reveal a pre-existing capacity. |
| Acquiring form with being attributed | A phenomenon and a criterion for recognizing it are different objects. |
| Formation with justification | A belief can form without being warranted; a warranted public claim can be learned poorly. |
| Social acceptance with epistemic warrant | Acceptance may reflect authority, convention, or institutional procedure. |
| Participation with internalization | Coordinated success need not imply an equivalent representation in every participant. |
| Transmission with preserved understanding | Expressions can persist while use, rationale, or competence decays. |
| Learning with knowledge | Learning is a change process; knowledge is an attribution or normative status under some criterion. |
| Memory with knowledge | Retention can preserve error; knowing can rely on external resources. |
| Confidence with justification | Metacognitive assurance and evidential warrant can diverge. |
| Individual, group, institution, and distributed system as equivalent bearers | Their composition, evidence, responsibility, and persistence conditions differ. |
| Stage-like aggregate pattern with a universal stage | Task and support effects can generate apparent discontinuities. |
| Correct response with stable competence | Guessing, cueing, imitation, or narrow task learning remain alternatives. |

## 5. Proposed corrections to the research frame

### 5.1 Replace the provisional question

Current form: a search for “movements” participating in “knowledge acquiring form.”

Recommended research question:

> Which changes, relations, practices, and attribution conditions are described as knowledge forming; with respect to what content or competence, bearer, criterion, timescale, mechanism, context, persistence or failure mode, normative standard, and evidence? Where do translations among these descriptions fail?

This is a research-frame correction, not proposed essay prose.

### 5.2 Split the object of inquiry

Maintain four linked but non-identical registers:

1. **formation/reorganization** of capacities, representations, strategies, practices, or coordination;
2. **discernibility/attribution** by the learner, observer, or community;
3. **warrant/acceptability** under an explicit norm;
4. **stabilization/preservation** across time, people, and settings.

Any later synthesis should state which register it occupies and justify crossings between them.

### 5.3 Add a claim-typing template

For every general sentence, record:

```text
content_or_competence:
bearer:
attribution_criterion:
timescale:
mechanism:
setting_and_culture:
persistence_or_failure_mode:
normative_standard:
evidence_and_alternatives:
```

### 5.4 Add four mandatory counterexample tests

1. **Criterion sensitivity:** infant expectation succeeds while manual search fails.
2. **Context sensitivity:** competence appears in market arithmetic but not school-format arithmetic, or conversely.
3. **Coexistence:** scientific reasoning is available while intuitive explanations remain active.
4. **Distributed accomplishment:** coordinated navigation succeeds although no individual contains the whole procedure or state.

A general formulation that cannot state what it predicts in all four cases is underspecified.

### 5.5 Type testimony

Replace a generic “testimony/transmission” relation with:

`speaker/source × content × recipient × source-evaluation cue × uptake × retention × warrant × later use`

This prevents reliable source selection from being reduced to passive reception.

### 5.6 Type persistence

Ask separately whether the following persist:

- wording or inscription;
- retrievable belief;
- inferential understanding;
- practical skill;
- coordinated practice;
- access to artifacts;
- provenance and defeaters;
- institutional capacity;
- reliable orientation in a setting.

### 5.7 Constrain the architectural definition

Use the operational definition only in the form:

> Within this architecture, an item receives the lifecycle status “knowledge” when it is accepted under recorded conditions of provenance, evidence, review, scope, version, and state.

Do not infer that this predicate exhausts ordinary, developmental, practical, social, or epistemological uses of knowledge.

## 6. Boundary decisions

### Decision A — no universal first movement

The developmental map does not justify choosing perception, information reception, explicit questioning, memory, social recognition, or justification as the universal beginning. Any such choice must be labeled as an expository entry point.

### Decision B — phenomenology is a perspective, not a bearer theory

Lived experience can organize an introduction, but it does not settle whether the relevant bearer is an individual, dyad, practice, institution, or distributed system.

### Decision C — architecture and development use different status predicates

Recorded acceptance is a lifecycle predicate. Developmental competence, practical knowing, and epistemic warrant require other criteria. Translation is possible only after the predicates are stated.

### Decision D — local ordered dependencies remain admissible

Rejecting a universal stage sequence does not require rejecting every prerequisite relation. A source may establish a local dependency for a specified task, population, mechanism, and support condition.

### Decision E — “knowledge” may appear early only with a typed local use

An early occurrence is defensible if the bearer and attribution criterion are named and the text does not silently universalize that use.

## 7. Collapse tests for the corrected frame

These are not novelty claims; they are tests of whether the proposed distinctions add analytical value.

- **Formation/attribution split collapses** if every change in the available evidence is shown to coincide with a change in the relevant competence across tasks and supports. Existing criterion-sensitive findings argue against that collapse.
- **Bearer typing collapses** if individual, group, institutional, and distributed attributions have identical evidence, composition, responsibility, and persistence conditions. Existing distributed-cognition cases argue against that collapse.
- **Persistence typing collapses** if preserved wording reliably entails preserved understanding, skill, practice, access, provenance, and orientation. The initial definitions themselves anticipate failures of this entailment.
- **Context typing collapses** if competence and transfer are invariant across task format, setting, cultural practice, and support. Existing transfer and situated-cognition evidence argues against that collapse.

## 8. Dissent and residual uncertainty

### Dissent

The initial definitions are careful enough to resist a stage narrative, but their central phrase still invites a single-process reading. Merely expanding the inventory of factors would not fix that problem. The inquiry needs a typed grammar of claims before synthesis.

### Residual uncertainty

- The boundary between tacit competence and knowledge depends on the normative standard adopted; the developmental literature alone cannot settle it.
- Distributed accomplishment does not automatically entail distributed epistemic responsibility.
- Selective trust shows source evaluation, but not that every accepted testimonial belief is justified or understood.
- Affect and motivation can mediate conceptual change; whether they partly constitute particular forms of knowing requires a more specific claim and evidence base.
- Cultural transmission remains an evidence gap in this supplemental pass. No claim about fidelity or reconstructive transmission here relies on the discarded search result.
- If the project intends only a local editorial lexicon, the full scientific typing template may be heavier than necessary. In that case, it should say that the register is local and refrain from general claims about formation.

## Tensions and translation failures

### Formal structure ↔ epistemic meaning

`Knowledge T`, `ObserverAct`, `KnowledgeLoop`, and reflection-tower carriers are mathematically valid structures. Their names do not prove that real knowledge, observers, learning, or reflection instantiate them. The files themselves repeatedly say “model-of-math ≠ real-process-obeys-it.” Translation fails when a conditional theorem is narrated as an empirical or phenomenological account.

### Representation ↔ possession

A representable functor has an owned categorical meaning. A person “holding” a theory is stipulated as a functor into it. No theorem bridges representability or slice membership to human possession, understanding, skill, belief, or justification. The free lever `Held/encode` carries the conceptual residue.

### Probe separation ↔ reconstruction

Yoneda probes can separate parallel maps while reconstruction requires full faithfulness/density and closure adds essential surjectivity. A probe family can therefore improve distinctions without generating a theory or warranting that the observed object has been recovered.

### Residue ↔ readout

The repository historically moved among complement, unit defect, entropy, cost, count, carrier, and functor-action readings. `RESIDUE-ONTOLOGY.md` corrects this by making adapters mandatory. Translation fails whenever a scalar or observed mark is called residue merely because it records loss.

### Observer choice ↔ functor data

Formal artifacts can store a chosen functor and prove properties of it. They do not explain who chose it, under what purposes and authority, why its distinctions matter, or whether another observer would adopt it. The philosophical corpus names this residue; Lean does not contain it.

### Learning ↔ preservation

Conservative `Learning` preserves an earlier encoding. H2 learning changes next-probe policy. Discovery/regularization in `distilled-knowledge/knowledge-evolution-typing.md` instead changes schema via failures of Full/EssSurj/Faithful. These may interact, but the repository has not proved them equivalent or sequential.

### Reflection ↔ repair

The tower adds or receives carrier vocabulary and proves persistence/nonclosure. It does not infer a repair policy from an obstruction. Calling promotion “learning from residue” silently inserts a selection and authority mechanism not present in the formal artifact.

## Knowledge-formation coherence test

Treat a repository account as a coherent knowledge-formation account only if it answers all eight questions without defining the answer into the interface:

1. **Unit:** What changes — person, held schema, instance, probe policy, institution, or corpus?
2. **Phenomenon:** Is the change information receipt, distinction gain, skill, belief, understanding, justification, discovery, or retention?
3. **Lens:** Which distinctions/readouts are supplied, by whom, and for which task?
4. **Probe:** What is observed, and does the family merely separate or actually reconstruct?
5. **Residue:** Which structural dock/component is used, and what explicit adapter produces the reported readout?
6. **Update:** What rule changes the state, and is that rule derived, independently justified, or stipulated?
7. **Reflection:** What authorizes a new vocabulary or level, and is carrier content produced or merely supplied?
8. **Warrant:** What proves the mathematical proposition, and what separately warrants the real-process/epistemic interpretation?

**Result on the fixed snapshot:** the repository passes these questions locally in several files because it states its ceilings, but fails them as a single unified formation process. The relevant objects live at different scales and claim types. “Knowledge formation” is therefore a defensible research umbrella, not a currently coherent formal unit. **Type: interpreted audit conclusion from explicit artifact boundaries, not theorem.**

## 4. Gaps and inconsistencies

### G1 - No reusable-understanding aggregate

There is no schema or service whose identity is an accepted understanding and whose required fields bind:

`content/fact version + provenance + evidence set + review result + accepting actor + authority/policy + scope + policy/version + effective state/time + supersession/revocation`.

Evidence: APT Domain **External References, Not APT Concepts** assigns knowledge/assertion/ontology acceptance to “Future external governance”; the installed Inventory is empty and non-authoritative; the overview that names the composite is `authority: proposal-only`. This is an absence claim bounded to the inspected snapshot and supported by the schema/code search, not a novelty claim.

### G2 - “Accepted” is overloaded across layers

`accepted_event_id` and command receipt `status: accepted` mean that a closed command was durably admitted. Review acceptance means a final approver accepted a change-request report. Dispatch confirmation means execution authorization. Proposed knowledge acceptance means permission for bounded reuse. No native type prevents a reader from conflating them.

Evidence: APT-R2; projector failure test where the authoritative receipt is accepted while the projection is absent; review skill **Close**; proposal-only overview **Continuidade do conhecimento**.

### G3 - Review provenance is intentionally incomplete

Review preserves the target quotation, conclusion, coverage summary, and accepted change requests, but explicitly discards attacker/verifier returns. This is an owner-accepted cost, not an accidental omission. It conflicts with any later requirement to reconstruct independence, rejected alternatives, or why a finding was refuted.

Evidence: `.claude/skills/review/SKILL.md`, warning **No transcript. At all** and the recorded owner ruling. The same skill states historical `attacks.md`/`findings.md` remain as history, proving the protocol changed.

### G4 - Document maturity cannot encode negative lifecycle outcomes

`vault/ontology-conventions.md` provides `draft|exploratory|active|consolidated|evergreen`; it says discoveries/plans may be superseded and evergreen leaves only by documented refutation plus formal review. Yet `superseded`, `refuted`, `retired`, `restricted`, and `revoked` are not document status values. Inventory has some of these statuses; APT has different research-local dispositions; none composes with document status.

### G5 - Supersession is local, not transitive

APT correctly preserves immutable predecessor chains for captures/facts, but citations, reviews, decisions, inventory cards, and narrative claims are not invalidated when their source capture becomes non-head. `input_now_superseded` is query-time display state for synthesis pins, not a dependency invalidation protocol.

Evidence: APT `ResearchCapture` lifecycle, APT-R5, and **Research Capture Currentness**.

### G6 - Scope is operationally exact but epistemically weak

Capabilities strongly constrain runtime operations. Formalization `scope` and proposed knowledge scope are semantic boundaries, but the former is free text and the latter has no schema. No operation checks whether a future application falls within the scope in which a claim was accepted.

### G7 - Source consultation is not understanding or support

The model correctly separates recommendation, delivery, access observation, declared use, claim relation and claim-support check. However, only part of that chain is implemented. Even a complete chain would show use/support evidence, not learning or understanding.

Evidence: APT-DOM-19..22; Stage G ingestion coverage table; `ReferenceUseKind.claimed_consulted` explicitly says it is not host access evidence.

### G8 - Canonical definition authority has no operational knowledge terms

Definitions Governance says only configured canonical definition sources may define normative semantics. `definitions/DEFINITIONS.md` currently maps system primitives and category-theory parallels but not the requested epistemic vocabulary. The proposal-only overview therefore cannot quietly become the canonical definition of knowledge.

### G9 - Spec/implementation boundary is easy to overread

APT specs define ReferenceChecks, reference-claim relations, policy-keyed dispositions, independent assessments, formalization governance refs, and other extensions. The convenience implementation accepts a smaller closed union: question, answer, reference use, problem, inferred claim, formalization. The detailed spec has `specAuthoringGate: in-review` and many rules say “planned.” Claims about implemented semantic review must use code/tests, not the complete spec.

### G10 - The repository records stale-state conflict but does not resolve it

`vault/audit/ledger-enum-drift-finding.md`, heading **Repair path (open)**, calls enum drift the keystone next step for Phase 2. `sessions/2026-07-22-1315-phase2-confirm-handoff.md`, **Contradictions**, says Phase 2 shipped after re-scoping the drift to a veracity label. ## 9. Rejected synthesis move

**Rejected:** construct one epistemic ladder such as

`captured -> accepted -> current -> supported -> reviewed -> promoted -> active -> knowledge`.

This move is invalid because every term belongs to a different owner and answers a different predicate:

- `captured`: whether a raw witness exists;
- `accepted`: whether ACI durably admitted a command/event;
- `current`: which immutable chain member is the replay head;
- `supported/contested/refuted`: research-local assessment under a policy;
- `reviewed`: a formalization was examined without becoming canonical;
- `promoted`: an Inventory candidate points to a downstream governed artifact;
- `active/evergreen`: document maturity;
- `resolved`: a review dispatch delivered an accepted change-request list.

Collapsing these labels would manufacture an authority transition that no owner grants. The repository's native separation should be retained until a real acceptance aggregate and owner contract exist.

**Decision: needs review, with a negative operational verdict on end-to-end reusable understanding.** The provenance and authority substrate is substantial and test-backed, but the operational model stops before governed acceptance, semantic reuse scope, and knowledge revocation. The appropriate next inspection target is not another vocabulary synthesis; it is the missing cross-owner acceptance contract, reviewed against the rejected-ladder failure above. This is a gap classification, not a claim of novelty.

## Repository protocol for claims that may become reusable

The protocol implicit across the artifacts is:

1. Preserve the source occurrence and distinguish it from later interpretation.
2. State the lens, expectation, relevance criterion, scope, and contact by which a readout matters.
3. Separate signal, evidence, witness, judgment, and represented residue.
4. Keep descriptions provisional until evidence, review, acceptance, and authority are independently recorded.
5. Record direct relations and their witnesses; do not infer meaning from folder proximity, chronology, summaries, or lineage.
6. Append corrections and supersessions so the earlier account remains recoverable.
7. Promote only supported claims into a new artifact with its own contract; do not promote a notebook wholesale.
8. Transport content without silent rewriting, and keep the receiver’s interpretation attributable as a new act.

This is a coherent protocol for repository truthfulness. It becomes an experiential theory only by an unsupported translation: replacing “what can be retrieved and audited” with “what is known,” or replacing “what can be executed from an instruction artifact” with “what someone knows how to do.”

## Drift, gaps, and inconsistencies

### 1. “Knowledge machine” → governed record

The superseded seed Plan proposed a single T0 engine in which probing and residue enrich a knowledge model. The later architecture essays retreat to a safer account: append-only facts, hypothesis descriptions, acceptance boundaries, and inspectable provenance. This is a justified demotion, but the vocabulary still occasionally inherits the old ambition. “Knowledge store,” “knowledge graph,” and “learning” often name storage/promotion/revision mechanisms even where no account of a knower survives.

### 2. Learning → model revision or curriculum order

Two meanings dominate: (a) revising a representation after residue and (b) ordering concepts for reader comprehension. Neither addresses retention, embodied capacity, skilled performance, changed perception, or social apprenticeship. The repository should not infer that these are smaller instances of the same generic `learn` operation without a bridge.

### 3. Skill → instruction artifact

The protocol-compilation experiment preserves a skill’s “stable meaning” by versioning instructions, topology, parameters, and review obligations. That is useful engineering, but it silently replaces practical mastery with an executable text. A person following a skill file, an agent producing a compliant trace, and a practitioner who can discern what the instructions do not say are presently indistinguishable unless outcome evidence exposes the difference.

### 4. Observation → accepted event

The conceptual notebook makes observation situated and contact-dependent. Runtime architecture makes observations candidates for validation, journaling, aggregation, and replay. The translation is legitimate only if the accepted event keeps the contact, observer, lens, scope, and transformation that produced it. Otherwise the architecture preserves a payload while deleting the conditions under which it became a readout.

### 5. Interpretation is necessary conceptually and prohibited operationally

The notebook needs interpretation to turn signal into evidence. The fabric forbids reinterpretation in transit. This separation is sound for provenance, but there is no fully owned receiving layer where situated understanding is reconstructed, contested, or learned. The aggregator may interpret outputs, yet its product is another artifact/judgment, not evidence that recipients acquired the same sense.

### 6. Explicitness is both safeguard and distortion risk

The architecture repeatedly says that unrecorded distinctions cannot be recovered. The exploratory essay explicitly warns that some tacit, unstable, or contested distinctions should remain informal because explicitness can falsely stabilize them. The repository has no policy for preserving a *known non-explicit remainder* except open questions and prose caveats.

## Strongest conflict with operational containment

The strongest conflict is between **R5/D6 lossless transport** and the repository’s own **lens/contact account**.

- Operational containment correctly requires that the fabric not author, summarize, reinterpret, or rank content.
- Experiential inquiry says that a readout becomes evidence only through a lens, expectation, relevance criterion, and judgment.
- Therefore content can be transmitted without the understanding that made it evidence being transmitted.

If the system treats exact payload + provenance + accepted state as preserved knowledge, it commits the very category error the notebook avoids: it collapses occurrence, readout, interpretation, warrant, and residue into a transportable record. The repair is not to let the fabric interpret. It is to represent the recipient-side act of interpretation as a distinct, attributable, revisable occurrence and to keep open the possibility that sense was not transmitted.

## Reversal evidence

1. **T0/self-similarity reversal:** the archived seed claims one root loop across epistemic, categorical, and engineering faces, then records that no close row enriches the model and marks the current self-similar instance falsified. The Plan is now superseded.
2. **“Laws” → heuristics:** the operational notebook says earlier notes called compression/composition/refinement statements “laws”; it demotes them to conditional heuristics with domains, costs, exceptions, and falsifiers.
3. **Predictive grammar → accepted negative:** the notebook records `confirmed-kill early-stop`; its grammar remains editorial/integrative but loses incremental predictive status.
4. **Minimal kernel → dependency hypothesis:** the notebook prohibits calling orientation, distinction, relation/transformation, and composition minimal, sufficient, independent, or primitive until counterexamples and dependency analysis support those words.

These reversals are evidence of a healthy demotion protocol. They are also evidence that the architecture’s current containment vocabulary should not be mistaken for the mature epistemology the older “knowledge machine” name anticipated.

## Coherence test

**Test case:** transmit one identical readout, with identical source provenance, to two recipients who have different prior lenses or practical capacities.

The native account is coherent only if the repository can record all of the following without forcing equality:

1. one payload and one transmission history;
2. two recipient-side interpretations with their own lens/context;
3. possibly different evidence judgments and confidence;
4. possibly different abilities to use the result;
5. later revision of either interpretation without rewriting the original readout; and
6. an explicit “uptake unknown” state when no evidence of understanding or ability exists.

**Failure condition:** byte identity, provenance identity, or shared acceptance automatically produces one shared knowledge state. No launch-snapshot artifact demonstrates that the test passes. The test is therefore proposed, not implemented.

## Tensions and translation failures

| Translation attempted by the repository | What survives | What is lost or unowned |
|---|---|---|
| experience → artifact | attributable statement, scope, timestamp, status | felt salience, attention, bodily orientation, unarticulated discrimination |
| practice → skill file/protocol | repeatable instruction and execution boundary | connoisseurship, improvisation, knowing when the rule misfits |
| observation → event/readout | payload, observer metadata if supplied, replay | perceptual availability and the learned capacity to notice |
| interpretation → judgment | explicit claim, scale, rationale, acceptance state | pre-predicative integration and transformation of the interpreter |
| learning → revision | before/after versions, residue, new distinctions | retention, transfer, skilled action, changed perception |
| testimony/transmission → exact delivery | content identity, source, provenance, acknowledgement | recipient uptake, trust calibration, preservation of sense |
| memory → append-only history | recoverable prior records | living recollection, forgetting, salience, embodied readiness |

## Rejected synthesis move

Reject the smooth sequence:

`experience → observation → information → interpretation → learning → understanding → acceptance → knowledge → transmission`

It is unsupported for four independent reasons: the notebook says registers are not stages; its motifs may be projections; the learning-order artifact is pedagogical rather than epistemic; and architecture acceptance governs reuse/authority rather than truth or understanding. A polished sequence here would convert repository convenience into an origin claim.

## Closing assessment

The repository’s strongest native contribution is negative and architectural: do not erase the difference between a readout and its interpretation, a claim and its evidence, acceptance and authority, exact transport and authorship, or correction and historical rewrite. Its weakest area is the positive phenomenon Polanyi would force into view: skillful knowing often exceeds what can be stated, and explicit records can depend on capacities they do not contain. Until that remainder has an owner, the system should describe itself as governing explicit epistemic artifacts and their reuse—not as containing, transmitting, or producing knowledge without qualification.

**Dissent:** the launch corpus is more coherent than a simple “repository equals database” critique would suggest; it repeatedly marks its own boundaries and accepted negatives. My dissent is narrower: those caveats have not yet been carried into an operational representation of recipient uptake, tacit competence, or interpretive transformation, so the architecture still defaults to containment when experiential evidence is absent.
