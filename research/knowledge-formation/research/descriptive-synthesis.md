Claim types remain distinct:

- **empirical**: behavioral, developmental, neuropsychological, interactional, or meta-analytic evidence;
- **phenomenological**: disciplined descriptions of lived organization, not causal findings by themselves;
- **historical/interpretive**: reconstructions of changing concepts, practices, and arguments;
- **formal proved**: a named Lean declaration with a proof term in the inspected source, without a new-build attestation;
- **implemented**: executable code, schema, test-backed runtime, or active protocol exists;
- **proposed/aspirational**: an interface, interpretation, experiment, or lifecycle is described but not operationally established;
- **retracted/stale**: a later audit, source correction, or current-tree check demoted the earlier claim.

No repository term receives epistemic authority merely from its name. In BR-05's wording, `Knowledge` is a “stipulation, not a theorem”; in BR-07, journal `accepted` means durable admission, not truth or reuse permission.

## 2. Descriptive result

The corpus supports a typed plurality, not a common formation sequence. The recurring comparison dimensions are:

`object or achievement × bearer/site × claim type × scale × warrant × diachrony × attribution rule × failure mode`.

Across the returns, the following are supported as relevant but not interchangeable:

- prior cognition, thought-signs, habits, genuine doubt, inquiry, collateral experience, interpretants, and future corrigibility (BR-01);
- historically effected understanding, testimony, participation, thought collectives, distributed cognition, archives, repertoires, classification, power, credit, and organized ignorance (BR-02);
- perception-action coupling, attention, affordances, embodied skill, affect, memory transformation, recognition, enaction, prior orientation, conceptual change, and social uptake (BR-03);
- schemes, assimilation, accommodation, equilibration, strategy distributions, core knowledge, bootstrapping, proceduralization, participation trajectories, distributed accomplishment, and public idea improvement (BR-04);
- schemas and instances, probes, separation, reconstruction, residue, update underdetermination, local/global witnesses, promotion carriers, provenance fields, and authority obligations (BR-05/06);
- immutable capture, scoped runtime authority, provenance, currentness, review, local supersession, exact transport, editorial revision, and the unimplemented boundary of reusable-understanding acceptance (BR-07/08).

The same word often names different objects. `Learning` can mean conservative commutation over a fixed theory, changed next-probe policy, conceptual reorganization, strategy redistribution, changed participation, curriculum order, or representation revision. `Acceptance` can mean journal admission, dispatch authorization, adoption of a review report, collective stance, or a proposed knowledge-lifecycle status. `Persistence` can mean habit, memory trace, repeatable performance, public record, practice continuity, current chain head, or institutional capacity. None of those lists is an ordered ladder.

## 9. Six cross-layer challenge questions

### Q1 — L1A × L1B

**Question:** Do historically inherited inquiry problems or embodied-developmental transformations supply the better unit of explanation, or are they non-comparable?

**Resolution: partial overlap, no general priority.** BR-01 says cognition is “determined by prior cognition” and epistemic significance is “diachronic, sign-mediated, environmentally constrained.” BR-03 describes “history-sensitive, selective, norm-laden change in organism–environment–social relations.” BR-04, however, requires a typed tuple of content, bearer, criterion, timescale, mechanism, setting, persistence, norm, and evidence. Historical problem genealogy and embodied/developmental transformation overlap in rejecting a punctual origin, but they use different units and warrants. Neither return supports declaring one the better universal unit.

### Q2 — L1A × L1C

**Question:** Can formal structures constrain historical and social accounts without converting interpretation into theorem?

**Resolution: possible constraint.** BR-05 says the formal corpus supplies “distinctions and non-implications that a future account must preserve” and that empirical or scholarly evidence could license a cross-corpus “possible constraint,” but “not turn prose into Lean proof.” BR-01 independently blocks `acceptance = belief = warrant = truth`, while BR-05 proves only bounded mathematical distinctions such as separation versus reconstruction and lineage versus transmitted capability. Formal results can exclude an invalid translation when a typed adapter is supplied; they do not prove Peircean, hermeneutic, social, or historical interpretations.

### Q3 — L1A × L1D

**Question:** Do operational acceptance lifecycles preserve or erase the historically and socially variable conditions of knowing?

**Resolution: partial overlap with an explicit preservation limit.** BR-07 shows that the runtime preserves exact bytes, provenance, scoped action authority, and local currentness, but “cannot establish that the record is reusable understanding.” BR-08 says exact transport preserves an utterance, “not necessarily its sense, skillful use, or uptake by a recipient.” BR-01 requires collateral experience and future corrigibility; BR-02 requires practice/media, power, classification, attribution, and possible controlled equivocation. The lifecycle preserves some historically relevant records, but the frozen evidence does not show that it represents the full social-historical conditions or recipient-side understanding.

### Q4 — L1B × L1C

**Question:** Can formal observation and preservation structures constrain embodied-developmental evidence at the same scale?

**Resolution: non-comparable at present; possible constraint only after an adapter.** BR-05 states that observation is “explicit maps and categorical thresholds, not perception or experience.” BR-03 warns that a neural or performance account can explain discrimination without explaining epistemic norm or attribution; BR-04 warns that task success can reflect cueing, support, or response demands rather than stable competence. The formal thresholds provide useful non-implications, but no frozen artifact connects their carrier, scale, or warrant to embodied or developmental measures. They therefore do not constrain that evidence “at the same scale” yet.

### Q5 — L1B × L1D

**Question:** Can operational records represent embodied skill and developmental change, or only their outputs?

**Resolution: possible constraint; current implementation represents outputs and histories, not the positive phenomena.** BR-08 says repository skill is “an instruction artifact/capability contract, not knowing-how or embodied competence,” and learning-as-revision omits “retention, transfer, skilled action, changed perception.” BR-07 says the runtime stores attributable records, “not a general account of perception, learning, skill, memory, or understanding.” BR-03/04 supply empirical and phenomenological discriminators—transfer, flexibility, task support, strategy coexistence, breakdown, and embodied responsiveness—that an operational representation would need to keep distinct. The records may preserve evidence about those phenomena; the snapshot does not identify record identity with the phenomena themselves.

### Q6 — L1C × L1D

**Question:** Does formal proof constrain operational knowledge governance, or are the relevant claims typed at different levels?

**Resolution: possible constraint with mostly different levels.** BR-05's provenance/authority theorems prove bounded facts about stored fields, obligation sets, lineage non-transmission, and operational equivalence, while explicitly denying provenance truth and product correspondence. BR-07 reports implemented runtime semantics for scoped capabilities, immutable captures, projections, and local supersession. Where the same proposed invariant is explicitly modeled, formal results may be reused as constraints—for example, lineage alone transmits no authority. But Lean proofs do not establish that the runtime implements the model, and the runtime does not upgrade a theorem into epistemic acceptance. Product correspondence and semantic adequacy remain separate obligations.

## 10. Preserved questions and limits

The descriptive corpus does not resolve:

- which local success norms warrant an attribution of knowledge in each domain;
- when causal support becomes constitutive participation;
- when an individual, dyad, group, practice, institution, or coupled system is the bearer rather than a vehicle, contributor, authority, or attribution target;
- which forms of persistence preserve belief, justification, understanding, skill, interpretability, practice, or institutional capacity;
- whether and when testimony transmits understanding rather than enabling recipient reconstruction;
- how tacit capacities, refusal, organized ignorance, credit, and power should be represented without reducing them to missing fields;
- which typed adapters, if any, legitimately connect formal probe/reconstruction results to empirical or phenomenological observation;
- whether a future operational acceptance aggregate would govern reuse without misrepresenting truth, understanding, embodied competence, or recipient uptake.

These are preserved as unresolved descriptive boundaries. No candidate expository family, opening, universal sequence, or novelty claim is derived here.
