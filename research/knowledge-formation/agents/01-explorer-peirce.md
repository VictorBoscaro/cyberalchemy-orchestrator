Peirce confirms the boundary's strongest cautions: knowledge need not begin in an explicit question; information, belief, confidence, inquiry, learning, and truth are not stages of one sequence; present settlement is not truth; and an isolated individual is not an adequate universal epistemic subject. His native units are prior cognition, thought-sign, habit, genuine doubt, inquiry, abduction/deduction/induction, collateral experience, interpretant, self-control, and an indefinite community.

The boundary imposes `formation`, `movement`, and `acquiring form`. These are acceptable umbrella terms only if kept disjunctive. Peirce offers no universal formation process: some beliefs persist through tenacity or authority; some sign-effects add no knowledge; science may require confidence or hope rather than belief; and vital matters follow a different practical economy.

Main omissions are the object-side constraint of Secondness/collateral experience, coercive communal fixation, the normative ethics of inquiry, and the historical change from early hypothesis to mature abduction. Main false equivalences to block are acceptance = belief = warrant = truth, transmission = preservation of knowledge, community = consensus, and semiosis = knowledge-growth. The local operational definition is properly labeled architectural, but Peirce would require its recorded conditions themselves to remain criticizable by future inquiry.
