The boundary document substantially confirms the external map: it rejects a universal origin, sequence, isolated subject, exhaustive definition, and equivalence among information, learning, understanding, belief, memory, skill, testimony, justification, and knowledge. Its strongest supported move is treating the essay's entry point as expository rather than ontological.

Corrections remain necessary. “Knowledge acquiring form” and “movements” can still insinuate one directed process. “Subject or unit” conflates bearer, site, contributor, target of attribution, and authority. The baseline’s operational formula—understanding accepted under recorded conditions—is a governance criterion, not an epistemic definition: acceptance is neither necessary nor sufficient for knowledge. The gaps omit organized ignorance, silencing, epistemic injustice, credit allocation, classifications, embodied repertoire, coordination without consensus, and comparative equivocation.

Recommended typing: compare indexed epistemic achievements by bearer, practice/media, warrant or authority regime, temporal-material support, and failure mode. Keep formation, recognition, attribution, transmission, preservation, and loss distinct but potentially co-constitutive.

Dissent: A phenomenological opening centered on understanding would still privilege one tradition unless counterposed immediately by practice, distribution, power, and relational accountability.
