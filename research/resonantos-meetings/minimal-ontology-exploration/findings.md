# Findings: minimal meeting ontology exploration

## Evidence boundary

This synthesis compares six tentative attempts produced under participant, operational, and authority-boundary perspectives. It does not validate the proposals against real meetings or participant language, and it does not ratify definitions or policy ([initial definitions](../research-initial-definitions.md), [a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md)).

## Concepts proposed by each attempt

| Attempt | Proposed concepts |
| --- | --- |
| Authority A | Meeting occurrence; Agent; Recommendation; Decision ([a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md)) |
| Authority B | Meeting occurrence; Actor ([b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md)) |
| Operation A | Meeting context; Meeting instance; Participant; Result ([a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md)) |
| Operation B | Meeting occurrence; Actor; Intent; Meeting record ([b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md)) |
| Participant A | Meeting; Purpose; Access; Participation; Authority ([a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md)) |
| Participant B | Meeting; Purpose ([b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md)) |

## Genuine semantic convergences

All six attempts require one identifiable, bounded synchronous occasion, despite naming it `Meeting`, `Meeting instance`, or `Meeting occurrence`; this is the only concept-level convergence present in every attempt ([a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md), [a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md)).

All six also require people or collectives to stand in occurrence-specific relations, but they disagree on whether the bearer is an ontology concept (`Agent`, `Actor`, or `Participant`) or an external `Person` reference ([a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md), [a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md)).

The attempts consistently protect a semantic separation between mere access/presence, active participation, and decision effect or authority: none treats attendance or participation as automatically granting decision power ([a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md), [a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md), [a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md)). This is convergence on a distinction, not convergence on reifying `Access`, `Participation`, or `Authority` as concepts ([a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md), [a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md)).

Purpose and intent are semantically aligned as the reason or sought change for convening, but only both participant attempts and operation B elevate that meaning to a concept; operation A makes purpose/objective an attribute, and both authority attempts exclude it from their narrow cores ([a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md)).

Both operational attempts require durable post-meeting residue, although one models the addressable result and the other the access-bounded record containing result material; neither convergence extends to the participant or authority cores ([a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md), [a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md)).

## Disagreements

- **Whether purpose/intent is irreducible:** participant A, participant B, and operation B say yes; operation A treats it as an attribute, while the authority attempts deem it unnecessary for their boundary ([a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md)).
- **Whether the human/collective bearer belongs inside the ontology:** both authority attempts and both operational attempts reify an `Agent`, `Actor`, or `Participant`; both participant attempts keep `Person` external or treat participant as a relation-derived role ([a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md), [a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md)).
- **Whether access, participation, and authority are concepts:** participant A makes all three concepts; participant B makes access and participation relations and decision effect an attribute; both authority attempts use relations and qualifiers; both operational attempts use participation roles or values rather than three separate concepts ([a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md), [a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md), [a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md)).
- **Whether recommendations and decisions need independent identity:** authority A says both need objects to retain what was advised or determined; authority B treats them as governed effects unless a retention/audit need appears; operation A treats them as result types, operation B as material inside a record, and the participant attempts treat decision effect as a boundary/value rather than an output object ([a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md), [a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md)).
- **Whether recurring context is a concept:** operation A includes `Meeting context`; all other attempts omit, defer, or represent recurrence through grouping, relations, keys, or attributes ([a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md)).
- **What durable residue denotes:** operation A models a granular `Result`; operation B models a `Meeting record`; authority A separately models `Recommendation` and `Decision`; the remaining attempts do not require durable residue as core ([a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md), [a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md)).

## Likely attributes, relations, labels, or mechanics rather than ontology concepts

The strongest cross-attempt exclusions are time/date, duration, location/channel, cadence, topic, format, and meeting-family labels: attempts repeatedly classify these as attributes, labels, or optional classifications rather than independently identified things ([a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md), [a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md)).

Organizer, observer, attendee, speaker, facilitator, recommender, decider, assignee, and viewer are generally treated as roles or values on actor-to-meeting/record relations, not separate kinds of entity ([a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md)).

Invitations, agendas, pre-reads, scheduling, turn-taking, consent checks, recordings, publishing notes, and follow-up/routing are treated as procedures, artifacts, or implementation mechanics, though records or results may become concepts when durable retrieval is within scope ([a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md)).

Open/private/member-only and `inform`/`advise`/`recommend`/`decide-within-delegated-scope` are predominantly modeled as policy values, effects, or relation qualifiers; authority A is the notable exception for `Recommendation` and `Decision` as durable propositions/determinations ([a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md), [a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md)).

Contribution, reward, transparency, privacy policy, quorum, ratification, and delegation mechanics are consistently placed outside this minimal meeting boundary or deferred pending a demonstrated information need ([a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md), [a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md)).

## Smallest defensible common core

The strict intersection is one concept: **Meeting occurrence**, meaning one identifiable bounded synchronous occasion. Every attempt needs this referent, while no other proposed concept appears as a concept in all six ([a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md), [a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md)).

That one-concept core is not operationally self-sufficient without external references and typed facts. At minimum, the attempts collectively require relations or attributes that can express who relates to the occurrence and distinguish access/presence, participation, and authority effect; this statement identifies shared representational obligations, not additional agreed concepts ([a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md), [a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md)).

`Purpose/Intent` is the strongest candidate addition beyond the strict intersection because three attempts reify it and another retains purpose/objective as an attribute, but the authority attempts show it is not indispensable to every bounded question ([a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md)).

## Perspective-specific additions

- **Participant-facing:** `Purpose` supports relevance judgments; explicit access, participation, and authority boundaries protect newcomers from confusing permission to enter, permission to contribute, and decision effect ([a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md)).
- **Operational:** an actor/responsibility referent, intent or standing context, and durable `Result` or `Meeting record` support convening, accountability, continuity, retrieval, and follow-through ([a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md)).
- **Authority-boundary:** an actor/agent referent plus separate scoped presence, participation, recommendation, and decision facts prevent authority from being inferred from attendance or activity; durable `Recommendation` and `Decision` objects remain disputed even within this perspective ([a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md)).

## Unresolved questions

- Does the ontology need an internal `Actor`/`Person` concept, or is an external identity reference sufficient across operational and governance uses? ([a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md), [a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md))
- Must purpose/intent have independent identity, or can it remain a required attribute of an occurrence or recurring context? ([a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md), [a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md))
- Do access, participation, and authority need first-class boundary objects, or are typed relations and qualified values sufficient without losing consequential distinctions? ([a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md), [a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md), [a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md))
- When do recommendations, decisions, commitments, questions, or other results require stable identity and lifecycle rather than types or contents of a meeting record? ([a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md), [a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md))
- Does a recurring context need independent identity and inherited rules, or will occurrence grouping plus shared attributes suffice? ([a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md))
- Which distinctions survive testing against actual ResonantOS meeting cases and participant language? The evidence baseline explicitly says that validation has not happened ([initial definitions](../research-initial-definitions.md), [a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md)).

**Dispatch answer:** The smallest defensible common ontology is one bounded synchronous **Meeting occurrence**, supplemented by typed relational facts; every additional concept remains perspective-dependent and unvalidated ([a-authority](attempts/a-authority.md), [b-authority](attempts/b-authority.md), [a-operation](attempts/a-operation.md), [b-operation](attempts/b-operation.md), [a-participant](attempts/a-participant.md), [b-participant](attempts/b-participant.md)).
