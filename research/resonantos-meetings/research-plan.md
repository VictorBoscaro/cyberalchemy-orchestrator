# Research plan: ResonantOS meetings

> **Admission note:** `research-plan` is a candidate canonical kind. Its producer, authority rollup,
> instance contract, and permitted relations remain open in `CANONICAL-KINDS.md` OQ-20. This file
> tests the artifact shape; it does not claim admitted canonical conformance.

## Purpose

Produce enough evidence to recommend a meeting model that a new community member can understand
and an organizer can use. The model should make five things clear:

- why a meeting exists;
- what result it seeks;
- who may observe, participate, recommend, or decide;
- what preparation is needed; and
- what memory remains afterward.

The research will inform a public, plain-language proposal. It will not decide the complete
governance model of ResonantOS.

## Starting point

### Source-backed baseline

- ResonantDAO describes weekly calls as early social infrastructure for alignment, learning,
  roadmap understanding, relationships, and active participation.
- Its contribution model treats onboarding, mentoring, moderation, care, education, governance,
  and technical work as distinct but legitimate contributions.
- Current governance is in a custodian-led bootstrap with community advisory participation.
- The official sources do not establish that every meeting must be public or recorded.

Primary sources:

- <https://resonantdao.com/whitepaper/>
- <https://resonantos.com/>
- <https://augmentatism.com/>

### Proposals to test

1. `work` and `community life` are useful primary families.
2. They describe primary purpose, not mutually exclusive boxes.
3. Format, access, participation, authority, preparation, and documentation are independent
   dimensions.
4. A recurring meeting series and one occurrence need different records.
5. Openness and transparency should be defaults with explicit, justified exceptions.

None of these proposals is a conclusion.

## Research questions

1. Which meetings exist or are concretely planned?
2. What need and expected result justify each one?
3. Can real meetings be oriented by the two proposed families without forcing ambiguous cases?
4. Which smaller formats recur inside each family?
5. What must a person know to decide whether and how to participate?
6. Who may observe, speak, facilitate, recommend, and decide in each forum?
7. When are agenda, pre-read, notes, decisions, actions, recording, or privacy necessary?
8. How much maintenance does the model impose on organizers?
9. Can a new or occasional participant use its language without specialist explanation?

## Scope

### Included

- official sources that directly constrain meetings, participation, authority, memory, or trust;
- current and concretely planned ResonantOS meeting formats;
- available calendars, invitations, agendas, notes, recordings, and follow-up artifacts;
- short conversations with organizers and participants;
- counterexamples that challenge the proposed families; and
- at most three external precedents, used only to resolve a concrete ambiguity.

### Excluded

- the complete architecture of ResonantDAO governance;
- final authority, privacy, recording, retention, or contribution-reward policies;
- selection of calendar, video, or documentation tools;
- deciding which meetings must be created or cancelled; and
- a complete ontology of the community.

## Evidence sample

Analyze 10–15 real meetings or formats, covering where available:

- technical or project teams;
- coordination or stewardship;
- governance or decision forums;
- building sessions or hackathons;
- Academy, onboarding, or learning;
- weekly calls or town halls; and
- social, care, or celebration gatherings.

Conduct 5–8 short conversations across these perspectives:

- organizers;
- community leaders or stewards;
- frequent contributors;
- occasional participants;
- at least one new or less-contextualized member; and
- when available, someone who stopped attending or encountered participation friction.

This is a diagnostic sample, not statistical representation.

## Method

1. **Inventory:** record each meeting's current purpose, convener, participants, authority,
   expected result, and documentation.
2. **Directed reading:** extract only official commitments relevant to the model.
3. **Short interviews:** ask about concrete situations before presenting the proposed taxonomy.
4. **Classification test:** apply the candidate model and record ambiguity, overlap, and missing
   fields.
5. **Comprehension test:** ask people uninvolved in drafting to classify examples and explain the
   fields in their own words.
6. **Adversarial check:** search for meetings that do not fit and cases where access, participation,
   authority, or documentation are confused.
7. **Community validation:** present the recommendation together with surviving ambiguities.

## Evidence standard

- Separate official facts, observed practice, participant reports, inference, and proposal.
- Cite every claim about ResonantOS policy to a current official source.
- Do not generalize interview observations as community consensus.
- Record disconfirming cases; do not repair the model by hiding them.
- Treat founder articles and existing practice as evidence of use, not automatically as policy.

## Outputs

1. Inventory of the meetings examined.
2. Research aggregate and cited findings.
3. Minimal glossary and `ontology-view` for load-bearing terms.
4. Plain-language `system-view` of the model and its stakes.
5. Decision register owning unresolved policy choices.
6. Minimal schemas for a recurring series and a specific occurrence.
7. Filled examples using real meetings.
8. A 4–6 week pilot proposal with revision criteria.
9. An `x-ray` only if the draft leaves a relationship that prose or a table cannot explain clearly.

The public document is the primary deliverable. Research detail may remain in supporting artifacts.

## Sufficiency and stopping conditions

Stop when:

- the main current formats are represented;
- two consecutive new examples reveal no necessary new dimension;
- each recommended category has a real example or is marked provisional;
- non-fitting and ambiguous cases are recorded;
- people outside the drafting process can use the model without a long explanation;
- no known counterexample invalidates the main structure; and
- evidence-backed conclusions are separated from decisions that still require community authority.

If these conditions are not met, report the uncertainty. Do not silently expand the claim.

## Risks

- **Confirmation bias:** treating `work / community life` as the answer instead of a hypothesis.
- **False openness:** equating public attendance with voice, decision rights, recording, or public
  records.
- **Bureaucracy:** requiring documentation whose maintenance cost exceeds its value.
- **Leadership ambiguity:** leaving community-leader coordination indistinguishable from a
  decision-making body.
- **False clarity:** using stable-sounding names before real meetings support the distinctions.

## Handoff

The research aggregate should support `findings`, which then supplies evidence to the glossary,
views, decision register, and public proposal. The handoff order is:

```text
meeting inventory + source review + participant evidence
  -> research aggregate
  -> findings
  -> ontology-view + decision register
  -> system-view / public draft
  -> optional x-ray
  -> community pilot
```

The diagram is retained because it answers one question better than prose: which artifacts depend
on which earlier evidence. It does not describe governance or imply that every stage is automated.

## Decisions intentionally left open

- final names for the two primary families;
- whether one family plus secondary emphases is sufficient;
- the default and exceptions for access, participation, recording, and records;
- which forums inform, recommend, or decide;
- when a pre-read is mandatory;
- what authority a community-leaders forum holds; and
- who owns final ratification of the meeting model.
