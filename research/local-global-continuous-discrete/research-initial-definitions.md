# Initial Research Definitions: Local–Global and Continuous–Discrete

## Context

Cyberalchemy Orchestrator develops infrastructure that keeps agent work connected to the objectives, decisions, assumptions, actions, and evidence that give it meaning. Its broader concern is how locally bounded work can compose into globally coherent judgment without erasing disagreement, uncertainty, or provenance.

The present discussion noticed a possible analogy between continuous versus discrete optimization and local-to-global reasoning, prompted in part by Selmer groups. Clarifying how far that analogy survives matters because an attractive but false equivalence could distort the repository's models of decomposition, coordination, observation, and evidence.

## Purpose

This document establishes the shared informational context for a repository-wide exploration. The resulting research will inform whether the analogy should become a reusable conceptual model, remain a limited explanatory metaphor, or be rejected at a clearly identified boundary.

## Research Question (Can be refined)

What existing repository concepts, artifacts, or recent changes bear on the relationship among local and global reasoning, continuous and discrete search, compatibility, obstruction, and compositional agent work; what can already be inferred from them; and at what point does the proposed analogy break?

## Confirmed Product Constraints

- The exploration is repository-wide and initially bounded rather than an exhaustive literature review.
- Claims must not be stronger than their supporting repository evidence.
- Continuous/discrete and local/global are not assumed to be equivalent axes.
- The desired result distinguishes existing evidence, defensible inference, open research questions, practical value, and failure boundaries.
- The work should use small, fast, directed subagent groups, including a distinct examination of recent repository changes.

## Current Evidence Baseline

- The repository policy describes the project as governing how agent work is decomposed, handed off, observed, evaluated, and connected back to its source objective; it also says that local correctness is insufficient when work no longer serves its larger purpose (`AGENTS.md`).
- The initiating discussion established only a provisional formulation: continuity often supplies infinitesimal/local navigation, while discreteness can expose separated configurations and compatibility constraints; neither axis alone guarantees local or global optimization.
- Selmer groups were introduced conversationally as a motivating local-to-global example, not yet as a repository-supported formal model.

## Known Gaps

- Whether the repository already names or operationalizes a local-to-global mechanism analogous to compatibility plus obstruction.
- Whether continuous/discrete distinctions appear explicitly, implicitly, or not at all in current architecture and research artifacts.
- Whether recent changes strengthen, weaken, or merely resemble this conceptual connection.
- Which inferences would be explanatory metaphors and which could support an operational or formal design.
- The smallest concrete witness for the proposed connection and the first counterexample or collapse point.
