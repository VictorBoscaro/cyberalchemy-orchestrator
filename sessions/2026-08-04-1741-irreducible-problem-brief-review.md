# Irreducible-Problem Brief: Cross-Repository Grounding and Review

## Summary

The session began as a reading of `../domainspec-core` for material related to stewardship and to a
remembered "four-faced action method", which resolved to the sibling's active `CAV2-D21` four-facet
action method and its two stewardship discipline cards. Comparing those against this repository's
target-architecture kernel material established a strong correspondence through typed residue and
the human-plus-machine view pair, and one genuine tension: `CAV2-D21` asserts its four-facet frame
as a universal for every governed action, while the kernel material refuses exactly that
universality for its own seven guarantees and leaves it open as `OD-K1`. Two candidate actions were
rejected on principle — editing the derived x-ray by hand, because it is a skill-produced artifact
with a pinned evidence boundary whose content changes belong in its source essay, and porting the
sibling's definitions here, because copying a frame beyond its derivation context is the failure the
kernel material names and the stewardship card forbids. Instead the material was routed into the
already-open `repository-irreducible-problem-inventory` brief, which had cited the sibling only
through its presentation documents; the count of seven research topics standing at brief-only argued
against opening an eighth. The brief gained four sibling-authority and x-ray evidence entries plus
gaps covering criterion availability, co-origination, and completeness probes. Two independent
reviewers on structurally different lenses — the document's declared contract versus its external
sources — ran three zig-zag rounds and converged on the same defects in every round, including two
false claims the parent introduced while fixing round-one findings. The two lenses disagreed
productively on whether established facts belonged in gaps, resolved by placing verified facts in
the evidence baseline and leaving only genuine unknowns in gaps. The loop hit its three-round
ceiling with an open `BLOCK`, because a zig-zag whose parent is the sole writer always terminates on
an unreviewed edit regardless of round count; a terminal verification pass with no edit following it
closed that gap and confirmed the repaired passages. The dispatch was registered and closed as
`loop_ceiling_reached` rather than `resolved`, preserving the fact that the designed loop did not
converge on its own.

## Recommendation

The keystone is the review topology, not the brief. Any dispatch where the parent is the sole writer
and subagents only read will terminate on an unreviewed edit, and adding rounds cannot fix it
because each round ends with another parent edit; this session needed a terminal verification pass
outside the loop to reach a verified state at all. Attack it by making the last actor a verifier
rather than the writer whenever a dispatch edits a governed artifact. A weaker contributing cause,
offered as a hunch rather than a licensed claim: the parent's fixes repeatedly introduced new errors
because they were drafted from the reviewers' reports instead of from the sources those reports
cited, and three of the confirmed defects trace to that second-hand chain.
