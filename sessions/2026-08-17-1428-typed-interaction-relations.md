# Typed Interaction Relations Research and Staged Execution

## Summary

The repository objective is to keep multi-agent work structurally connected to its objectives, decisions, authority, actions, and evidence. This session broadened the interaction-relations research from solving zig-zag to asking which smallest semantic relation basis can reconstruct the examined agent-communication patterns and current market behaviors. The user confirmed graphs as the substrate, with typed relations whose types express observably different meanings rather than workflow names. A single connected research dispatch could not compile because the current runtime requires downstream handoff evidence before upstream execution, so the work was explicitly staged into governed dispatches. Four independent explorations reconstructed the local patterns, authority and evidence semantics, a generative basis, and current official solutions, after which a separate synthesizer produced the top-level research and findings. The synthesis proposes the bounded candidate basis `requires`, `supplies`, `delegates`, `transfers_control`, and `gates`, while treating sequential, review, zig-zag, feedback, and robot-talks as recipes over relations, topology, roles, schemas, policies, and runtime effects. `assessed_by` was demoted from primitive relation to a typed assessment occurrence because its subject, obligation, authorship, criteria, and authority remained recoverable without a dedicated edge. The external comparison found broad convergence on graph/state orchestration but no proven minimal semantic algebra, while independently witnessing the difference between delegation with retained responsibility and handoff with control transfer. The five-relation basis remains provisional because the precedent, non-vacuity, and definitional-soundness gates were materialized but not opened without a new explicit confirmation. The current runtime still executes only narrow sequential handoff compilation, rejects zig-zag and feedback semantics, and lacks progressive graph scheduling, so this research does not authorize schema or runtime implementation.

## Open questions

- Do all five retained candidates survive the precedent, non-vacuity, and definitional-soundness gates?
- Can mandatory evidence slots safely derive some uses of `requires`, or must pure dependency remain explicit?
- Do negotiation, revocation, shared control, confidentiality, dynamic membership, or compensation require additional primitive relations?

## Next steps

1. Obtain explicit confirmation and execute the prepared three-gate dispatch.
2. Preserve the gate returns verbatim and revise or kill candidate relations when the evidence requires it.
3. Run the planned final audit and close the research with a bounded readiness verdict.
4. Design schema and runtime migration only after the basis survives those gates.

## Recommendation

Execute the three independent gates before treating the five-relation basis as an implementation contract; the most important attacks are the possible collapse of `requires` into mandatory supply and the claimed irreducibility of delegation versus control transfer.
