# Reframing Systems and Categories Around Work Context

## Summary

This session examined how `systems-and-categories` can support the repository objective of keeping agent work connected to the objectives, decisions, and evidence that give it meaning. The immediate objective was to understand the function of Section 2 and whether category theory had been earned by the argument. The review found that Section 2 currently shifts from relations within a system to relations among representations without first establishing a consequential reason for that shift. Attempts to motivate preservation through cross-domain coordination clarified the issue but did not independently justify category theory. The session then distinguished the essay's declared objective (introducing a way to represent domains) from what it currently delivers, which is a conceptual argument for keeping meaningful distinctions available as work becomes distributed. Comparison with `work-context-system-view` showed that both essays converge on the loss of context between larger objectives and local work. A possible distinct role for `systems-and-categories` emerged: explain why recoverable connections are insufficient when the representing language collapses distinctions needed for interpretation, governance, or evaluation. No rewrite or decision about the final scope was made because the essay's unique reader transformation remains unsettled. The main implication is that category theory should not be positioned until the document's own problem and contribution are clear.
