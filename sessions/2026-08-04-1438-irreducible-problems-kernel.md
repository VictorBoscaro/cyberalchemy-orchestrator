# Kernel relevance and irreducible project problems

## Summary

The repository's main objective is to keep agent work connected to the objectives, decisions, assumptions, authority, actions, and evidence that give it meaning. The session examined the target-architecture kernel hypothesis and explained it as a bounded composition boundary rather than a universal semantic owner or runtime service. The kernel was judged relevant because it protects integrity across independently governed domains by requiring explicit, versioned, attributable composition witnesses without manufacturing authority or truth. “Internal consistency” was refined to “governed coherence” or “integrity,” because explicit conflict and residue may be valid system states while hidden contradiction is not. The session then challenged whether loss of linkage to the source objective is itself the smallest project problem or an aggregate symptom. A cross-repository investigation was scoped over this repository and `../domainspec-core`, whose additional framing connects typed intent, reusable capability, and evidence from intent to reality. The governed research route selected two independent explorer concerns and one definitional skeptic to distinguish irreducible problems from symptoms, desired properties, and architectural mechanisms. The dispatch was fully proposed but not opened because the lifecycle's final confirmation was not received before session closure. The research-initial-definitions artifact was created as a precondition of the governed `research` route, not as an output of `robot-talks`.
