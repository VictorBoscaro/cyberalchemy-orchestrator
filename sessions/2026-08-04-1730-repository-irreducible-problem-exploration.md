# Repository Irreducible-Problem Exploration

## Summary

The repository aims to keep agent work connected to the objectives, decisions, assumptions, actions, and evidence that give it meaning. This session set out to revise the initial research framing and begin a cross-repository inventory of problems that may be irreducible rather than symptoms, required properties, or mechanisms. The initial definitions were made more neutral by removing assumptions that the investigation or a fixed irreducible set already existed. The research was segmented into independent exploration, later synthesis, and later skeptical gates because the current compiler cannot bind dynamic upstream outputs inside one dispatch. Three early dispatch attempts exposed invalid task-name generation, a missing automatic Codex hook, altered manually relayed envelopes, and persistent task-name reservation after interruption. The successful `r4` attempt used unique task names, programmatically extracted launch arguments, direct bridge binding, and pre-launch verification of all three seats. All three explorers completed source-grounded reports spanning objective and collective judgment, authority and provenance, and execution and intent-to-reality. Their results converge on a layered family of candidate failures rather than one demonstrated fundamental problem, while distinguishing provenance from authority, compatibility from licensed composition, and terminal realization failure from its several upstream causes. The session closed before the parent persisted the verbatim reports or ran synthesis and skepticism, so the exploration dispatch was closed as `user_abort` without treating the research program as complete.
