# Priority diagrams for *Systems and Categories*

## Scope

This investigation selects the first five diagrams that would materially improve the essay. It draws from the visual grammar of *A Small Atlas of Contextual Forms* without importing the work-context ontology wholesale. It proposes diagrams; it does not draw or insert them.

## Central question

Which five diagrams, inspired by the artbook's visual grammar, make the essay's five most important ideas perceptible without introducing structure that the text has not earned?

## Assumptions challenged

- The essay needs five additional diagrams rather than fewer.
- Artbook symbols can transfer without carrying their original infrastructure ontology.
- Each selected diagram clarifies an essential passage instead of merely decorating the PDF.
- Different lenses can be shown adequately through color and emphasis alone.
- Quiet symbolic drawings remain intelligible without a full explanatory page.

## Strategy

The investigation is decomposed by concern, with three independent roles:

1. **Argumentative arc** — identify where a figure materially changes understanding. Excludes aesthetics and implementation.
2. **Visual semantics and epistemic restraint** — identify what each mark claims about preservation, absence, uncertainty, composition, translation, or identity. Excludes stylistic preference.
3. **Reading experience and symbol system** — evaluate sequence, rhythm, recurrence, and whether the figures form a coherent small visual language. Excludes prose revision.

The orchestrator will compare all proposals against the essay's exact claims and the ten forms in the artbook, then produce a ranked top five and near misses.

## Rejected decomposition

An agent-per-section decomposition was rejected because it would encourage local illustration without testing the essay-wide hierarchy of needs. Asking every agent for an undifferentiated top five was also rejected because it would produce redundant aesthetic voting rather than independent scrutiny.

## Expected outputs

Each role writes one report in `reports/` using the required Robot-Talks structure: key findings with evidence, gaps or inconsistencies, local tensions, and questions for synthesis. `findings.md` will preserve the final ranking, disagreements, near misses, and the human disposition at the gate.

## Conversation protocol

Agents investigate independently before seeing one another's conclusions. Recommendations must cite the essay section or line and the relevant artbook form. The synthesis favors argumentative necessity and claim fidelity over visual novelty.

## Agent prompts

Prompts are recorded here before launch and repeated verbatim in the agent assignments.

### 01 — Argumentative arc

Read the evidence base. Determine which conceptual transitions most need a diagram to become understandable in sequence. Recommend a ranked set, but do not evaluate drawing style or implementation. Explain what each proposed diagram lets the reader understand that prose alone presently does not. Report in the mandatory Robot-Talks format with exact file/line or artbook page evidence.

### 02 — Visual semantics and epistemic restraint

Read the evidence base. Audit which visual claims the essay has earned and which would overstate it. Recommend diagram concepts and transferable artbook symbols for preservation, uncertainty, absence, translation, composition, identity, and revision. Do not rank by beauty. Report in the mandatory Robot-Talks format with exact evidence.

### 03 — Reading experience and symbol system

Read the evidence base. Propose the smallest coherent sequence of diagrams across the essay, considering pacing, recurrence, cognitive load, captions, and symbol reuse. Identify whether five is too many or too few. Do not rewrite prose. Report in the mandatory Robot-Talks format with exact evidence.

## Exploration outcome

All three roles rejected five wholly additional figures. They converged on five priority interventions across new, recurring, and redesigned figures. Their strongest agreement concerns the recurring situation/lenses motif, a real preservation test in the translation figure, and a narrower infrastructure figure. The principal disagreement is whether conditional formalization or rupture/residue deserves the scarce new full-page slot; the synthesis retains both in the conceptual top five and places the already-serviceable syntax/semantics/adequacy figure among the near misses.
