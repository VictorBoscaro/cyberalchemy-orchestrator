# 02 — Visual semantics and epistemic restraint

## Key Findings

- **A translation diagram should show declared obligations, not visual sameness.** Section 3 moves from analogy to translation by asking which relations and compositions must survive, and explicitly makes equivalence relative to the represented structure (`essays/systems-and-categories/source.md:51-66`). The artbook's *Witnessed composition* supplies a useful transferable mark: an absent shortcut plus a small gate at the point where support is required (`plans/governed-agent-work-infrastructure/essays/work-context-system-view/pdf-version/short-version/drawings/work-context-symbols-art-book.html:469-484`). A suitable diagram would place two non-identical representations side by side, mark only the correspondences being asserted, and place a gate on a composed path whose preservation must be checked. Solid links should mean “declared correspondence”; a dashed link should mean “candidate analogy”; the gate should mean “obligation to verify,” not “verification succeeded.” Reusing identical graph geometry on both sides would overstate the claim by making preservation appear visually given.

- **Residue and revision have the strongest earned visual semantics in the essay.** Section 6 distinguishes a newly relevant, unrepresented difference from a bad instance; residue identifies a failure of preservation but does not select its repair, and several revisions may remain possible (`essays/systems-and-categories/source.md:103-113`). *Revision without erasure* already encodes a witnessed divergence while keeping the earlier path visible (`plans/governed-agent-work-infrastructure/essays/work-context-system-view/pdf-version/short-version/drawings/work-context-symbols-art-book.html:440-457`). The adapted diagram should therefore show one previously adequate path reaching a rupture, the old path remaining faint but addressable, and two or more unresolved candidate continuations. A hollow or interrupted endpoint may mark “not expressible in the current language”; a dashed continuation may mark “candidate revision.” It must not use one clean replacement branch, because that would imply the residue determines its own repair, which the text denies.

- **The lenses figure has earned selection and salience, but not invariant identity under projection.** The essay defines a lens modestly as a structured way of making some relations salient, describes a frame as a situated application, and explicitly declines to claim a category or unique formal carrier for lenses (`essays/systems-and-categories/source.md:117-133`). The artbook's *Family of graph views* offers the safe part of the grammar—partial views for declared questions, with quieter unselected material (`plans/governed-agent-work-infrastructure/essays/work-context-system-view/pdf-version/short-version/drawings/work-context-symbols-art-book.html:629-655`). By contrast, *Invariant identity under projection* asserts a stable axis, unchanged provenance, and identity surviving every view (`plans/governed-agent-work-infrastructure/essays/work-context-system-view/pdf-version/short-version/drawings/work-context-symbols-art-book.html:602-621`), while the essay itself leaves the criterion for “the same lens” open (`essays/systems-and-categories/source.md:231-235`). A revised lenses drawing may repeat the same labeled situation and vary emphasis, but should avoid a dark invariant center or common projection axis. It should also avoid describing the categorical panel as “translation and preservation” unless it actually depicts at least two representations and a preservation question; the current four-node emphasis diagram depicts selection only (`essays/systems-and-categories/source.md:125-129`; visual inspection of essay PDF p. 9).

- **Progressive formalization requires a conditional, non-teleological diagram.** Section 4 permits a progression from ordinary vocabulary to typed graphs, signatures, and possibly categorical structure, but says stronger laws are warranted only when they constrain something the domain cares about; arrows and morphism labels alone do not earn composition (`essays/systems-and-categories/source.md:70-80`). A drawing can reuse the artbook's gate as an evidential threshold, but should branch rather than form a smooth ascending ladder: vocabulary → typed relation → compositional law only if a named domain pressure warrants it. A side branch ending without further formalization is semantically important. Otherwise the image would contradict the essay by presenting category formation as maturation or inevitable progress.

- **The infrastructure drawing should visualize transversal languages and recoverability without inventing a canonical common schema.** Sections 8–9 claim that local context may remain partial, missing or uncertain connections must stay visible, domain languages participate non-uniformly across intention, orchestration, work, and effect, and recoverable context preserves translations, versions, scope, and mismatches (`essays/systems-and-categories/source.md:137-176`). *Simultaneous scopes* safely contributes overlapping fields that do not collapse (`plans/governed-agent-work-infrastructure/essays/work-context-system-view/pdf-version/short-version/drawings/work-context-symbols-art-book.html:549-569`), while *Planes aligned through one record* makes the stronger claim that all representations touch a shared record through common identifiers (`plans/governed-agent-work-infrastructure/essays/work-context-system-view/pdf-version/short-version/drawings/work-context-symbols-art-book.html:663-683`). The essay has not earned that shared-axis claim. A defensible diagram would use overlapping language bands across the work path, explicit translation junctions where languages meet, broken or offset junctions for unresolved mismatches, and a recoverability trace below rather than one universal backbone. The current Figure 4 is close, but its uninterrupted bands risk implying that each language retains stable meaning across every stage without translation (`essays/systems-and-categories/source.md:164-172`; visual inspection of essay PDF p. 11).

## Gaps or Inconsistencies

- The essay uses “same situation” and “the object remains” in Figure 3 (`essays/systems-and-categories/source.md:125-129`) but later leaves lens identity and its formal carrier unresolved (`essays/systems-and-categories/source.md:133, 234-235`). The drawing needs a modest criterion of sameness—shared labels, source episode, or record—without upgrading that criterion to formal invariance.
- Figure 3's categorical panel currently changes color and attenuation on the same network, but the prose says that the categorical lens highlights representations, translations, composition, and preservation (`essays/systems-and-categories/source.md:125-126`). Those are relations between structured descriptions, not merely a subset of sales-system edges. The present image cannot carry its own caption's categorical claim.
- Figure 4 names translations and unresolved mismatches in prose and caption (`essays/systems-and-categories/source.md:166-172`) but does not give either a distinct visual mark. Continuous horizontal bands make participation legible, but conceal where meaning changes or fails to align.
- The source has no explicit symbol contract for solid, dashed, faint, interrupted, hollow, or gated marks. Without a local legend, the artbook's symbols retain their original work-context meanings by default, despite the investigation's stated intention not to import that ontology wholesale.

## Local Tensions

- **Continuity versus recoverability:** a continuous line conventionally asserts an unbroken relation, while the essay promises only that omitted context can be recovered and admits uncertain or missing links (`essays/systems-and-categories/source.md:143-147`). Recoverability should therefore be a trace or retrievable reference, not automatically a continuous causal or semantic path.
- **Selection versus absence:** fading a relation is appropriate for “not salient under this lens,” but not for “absent,” “unknown,” “not represented,” or “known not to hold.” The essay needs at least three states because it distinguishes what a representation selects, what it cannot express, and what remains uncertain (`essays/systems-and-categories/source.md:28-33, 105-113, 147`). One gray style for all three would erase precisely the distinctions the essay argues should be preserved.
- **Composition versus adjacency:** connected boxes or successive arrows can look compositional even where the essay says composition must be justified by domain laws (`essays/systems-and-categories/source.md:55-64, 74-78`). The artbook gate is valuable only if its caption consistently says whether it marks a required witness, an available witness, or a completed proof.
- **Identity versus reference:** repeating a node across panels can mean “the same object,” “the same label,” “the same source record,” or merely “a proposed correspondence.” *Invariant identity under projection* collapses these possibilities into a stable identity claim (`plans/governed-agent-work-infrastructure/essays/work-context-system-view/pdf-version/short-version/drawings/work-context-symbols-art-book.html:617-621`), stronger than the essay's working concept of lenses permits.
- **Revision versus correction:** a branch suggests changed understanding or conditions, while a corrected data point need not change the language at all (`essays/systems-and-categories/source.md:105-109`). The residue figure must locate the rupture at the representational rule, not depict every empirical error as a schema revision.

## Questions for Synthesis

1. Should the top five prioritize new conceptual coverage, or may they include replacement/redesign of Figures 1, 3, and 4 where current drawings overstate or under-express the prose?
2. What minimal legend will distinguish **salient**, **present but quiet**, **unknown/uncertain**, **not representable**, **candidate translation**, and **verified preservation** without turning the essay into a notation manual?
3. What is the weakest defensible sense in which Figure 3's two panels show “the same situation”: shared episode, shared labels, shared data record, or shared underlying graph? The choice determines whether any invariant mark is warranted.
4. Can one drawing combine translation and witnessed composition without implying categorical structure before Section 4 has earned it, or should analogy/translation and categorical composition remain separate figures?
5. Is the infrastructure figure allowed to posit explicit shared identifiers or a canonical record? If not, the synthesis should reject *Planes aligned through one record* as a direct template and borrow only its visual distinction between alignment and uniformity.
