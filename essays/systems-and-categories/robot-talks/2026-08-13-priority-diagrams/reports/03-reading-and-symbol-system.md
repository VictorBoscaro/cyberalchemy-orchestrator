# 03 - Reading Experience and Symbol System

## 1. Key Findings

- **Five is the right number only if it means five principal figures in total, not five additions.** The current 18-page PDF already places figures in Sections 3, 5, 7, and 9 (PDF pp. 5, 7, 9, and 11; `source.md`, lines 58-60, 93-95, 125-129, 162-166). Adding one early figure in Section 1 would create a quiet alternating cadence across Sections 1, 3, 5, 7, and 9. Adding five more would bring the essay to nine figures, make nearly every conceptual turn demand a visual decoding task, and weaken the special status of the diagrams.

- **The smallest coherent five-figure sequence is a progression from selection to synthesis:**

  1. **Same event, successive descriptions - Section 1 (new).** Hold the falling-sales situation stable while acquisition, retention, and promise/capacity relations become successively salient. This gives the reader a concrete visual object before any formal vocabulary and teaches the first rule of the system: selection changes what can be asked without changing the event itself (`source.md`, lines 24-33).
  2. **Analogy becomes translation - Section 3 (redesign current Figure 1).** Two related structures remain visible, but a witnessed crossing distinguishes suggestive correspondence from preservation obligation (`source.md`, lines 51-66). The artbook's gate and missing shortcut already encode the difference between adjacency and warranted composition (`work-context-symbols-art-book.html`, lines 462-484).
  3. **Syntax, semantics, adequacy - Section 5 (refine current Figure 2).** Preserve the three-stage reading because it gives a necessary epistemic pause at the essay's midpoint: formal coherence supports but does not settle empirical adequacy (`source.md`, lines 84-99). This figure may remain more schematic than the others, but its arrows, accent, and boundary treatment should use the common symbols.
  4. **Same situation, different lenses - Section 7 (realize current Figure 3).** Return to the exact substrate introduced in Figure 1 of this sequence, now showing simultaneous systemic and categorical selections. The repetition produces recognition before interpretation and makes the lens concept feel discovered rather than newly imposed (`source.md`, lines 117-133). The artbook's stable axis and multiple projections provide a stronger model than color changes alone (`work-context-symbols-art-book.html`, lines 600-621).
  5. **Languages across work and recoverable context - Section 9 (redesign current Figure 4).** Conclude with the largest visual: intention, orchestration, bounded work, and effect remain distinct while several languages cross them and context preserves correspondence (`source.md`, lines 151-176). The artbook's planes touching one record supports correspondence without uniformity (`work-context-symbols-art-book.html`, lines 660-683).

- **The sequence should teach no more than five recurring visual primitives.** Recommended primitives are: a filled anchor for the stable referent; an ordinary line for a declared relation; a quiet or dashed line for a relation not currently selected or no longer current; a field/contour for a representation or situated view; and a gate/short transverse mark for a preservation or warrant obligation. The artbook already distinguishes base, accent, quiet, axial, and dashed relations consistently (`work-context-symbols-art-book.html`, lines 226-339). Reusing these marks matters more than importing the artbook's handwriting, page furniture, or infrastructure ontology.

- **Recurrence should carry the cognitive load that captions otherwise would.** Figures 1 and 4 in the proposed sequence should use the same sales/fulfillment nodes and spatial arrangement; only the active relations and contours change. Figures 2 and 5 should reuse the gate/axis distinction to show that translation across representations or languages requires an explicit meeting point. Existing captions already model the right economy: each states one relation between what remains and what changes (`source.md`, lines 60, 95, 129, 166). A compact decoding sentence under the first figure is enough; importing the artbook's four-note page apparatus would be too heavy for inline essay figures (artbook note grid and labels: `work-context-symbols-art-book.html`, lines 180-224).

## 2. Gaps or Inconsistencies

- The source contains four figure slots, but no essay-wide legend or semantic contract says whether accent, gray, dashing, containment, or crossing retain the same meaning from one figure to the next (`source.md`, lines 58-60, 93-95, 125-129, 162-166).
- Current Figure 1 uses paired graphs and two kinds of arrows, while current Figure 2 uses labeled boxes; they read as independent explanatory graphics rather than members of one visual language (`drawings/systems-lens.png`; `drawings/category-lens.png`).
- Figure 3 says that unselected relations remain gray, but gray alone communicates low prominence, not why the selection changed or what remains invariant (`source.md`, lines 125-129). The artbook uses a stable axis/anchor in addition to quieting (`work-context-symbols-art-book.html`, lines 600-621).
- Figure 4 has the greatest number of concepts but the thinnest visual specification. It must distinguish stages, transversal languages, translations, and recoverable context without turning all four into equivalent lines or containers (`source.md`, lines 151-174).
- The status of the Section 6 rupture/residue image is unresolved. That section contains a highly drawable transition but no figure slot (`source.md`, lines 103-113).

## 3. Local Tensions

- **Argumentative importance versus pacing:** rupture/residue is visually strong, and the artbook's faded old route maps closely to it (`work-context-symbols-art-book.html`, lines 437-457). Giving it a full figure, however, would place three substantial diagrams in close succession across Sections 5-7 and interrupt the otherwise legible alternating rhythm. My recommendation is a small unnumbered echo of the first figure's structure, or no image, unless synthesis determines residue is one of the essay's five governing claims.
- **Quietness versus legibility:** the artbook deliberately uses thin, low-opacity marks (`work-context-symbols-art-book.html`, lines 253-295). In a mixed text-and-diagram essay, those marks can be read as decoration or disappear in print. Semantic quietness should come from both line treatment and geometry, not opacity alone.
- **Reuse versus semantic drift:** a dashed line cannot mean "analogy" in one figure, "historical" in another, and "unselected" in a third without a caption repeatedly repairing the system. The artbook itself separates historical routes, traces, and quiet branches through class names, but their rendered differences are subtle (`work-context-symbols-art-book.html`, lines 259-295, 337-339).
- **Synthesis versus overload:** Figure 5 of the proposed sequence must close the argument, but adding lenses, authority, uncertainty, every domain language, and all context relations to the same drawing would make it a map of the whole essay rather than an aid to one claim. Section 9 earns transversal languages and recoverability; it does not require every earlier symbol to return (`source.md`, lines 151-176).

## 4. Questions for Synthesis

1. Will "five diagrams" be governed as five principal figures total, with the four current slots redesigned, or as five additional figures? The reading evidence strongly favors the former.
2. Is rupture/residue central enough to replace one of the proposed five, or should it receive only a small recurring glyph between Figures 3 and 4?
3. Which meanings will be reserved globally for accent, dash, quiet line, boundary, anchor, and gate? This contract should be decided before any drawing is produced.
4. Can the exact sales/fulfillment substrate recur in Sections 1 and 7 so the later lens figure is a recognition event rather than a new diagram to decode?
5. Should the syntax-semantics-adequacy figure remain a deliberate typological exception, or can its three-stage structure be expressed with the same fields, axes, and gate used elsewhere without losing clarity?
