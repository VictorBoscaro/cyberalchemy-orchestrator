# 01 — Argumentative Arc

## 1. Key Findings

- **1. Make the changing representation of one situation the essay's recurring anchor, rather than adding a separate late illustration of lenses.** The opening example performs the essay's central move: one sales decline is successively organized as acquisition, retention, and promise–capacity failure, and each organization changes the plausible action (`source.md`, lines 24–33). Section 7 later names this operation as a lens and explicitly says the reframing changes which relations govern the next question (`source.md`, lines 119–129); the coda depends on the reader recognizing that recurrence (`source.md`, lines 182–186). A diagram should let the reader hold the situation stable while seeing three successive relational selections and their different action spaces. In quick drawing terms: one persistent relational substrate, shown first through acquisition, then retention, then promise/capacity/delay, with unselected relations still recoverable and each view pointing toward a different next question. The closest artbook forms are **Invariant identity under projection** (artbook PDF, p. 8) and **A family of graph views** (p. 9). The current Figure 3 arrives only in Section 7 and compares two disciplinary labels; it does not carry the opening's three-stage discovery or its change in action. This is the highest-priority intervention because it makes the whole essay's movement perceptible, not merely one local claim.

- **2. Add a diagram of earned, non-inevitable formalization in Section 4.** The title promises movement *toward domain languages*, and Section 4 contains the essay's most distinctive methodological claim: ordinary vocabulary may become a typed graph, then acquire signatures, and only becomes categorical if identities and associative composition genuinely belong to the domain (`source.md`, lines 72–80). Prose presents this as a progression, but the argument explicitly rejects treating category as an automatic destination. A diagram should let the reader see thresholds without seeing a maturity ladder. In quick drawing terms: a path from ordinary distinctions to typed relations, followed by conditional gates for admissible composition, identities, and associativity; at each gate, a legitimate stopping branch remains visible. **Witnessed composition** (artbook PDF, p. 3) supplies the relevant argumentative form: adjacency does not authorize composition, and the gate marks where an additional obligation must be witnessed. This would be a genuinely new figure and the clearest visual statement of how a domain earns stronger structure.

- **3. Add a rupture-and-residue diagram in Section 6 that preserves the old representation while opening multiple possible revisions.** Section 6 distinguishes an incorrect instance from a language that cannot express a newly consequential difference, defines residue modestly, and refuses automatic enrichment (`source.md`, lines 103–113). That sequence is central but cognitively dense: readers must track the old model's prior adequacy, the new task, the exposed distinction, and the underdetermination of repair. A diagram would show what prose alone makes easy to collapse into “the model was wrong.” In quick drawing terms: an earlier representation merges two cases; a new task requires different actions; the newly visible split is marked as residue; two or more candidate enrichments branch from the witnessed rupture while the earlier path remains faintly addressable. **Revision without erasure** (artbook PDF, p. 2) is directly transferable, with **Append-only strata** (p. 4) as a secondary source for retaining the history. This is the second genuinely new figure.

- **4. Replace or strengthen Figure 1 so that translation visibly incurs a preservation test, rather than merely connecting similar-looking graphs.** Section 3's conceptual transition is from productive resemblance to obligations over relations and composed transformations (`source.md`, lines 53–66). The current figure states that a translation “must preserve declared structure,” but its two nearly identical graphs and straight inter-panel arrow do not show a path comparison, a possible failure, or what is declared. A reader can therefore accept the caption without understanding the test. In quick drawing terms: two structured representations with an explicit mapping; on each side, a two-step path and its composite; a witness or comparison mark indicates whether mapping-then-composing agrees with composing-then-mapping, while one optional distinction demonstrates task-relative loss. **Witnessed composition** (artbook PDF, p. 3) supports the need for an explicit warrant, and **Planes aligned through one record** (p. 10) supports correspondence without uniformity. This is a refinement of an existing argumentative slot, not an additional idea.

- **5. Keep one infrastructure diagram, but narrow its argumentative job to transversal languages plus recoverable context.** Sections 8–9 make a late transition from epistemology to a ResonantOS design direction: local work must remain bounded, omitted context recoverable, and domain languages may participate non-uniformly across intention, orchestration, work, and effect (`source.md`, lines 139–147 and 151–176). This application matters, but it should not displace the essay's prior argument. A diagram should let the reader understand the apparent compatibility of locality and continuity: not everything is locally present, but relevant relations remain reachable; languages cross stages without becoming a universal container. In quick drawing terms: the work path remains bounded in stages; several language traces cross different subsets of it; each stage has selective links into a recoverable contextual field; no single trace encloses “local work.” **Simultaneous scopes** (artbook PDF, p. 6), **A family of graph views** (p. 9), and **One relation, variable depth** (p. 11) provide the relevant argumentative forms. The current Figure 4 occupies this slot, but also includes a situated lens and several distinct governance claims; its priority is conceptual narrowing, not the addition of another infrastructure picture.

## 2. Gaps or Inconsistencies

- The essay does **not** presently justify five wholly additional diagrams. It already has four figure slots (`source.md`, lines 58–60, 93–95, 125–129, and 162–166). On argumentative need, the first pass should contain two new diagrams (earned formalization; rupture/residue), two substantial revisions (translation; infrastructure), and one recurring anchor that begins in Section 1 and returns in Section 7. Treating all five as additions would likely repeat claims rather than clarify transitions.
- The request is ambiguous about whether “add” means five new pages, five new figure concepts, or the five highest-priority diagram interventions. The evidence supports the third interpretation.
- Section 9 still contains a stale “Figure 3” placeholder and TODO immediately before the Figure 4 specification (`source.md`, lines 162–168). This creates uncertainty about which visual contract is canonical, although the prose clearly requires transversal—not locally contained—domain languages.
- The essay has no explicit figure in Section 4, even though that section carries the methodological thesis most directly tied to the subtitle. Conversely, Section 5 already has a serviceable diagram for a distinction that the prose also states clearly (`source.md`, lines 93–99).

## 3. Local Tensions

- **Figure 3 names a categorical lens without diagramming categorical work.** The source specification asks the categorical panel to highlight representations, translations, composition, and preservation (`source.md`, lines 125–129), but the current PDF page 9 presents the same four business nodes with one route muted. Argumentatively, it shows selective salience, but not translation, composition, or preservation. The label currently carries more of the claim than the drawing.
- **Figure 1 risks depicting preservation as already achieved.** Section 3 says translation is a claim that can fail for a formally nameable reason (`source.md`, lines 55–66), while the current PDF page 5 shows structurally matching graphs and an unqualified translation arrow. The figure's symmetry makes the hard case—failure or partial preservation—disappear.
- **Figure 4 bears too many independent claims at once.** The surrounding prose distinguishes selective context, recoverability, knowledge versus authority, transversal domain languages, lenses as optional capacities, and human control (`source.md`, lines 151–176). One figure cannot make all those transitions legible without becoming a summary map. For the essay's arc, it should establish only the relation between bounded work, transversal languages, and recoverable context; authority and optional lenses can remain in prose or in later work.
- **The existing Figure 2 is useful but its left-to-right arrows can be read as entailment.** Its caption and the phrase “requires an additional warrant” correctly separate syntax, compositional semantics, and empirical adequacy (`source.md`, lines 93–99). Because the figure already performs the needed argumentative distinction, it is a lower priority than the missing Section 4 and Section 6 diagrams; any future revision should preserve the non-entailment explicitly.

## 4. Questions for Synthesis

- Will the synthesis rank five **diagram interventions**, allowing replacement and recurrence, or insist on five additional figures? The latter is not supported by this audit.
- Should the stable-situation motif first appear in Section 1 and recur in Section 7, or remain a single figure in Section 7? Its argumentative value is much greater if the reader encounters it before the abstract vocabulary of lenses.
- Is Section 9 part of the essay's central argument or an application for ResonantOS? If it is an application, its diagram should remain fifth and deliberately narrower than the epistemic diagrams.
- How much categorical notation can the intended group absorb without the preservation test becoming a technical detour? The synthesis should preserve the path-comparison obligation even if it avoids conventional commuting-diagram notation.
- Can the other roles identify a small symbol grammar that distinguishes “unselected but recoverable,” “absent,” “not yet warranted,” and “failed preservation”? Argumentatively these statuses are different, and collapsing them would undermine Sections 3, 6, and 7.
