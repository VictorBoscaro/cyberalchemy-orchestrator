# Skill-engineering response to Investigator 01

## 1. Opening guidance

**Agreement.** I agree that "begin with something the reader can recognize" cannot reject an
interchangeable universal claim. Investigator 01 grounds this both in the permissive wording of the
skill and in an actual essay opening that satisfies recognizability only after first stating a universal
thesis (`reports/01-reader-movement.md:5-19`). This confirms my finding that the opening guidance has
the right altitude but insufficient operational leverage (`reports/02-skill-engineering.md:13-22`).

**Attempted refutation.** Read together, establishing the reader's starting point and desired change
(`SKILL.md:19-20`), beginning with something recognizable (`SKILL.md:22`), and requiring an opening need
that the ending answers (`SKILL.md:96-97`) could be interpreted as enough constraint for a capable
writer. That interpretation fails because none of those clauses tests whether the opening is specific to
this reader movement; a generic thesis can satisfy all three after later paragraphs retrofit the need.

**Impact on my recommendation.** The other report narrows my generic request for a falsifiable opening
gate into a useful negative boundary. It also persuades me not to request a preferred opening form.

**Minimum surviving change.** Add one compact test: ground the opening in a situation or tension available
to the established reader, and reject it if it could open unrelated essays through noun substitution. Do
not prescribe anecdote, question, scene, or thesis form. Add a negative example only if blind
forward-testing shows that the test alone does not change output.

## 2. Passage transitions and endings

**Agreement.** I agree that necessary passages do not imply necessary transitions, and that an ending
which answers the opening can still merely summarize. The distinction is precise: the existing passage
test evaluates local contribution (`SKILL.md:27-28`), while the reorder test applies to collections
(`SKILL.md:37-39`), not to the reader-state change between paragraphs
(`reports/01-reader-movement.md:21-36`). The ending clause checks correspondence but not transformation
(`reports/01-reader-movement.md:53-64`).

**Attempted refutation.** One could extend the current reorder test implicitly to paragraphs and treat
"reader's movement" as already requiring transformation. That would avoid more machinery. It is not a
reliable reading: the skill explicitly scopes the reorder test to items and catalogues, and a smooth set
of individually useful paragraphs can pass the final read without showing why each successor is now
needed. Likewise, "answer" does not exclude recap.

**Impact on my recommendation.** This supports operational tests, but not two elaborate new workflows.
Transition and closure are two observations of the same movement: intermediate passages must change what
the reader can see or ask; the ending must make the opening situation newly intelligible or judgeable.

**Minimum surviving change.** Extend the movement check with two short clauses: after each passage, name
what the reader can now understand or ask that earns the next passage; at the end, verify that the reader
can understand or judge the opening situation differently, rather than merely recite the route. Keep
these as invisible revision tests, not required signposting in the essay.

## 3. Autonomous documents in sequence

**Agreement.** I agree that the current skill only establishes a starting point for one document and does
not distinguish local grounding from recap (`reports/01-reader-movement.md:38-51`). This is a real omission
against the Robot-Talks question (`dialogue.md:18-20`) and matches my scope-gap finding
(`reports/02-skill-engineering.md:61-65`).

**Attempted refutation.** Sequence-aware autonomy could be excluded as a project-specific concern rather
than generalized into a writing skill. The description does not mention series, and adding every current
essay need would broaden the package into a house style. The refutation only partly succeeds: the proposed
rule is not domain-specific and addresses a common compositional case, but it should remain conditional so
single-document tasks do not pay its cognitive cost.

**Impact on my recommendation.** I support inclusion, but as one conditional instruction—not a new
top-level workflow or routing term. The description need not expand unless the project expects users to
invoke the skill specifically by asking for a series.

**Minimum surviving change.** When a document belongs to a sequence, reconstruct only the causal premise
needed to understand it alone, without summarizing or naming its predecessor, and require it to advance a
new question. Test it once alone and once after the prior document. This preserves both autonomy and the
benefit of order in minimal space.

## 4. Revision as a distinct operation

**Agreement.** I agree that material restructuring is routed in the description but not instructed in
the body (`reports/01-reader-movement.md:66-78`). The present final instruction to revise a failing passage
(`SKILL.md:96-100`) risks local repair when the route itself is broken. This directly resolves my earlier
question about whether restructuring fits the scope: because it is already an explicit trigger
(`SKILL.md:3`), either the body must support it or the description must narrow.

**Attempted refutation.** The existing instruction to establish the starting point and intended change
could be reapplied to a draft without a separate revision mode. A skilled editor may do so. But the skill
does not tell the editor to recover the draft's attempted movement before imposing a new one, so that
reading can produce wholesale rewriting and loss of valid material rather than diagnosis.

**Impact on my recommendation.** Revision merits explicit treatment, but not an independent subsection
with a large procedure. A conditional paragraph under "Compose the reader's movement" preserves coherent
scope and keeps instruction density low.

**Minimum surviving change.** Before materially restructuring, recover the draft's current starting
understanding, successive changes, and destination; locate the first unearned concept, inert transition,
or restart; then move or rewrite from that break. This is enough to distinguish structural diagnosis from
surface copyediting.

## 5. Voice without a style manual

**Partial agreement.** I agree that "keep rhythm, warmth, and emphasis when they help comprehension"
(`SKILL.md:56-57`) is too permissive to guide a writer consistently, and that voice can conceal a missing
relation (`reports/01-reader-movement.md:82-86,107-109`). I do not agree that the skill needs standalone
voice techniques or examples. Voice is not its primary responsibility, and such additions would compete
with the missing load-bearing tests.

**Attempted refutation.** The current sentence may already be the correct high-freedom guardrail: writing
voice varies too much for stronger instruction. This survives in part. What is missing is not stylistic
prescription but a sharper criterion for earning expressive choices.

**Impact on my recommendation.** Voice should be constrained through reader movement, not expanded into a
new capability. No routing, metadata, resource, or section change is justified.

**Minimum surviving change.** Amend the existing sentence so cadence, repetition, restraint, warmth, or
emphasis remains only when it makes a distinction, transition, or consequence easier to perceive; reject
it when it substitutes for the relation. Do not prescribe a house voice.

## 6. Examples versus compact tests

**Agreement with qualification.** Investigator 01 repeatedly favors operational tests and leaves open
whether opening and ending examples are necessary (`reports/01-reader-movement.md:111-122`). I agree with
that priority. My report found the current example density lopsided: three examples substantially block
the same relationless-grouping family while the newly identified failure surfaces have none
(`reports/02-skill-engineering.md:24-31`).

**Attempted refutation.** Examples can teach tacit distinctions more efficiently than abstract tests, so
removing them merely to reduce lines could weaken the skill. This refutes wholesale replacement, not
rebalancing. The current catalogue, co-presence, and structure examples overlap enough that retaining all
three has an opportunity cost.

**Impact on my recommendation.** The editor should first add compact tests and compress or remove one
redundant relationless-grouping example, keeping at least one concrete relation example and the terminology
example. New examples should be earned by failed forward-tests, not by completeness anxiety.

**Minimum surviving change.** Reallocate existing density rather than create a `references/` style guide:
preserve representative negative examples, add the opening/movement/ending/sequence tests inline, then
blind-forward-test realistic essay tasks. Add one targeted example only where agents repeatedly
misinterpret a compact test.

## Surviving synthesis position

The other report strengthens the case for five bounded behavioral changes: a reader-relative opening
test, a passage-to-passage movement test, a transformation-based ending test, a conditional rule for
sequential autonomy, and a conditional diagnosis step for restructuring. Voice needs only a tighter
load-bearing criterion. These changes should be funded partly by compressing redundant negative examples,
not by growing a general style manual. None of this resolves the separate routing, package-location,
metadata-validation, or blind-forward-testing findings in my report; those remain independently
actionable.
